## What you will learn

- how to approach electromagnetism questions;
- the right rule for each situation;
- typical traps.

## 1. Strategy

1. **Draw the diagram**: circuit, field lines, force directions.
2. **Convert units first** ($\mu\text{C}$, $\text{cm}$, $\text{mA}$).
3. **Identify series or parallel**; reduce step by step.
4. **Fix directions with the right rule for the job**: right-hand grip rule for the field of a current; left-hand rule for the force on a current or charge; right-hand rule for the induced current in a moving rod; Lenz's law for the direction of an induced current in a loop.
5. **Vector or scalar**: field and force are vectors (add as vectors); potential and energy are scalars.
6. **Capacitor changes**: decide connected ($U$ constant) or isolated ($Q$ constant).
7. **Magnetic force does no work**; use $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$.

In our reference mock papers EM typically takes about $7$ of $48$ questions.

## 2. Worked strategy

*"A $12\ \text{V}$ battery with $2\ \Omega$ internal resistance drives a $4\ \Omega$ load."* $I = \dfrac{12}6 = 2\ \text{A}$; terminal voltage $8\ \text{V}$; load power $16\ \text{W}$.

*"Two $6\ \Omega$ resistors in parallel in series with $3\ \Omega$ across $12\ \text{V}$."* $3 + 3 = 6\ \Omega$, $I = 2\ \text{A}$, $U_{\text{parallel}} = 6\ \text{V}$.

*"A transformer has $100$ primary and $500$ secondary turns with $20\ \text{V}$ in."* $U_2 = 100\ \text{V}$; the current falls by the same factor, the power is unchanged.

## 3. Traps

- Skipping the diagram.
- Adding parallel resistors directly.
- Using the resistor rule for capacitors.
- Adding electric fields as scalars.
- Assuming a step-up transformer increases power.
- Mixing left- and right-hand rules.

## Quick self-test

1. Two $6\ \Omega$ resistors in parallel in series with $3\ \Omega$ across $12\ \text{V}$: current.
2. Force on $0.5\ \text{m}$ of wire with $4\ \text{A}$ in $0.2\ \text{T}$ (perpendicular).
3. Field $0.5\ \text{m}$ from $5\ \text{nC}$.

**Answers.** (1) $2\ \text{A}$. (2) $0.4\ \text{N}$. (3) $180\ \text{N/C}$.
