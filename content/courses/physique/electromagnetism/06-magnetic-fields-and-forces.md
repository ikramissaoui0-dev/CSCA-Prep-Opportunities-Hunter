## What you will learn

- the magnetic field and its direction;
- the force on a moving charge and the circular motion it causes;
- the force on a current-carrying wire; applications.

## 1. Magnetic field

The magnetic field $\vec B$ (tesla, $1\ \text{T} = 1\ \text{N/(A m)}$) has the direction from N to S **outside** a magnet; field lines are closed loops and never cross. Magnetic poles come in pairs (no monopoles). Compass needles align with Earth's field.

## 2. Force on a moving charge

$$
\vec F = q\vec v \times \vec B, \qquad F = qvB\sin\theta.
$$

- The force is perpendicular to both $\vec v$ and $\vec B$; for positive charges use the **left-hand rule** (左手定则: palm facing the N pole of $\vec B$, fingers along the velocity, thumb gives the force); reverse for negative charges.
- The force does **no work**: it changes the direction, never the speed.
- If $\vec v \parallel \vec B$: no force.
- For $\vec v \perp \vec B$ the path is a circle:

$$
qvB = \frac{mv^2}r \ \Rightarrow\ r = \frac{mv}{qB}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi m}{qB} \ (\text{independent of } v).
$$

If $\vec v$ has a component along $\vec B$ the path is a **helix**.

**Example 1.** A proton at $10^6\ \text{m/s}$ perpendicular to $B = 0.1\ \text{T}$: $r = \dfrac{1.67 \times 10^{-27} \cdot 10^6}{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \cdot 0.1} \approx 0.10\ \text{m}$.

**Example 2.** An electron at $2 \times 10^7\ \text{m/s}$ in $5 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{T}$: $r \approx 0.23\ \text{m}$, $T \approx 7.1 \times 10^{-8}\ \text{s}$.

**Example 3.** Two ions with the same charge and speed, masses in ratio $1 : 2$: radii in ratio $1 : 2$.

## 3. Field regions

A particle crossing a region of uniform field follows an arc; the time in the field is $t = \dfrac\alpha{2\pi}T = \dfrac{\alpha m}{qB}$ with $\alpha$ the angle swept. The centre of the circle lies on the perpendicular to the velocity at the entry point and on the perpendicular bisector of the chord between entry and exit points.

**Velocity selector**: crossed $\vec E$ and $\vec B$; only particles with $v = \dfrac EB$ go straight. **Mass spectrometer**: ions of equal speed bend with $r \propto \dfrac mq$. **Cyclotron**: $T$ is independent of speed.

## 4. Force on a current

$$
\vec F = I\vec L \times \vec B, \qquad F = BIL\sin\theta.
$$

Direction by the left-hand rule (fingers along the current). A rectangular coil in a uniform field feels no net force but a torque $\tau = NBIA\sin\theta$ (the electric motor).

**Example 4.** A $0.5\ \text{m}$ wire carrying $4\ \text{A}$ perpendicular to $0.2\ \text{T}$: $F = 0.4\ \text{N}$.

**Example 5 (floating rod).** A horizontal rod of mass $m$ and length $L$ in a horizontal perpendicular field floats when $BIL = mg$, so $B = \dfrac{mg}{IL}$.

**Hall effect.** Charges in a current-carrying conductor in a field pile up on one side until the transverse electric force balances the magnetic force: $qE_H = qvB$.

## Common mistakes

- Using the right-hand rule for negative charges without reversing.
- Believing a magnetic field can speed up a charge.
- Forgetting $\sin\theta$.

## Practice problems

1. Force on $20\ \text{cm}$ of wire carrying $5\ \text{A}$ at $90^\circ$ to $0.3\ \text{T}$.
2. Radius for an electron at $4 \times 10^7\ \text{m/s}$ in $2 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{T}$.

**Answers.** (1) $0.3\ \text{N}$. (2) $\approx 0.114\ \text{m}$.
