## What you will learn

- light as an electromagnetic wave and the spectrum;
- polarisation and Malus's law;
- speed, frequency and wavelength in media.

## 1. Light as a wave

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave: $c = f\lambda = 3 \times 10^8\ \text{m/s}$ in vacuum. In order of increasing frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible (red $\approx 700\ \text{nm}$ to violet $\approx 400\ \text{nm}$), ultraviolet, X-rays, $\gamma$-rays. Visible frequencies are about $4.3$–$7.5 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$.

**Example 1.** $\lambda = 600\ \text{nm}$: $f = \dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{6 \times 10^{-7}} = 5 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$. In glass ($n = 1.5$): $v = 2 \times 10^8\ \text{m/s}$, $\lambda = 400\ \text{nm}$; $f$ is unchanged.

## 2. Evidence that light is a wave

Interference, diffraction and **polarisation**. Only **transverse** waves can be polarised; sound (longitudinal) cannot.

## 3. Polarisation and Malus's law

Unpolarised light passing through a **polariser** becomes plane-polarised with half the intensity. A second polariser (the **analyser**) at angle $\theta$ to the first transmits

$$
I = I_0\cos^2\theta \quad (\text{Malus's law}).
$$

**Example 2.** Polarised light of $100\ \text{W/m}^2$ meets an analyser at $60^\circ$: $I = 100\cos^260^\circ = 25\ \text{W/m}^2$.

**Example 3.** Unpolarised light through two polarisers at $45^\circ$: $\dfrac{I_0}2\cos^245^\circ = \dfrac{I_0}4$.

**Uses**: polarising sunglasses (cut glare reflected from water and roads), LCD screens, stress analysis in plastics, 3D cinema. Light can also be polarised by reflection (Brewster's angle) and scattering (the blue sky).

## 4. Comparing colours

Red has the longest wavelength: the widest fringes, the largest diffraction angles, and the smallest refractive index. Violet has the shortest wavelength, the narrowest fringes and the largest $n$. Sunlight dispersed by a prism or raindrops gives the spectrum from red to violet.

**Example 4.** Which colour of a double-slit pattern is nearest the central bright fringe? Violet ($\Delta y \propto \lambda$).

## 5. The speed of light and electromagnetic waves

All electromagnetic waves travel at $c$ in vacuum; in a medium the speed is $\dfrac cn$, and $n$ depends on frequency (dispersion). Light carries energy and momentum: radiation pressure; photons carry $E = hf$ (modern physics).

## Common mistakes

- Thinking the frequency changes in a medium.
- Applying Malus's law to unpolarised light directly (halve first).
- Assuming sound can be polarised.

## Practice problems

1. Wavelength of a $5 \times 10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$ wave in vacuum.
2. Intensity after an analyser at $30^\circ$ for polarised light of $80\ \text{W/m}^2$.

**Answers.** (1) $600\ \text{nm}$. (2) $80 \cdot 0.75 = 60\ \text{W/m}^2$.
