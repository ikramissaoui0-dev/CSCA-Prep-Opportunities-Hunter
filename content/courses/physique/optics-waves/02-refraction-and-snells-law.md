## What you will learn

- refractive index and Snell's law;
- total internal reflection and the critical angle;
- dispersion, apparent depth and prisms.

## 1. Refractive index and Snell's law

The refractive index $n = \dfrac cv$ ($n \ge 1$). When light crosses a boundary, its speed and wavelength change but its **frequency stays the same**: $\lambda = \dfrac{\lambda_0}n$.

$$
n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2.
$$

Into a denser medium ($n_2 > n_1$) the ray bends **toward** the normal; into a rarer medium, away from it.

**Example 1.** Light enters glass ($n = 1.5$) at $30^\circ$: $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{0.5}{1.5} = 0.333$, $\theta_2 \approx 19.5^\circ$.

**Example 2.** Light travels at $2 \times 10^8\ \text{m/s}$ in a medium: $n = 1.5$.

**Example 3.** Light from water ($n = 1.33$) into glass ($n = 1.5$) at $40^\circ$: $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{1.33 \cdot 0.643}{1.5} = 0.570$, $\theta_2 \approx 34.8^\circ$.

## 2. Total internal reflection

Going from a denser to a rarer medium ($n_1 > n_2$), beyond the **critical angle** no light is refracted:

$$
\sin\theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1} \quad (\text{for glass to air: } \sin\theta_c = \tfrac1n).
$$

Glass: $\theta_c \approx 41.8^\circ$; water: $\theta_c \approx 48.8^\circ$; diamond ($n = 2.42$): $24^\circ$, which explains its sparkle. Applications: optical fibres, binoculars' prisms, reflectors.

Conditions for TIR: (1) the light goes from the denser to the rarer medium; (2) the angle of incidence is at least $\theta_c$.

## 3. Apparent depth and other effects

Looking straight down into water of true depth $d$, the apparent depth is $\dfrac dn$: a $4\ \text{m}$ pool looks about $3\ \text{m}$ deep. A glass slab shifts the ray sideways but emerges parallel to the incident ray. A thin prism of apex angle $A$ deviates a ray by $\delta \approx (n - 1)A$.

## 4. Dispersion

$n$ depends on wavelength: $n_{\text{violet}} > n_{\text{red}}$, so violet travels slower and bends more. A prism splits white light into a spectrum (red deviated least, violet most); raindrops produce rainbows by refraction, internal reflection and dispersion.

**Example 4.** Two colours cross the same glass: the one with the greater $n$ has the smaller speed ($v = c/n$) and the shorter wavelength in glass; the frequencies are equal.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring angles from the surface.
- Applying TIR from the rarer to the denser medium.
- Thinking the frequency changes on refraction.

## Practice problems

1. Critical angle for water ($n = 1.33$) to air.
2. Speed of light in glass of $n = 1.5$.
3. Apparent depth of an object $3\ \text{m}$ below the surface of water ($n = 1.33$).

**Answers.** (1) $\arcsin\dfrac1{1.33} \approx 48.8^\circ$. (2) $2 \times 10^8\ \text{m/s}$. (3) $\approx 2.26\ \text{m}$.
