## What you will learn

- converging and diverging lenses and the principal rays;
- the thin-lens equation, magnification and power;
- image types, combined lenses and the eye.

## 1. Lenses and principal rays

A **converging** (convex) lens has $f > 0$; a **diverging** (concave) lens has $f < 0$. Principal rays: (1) parallel to the axis $\to$ through the focus; (2) through the centre $\to$ undeviated; (3) through the focus $\to$ parallel to the axis.

## 2. Thin-lens equation

$$
\frac1f = \frac1{d_i} + \frac1{d_o}, \qquad m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}, \qquad P = \frac1f\ (\text{diopters, } f \text{ in metres}).
$$

$d_i > 0$: real image (opposite side of the lens); $d_i < 0$: virtual image (same side as the object); $m < 0$: inverted.

| Object (convex lens) | Image |
|---|---|
| $d_o > 2f$ | real, inverted, reduced (camera) |
| $d_o = 2f$ | real, inverted, same size |
| $f < d_o < 2f$ | real, inverted, enlarged (projector) |
| $d_o = f$ | image at infinity (parallel beam) |
| $d_o < f$ | virtual, upright, enlarged (magnifying glass) |
| $d_o \to \infty$ | image at the focus |

A **diverging lens** always gives a virtual, upright, reduced image.

**Example 1.** Convex lens, $f = 15\ \text{cm}$, object at $30\ \text{cm}$: $d_i = 30\ \text{cm}$, $m = -1$ (same-size inverted real image).

**Example 2.** $f = 10\ \text{cm}$, object at $5\ \text{cm}$: $d_i = -10\ \text{cm}$ (virtual), $m = +2$.

**Example 3.** Diverging lens $f = -20\ \text{cm}$, object at $40\ \text{cm}$: $\dfrac1{d_i} = -\dfrac1{20} - \dfrac1{40} = -\dfrac3{40}$, $d_i = -13.3\ \text{cm}$, $m = +\dfrac13$.

**Example 4 (strategy).** An object $15\ \text{cm}$ from a converging lens with $f = 10\ \text{cm}$: $d_i = 30\ \text{cm}$, real, inverted, $m = -2$ (twice the size).

## 3. Power and combined lenses

For thin lenses in contact, $P = P_1 + P_2$ ($\dfrac1f = \dfrac1{f_1} + \dfrac1{f_2}$). A $20\ \text{cm}$ lens has $P = 5\ \text{D}$. An **achromatic doublet** combines a converging and a diverging lens of different glasses to cancel chromatic aberration.

## 4. The eye and instruments

The eye forms a real, inverted image on the retina. **Myopia** (short-sighted): image falls in front of the retina; corrected by a **diverging** lens. **Hyperopia** (long-sighted): image behind the retina; corrected by a **converging** lens. A magnifier has angular magnification $\approx \dfrac{25\ \text{cm}}f$; a camera, projector and microscope use the real-image cases above.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing sign conventions in the lens equation.
- Using the wrong lens for a given eye defect.
- Forgetting that a virtual image cannot be projected on a screen.

## Practice problems

1. $f = 8\ \text{cm}$, object at $12\ \text{cm}$: $d_i$ and $m$.
2. Power of a lens with $f = 25\ \text{cm}$.

**Answers.** (1) $24\ \text{cm}$; $m = -2$. (2) $4\ \text{D}$.
