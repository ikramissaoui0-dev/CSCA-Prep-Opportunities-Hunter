## What you will learn

- the law of reflection and plane mirrors;
- spherical mirrors, the mirror equation and magnification;
- aberrations and polarisation by reflection.

## 1. Law of reflection

The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection ($\theta_i = \theta_r$), both measured from the **normal**; the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in one plane. A **plane mirror** forms a virtual, upright image the same size as the object, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. Rotating a mirror by $\alpha$ turns the reflected ray by $2\alpha$.

## 2. Spherical mirrors

A concave mirror with radius of curvature $R$ has focal length $f = \dfrac R2$ and converges parallel rays; a convex mirror diverges them (virtual focus, $f < 0$). With object distance $d_o$ and image distance $d_i$:

$$
\frac1f = \frac1{d_i} + \frac1{d_o}, \qquad m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}.
$$

$d_i > 0$: real image (in front of the mirror); $d_i < 0$: virtual (behind); $m < 0$: inverted; $|m| > 1$: enlarged.

| Concave mirror, object position | Image |
|---|---|
| beyond $2f$ | real, inverted, reduced |
| at $2f$ | real, inverted, same size |
| between $f$ and $2f$ | real, inverted, enlarged |
| inside $f$ | virtual, upright, enlarged (shaving mirror) |

A convex mirror always gives a virtual, upright, reduced image (wide field of view: car mirrors).

**Example 1.** Concave, $f = 20\ \text{cm}$, object at $30\ \text{cm}$: $\dfrac1{d_i} = \dfrac1{20} - \dfrac1{30} = \dfrac1{60}$, $d_i = 60\ \text{cm}$ (real), $m = -2$.

**Example 2.** Same mirror, object at $10\ \text{cm}$: $\dfrac1{d_i} = \dfrac1{20} - \dfrac1{10} = -\dfrac1{20}$, $d_i = -20\ \text{cm}$ (virtual), $m = +2$.

**Example 3.** Convex mirror $f = -15\ \text{cm}$, object at $30\ \text{cm}$: $d_i = -10\ \text{cm}$, $m = +\dfrac13$.

## 3. Aberrations

*Spherical aberration*: rays far from the axis focus at a different point, blurring the image; a **parabolic** mirror removes it (telescopes, headlights). *Chromatic aberration* affects lenses (colours focus at different points), not mirrors.

## 4. Reflection and polarisation

Reflected light is partially polarised; it is completely polarised when the reflected and refracted rays are at $90^\circ$ (**Brewster's angle**): $\tan\theta_B = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$ (about $53^\circ$ for air to water). Polarising sunglasses cut glare this way.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring angles from the surface instead of the normal.
- Mixing up sign conventions for $d_i$.
- Saying a plane mirror reverses left and right (it reverses front and back).

## Practice problems

1. A plane mirror $2\ \text{m}$ from you: distance to your image.
2. Concave mirror $f = 10\ \text{cm}$, object at $15\ \text{cm}$: $d_i$ and $m$.

**Answers.** (1) $4\ \text{m}$. (2) $30\ \text{cm}$, $m = -2$.
