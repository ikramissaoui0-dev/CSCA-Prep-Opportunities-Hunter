## What you will learn

- how to approach optics and wave questions;
- worked strategy examples and traps.

## 1. Strategy

1. **Sketch the ray and draw the normal**; measure all angles from the normal.
2. **Check for total internal reflection** when light goes from a denser to a rarer medium.
3. **Lens and mirror problems**: apply $\dfrac1f = \dfrac1{d_i} + \dfrac1{d_o}$ with one sign convention; then read $d_i$ (real/virtual) and $m$ (inverted/upright, size).
4. **Convert units** ($\text{nm}$, $\text{mm}$, $\text{cm}$) before substituting.
5. **Count spacings, not fringes.**
6. **Frequency never changes** across a boundary; speed and wavelength do.
7. **Standing waves**: draw the node/antinode pattern; fixed end = node; open pipe end = antinode.
8. **SHM**: period of a spring or pendulum is independent of amplitude.

In our reference mock papers optics and waves take about $5$ of $48$ questions.

## 2. Worked strategy

*"An object sits $15\ \text{cm}$ from a converging lens with $f = 10\ \text{cm}$."* $d_i = 30\ \text{cm}$, $m = -2$: real, inverted, twice the size.

*"Light of $500\ \text{nm}$ falls on a metal with work function $2\ \text{eV}$."* (modern physics link) $E = 2.48\ \text{eV} > 2$: emission; photon energy $= 1240/\lambda$.

*"A double slit has $d = 0.2\ \text{mm}$, $L = 1\ \text{m}$ and $\lambda = 600\ \text{nm}$."* $\Delta y = 3\ \text{mm}$; five fringes span $4\Delta y = 12\ \text{mm}$.

## 3. Traps

- Skipping the ray diagram.
- Applying TIR from rarer to denser.
- Thinking frequency changes on refraction.
- Using the wrong pipe formula (open vs closed).
- Counting fringes rather than spacings.

## Quick self-test

1. Critical angle from glass ($n = 1.5$) to air.
2. Pendulum of length $1\ \text{m}$: period ($g = 9.8$).
3. Fundamental frequency of a $1\ \text{m}$ string with $v = 200\ \text{m/s}$.

**Answers.** (1) $41.8^\circ$. (2) $2.0\ \text{s}$. (3) $100\ \text{Hz}$.
