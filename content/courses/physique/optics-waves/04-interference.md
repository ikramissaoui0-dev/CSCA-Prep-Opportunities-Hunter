## What you will learn

- superposition and coherent sources;
- Young's double-slit experiment;
- thin-film interference.

## 1. Superposition and interference

When waves overlap, the displacements add (**superposition**). **Interference** produces a stable pattern only if the sources are **coherent** (same frequency and a constant phase difference). At a point where the **path difference** is $\Delta$ (for sources in phase):

- **constructive** (bright, maximum): $\Delta = m\lambda$, $m = 0, 1, 2, \dots$
- **destructive** (dark, minimum): $\Delta = \left(m + \tfrac12\right)\lambda$.

(For sources in antiphase the conditions swap.) Interference is evidence that light is a wave.

## 2. Young's double slit

Slit separation $d$, screen distance $L \gg d$:

$$
\Delta y = \frac{L\lambda}d \ (\text{fringe spacing}), \qquad y_m = \frac{mL\lambda}d, \qquad \lambda = \frac{d\,\Delta y}L.
$$

Larger $\lambda$ or $L$ and smaller $d$ spread the fringes. White light: a white central fringe flanked by coloured fringes (violet nearest, red outermost).

**Example 1.** $d = 0.2\ \text{mm}$, $L = 1.0\ \text{m}$, $\lambda = 600\ \text{nm}$: $\Delta y = \dfrac{1 \cdot 6 \times 10^{-7}}{2 \times 10^{-4}} = 3\ \text{mm}$.

**Example 2.** The third bright fringe is $9\ \text{mm}$ from the centre with $L = 1\ \text{m}$ and $d = 0.1\ \text{mm}$: $\lambda = \dfrac{y\,d}{3L} = 300\ \text{nm}$.

**Example 3.** A path difference of $1.2\ \mu\text{m}$ for $\lambda = 0.6\ \mu\text{m}$ is $2\lambda$ (bright); $0.9\ \mu\text{m}$ is $1.5\lambda$ (dark).

**Counting fringes.** If $N$ bright fringes span a distance $D$, the spacing is $\dfrac D{N - 1}$ (count **spacings**, not fringes). Immersing the setup in a liquid divides $\lambda$, and so $\Delta y$, by $n$.

## 3. Thin films

Soap bubbles, oil on water and anti-reflection coatings: light reflected from the top and bottom surfaces interferes. A reflection from a medium of **higher** refractive index causes a phase change of $\pi$ (half a wavelength). For a coating of thickness $t$ and index $n$ with phase changes at both surfaces (or at neither), the reflected rays are in antiphase when

$$
2nt = \left(m + \tfrac12\right)\lambda \quad (\text{minimum thickness } t = \tfrac{\lambda}{4n}),
$$

which is the **anti-reflection coating** condition. Colours of soap films change with thickness.

## 4. Other examples

Newton's rings, the air wedge between two glass plates, and sound interference (two speakers) follow the same path-difference rules.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the bright and dark conditions.
- Using the wavelength in vacuum inside a medium.
- Counting fringes instead of spacings.

## Practice problems

1. $d = 0.5\ \text{mm}$, $L = 2\ \text{m}$, $\lambda = 500\ \text{nm}$: fringe spacing.
2. Minimum thickness of an anti-reflection coating ($n = 1.25$) for $\lambda = 500\ \text{nm}$ (phase changes at both surfaces).

**Answers.** (1) $2\ \text{mm}$. (2) $\dfrac{500}{4 \cdot 1.25} = 100\ \text{nm}$.
