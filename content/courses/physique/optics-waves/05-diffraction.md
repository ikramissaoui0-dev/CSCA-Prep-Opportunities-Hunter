## What you will learn

- diffraction and when it is noticeable;
- the single-slit pattern and the diffraction grating;
- resolution.

## 1. Diffraction

Waves spread around obstacles and through openings. Diffraction is noticeable when the opening or obstacle is **comparable to the wavelength** (a ripple tank, sound around a corner, light through a narrow slit). It is the reason light does not form sharp shadows for tiny objects.

## 2. Single slit

For a slit of width $a$, dark fringes occur at

$$
a\sin\theta = m\lambda, \qquad m = \pm1, \pm2, \dots
$$

The central bright band is **twice as wide** as the others and the brightest. A **narrower slit gives a wider pattern**; a longer wavelength also widens it.

**Example 1.** $a = 0.1\ \text{mm}$, $\lambda = 600\ \text{nm}$, screen at $2\ \text{m}$: first minimum at $y = \dfrac{\lambda L}a = 1.2\ \text{cm}$, so the central maximum is $2.4\ \text{cm}$ wide.

## 3. Diffraction grating

A grating with spacing $d$ gives bright lines at

$$
d\sin\theta = m\lambda, \qquad m = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \dots
$$

The lines are narrower and brighter than for a double slit; gratings measure wavelengths accurately. Since $\sin\theta \le 1$, the highest order is $m_{\max} = \left\lfloor\dfrac d\lambda\right\rfloor$.

**Example 2.** A grating with $5000$ lines/cm: $d = \dfrac{1\ \text{cm}}{5000} = 2\ \mu\text{m}$. For $\lambda = 500\ \text{nm}$: $m_{\max} = 4$; first order: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{0.5}2 = 0.25$, $\theta \approx 14.5^\circ$.

**Example 3.** White light on a grating: each order (except the central one) is a spectrum, with violet nearest the centre and red farthest, since $\sin\theta \propto \lambda$.

## 4. Resolution and applications

Diffraction limits what an instrument can resolve: for a circular aperture of diameter $D$, $\theta_{\min} \approx 1.22\dfrac\lambda D$ (the Rayleigh criterion). Larger telescope mirrors and shorter wavelengths (electron microscopes) give finer detail. Diffraction of X-rays by crystals reveals atomic structure.

## Common mistakes

- Using $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ (grating) for the single-slit minima.
- Forgetting that a narrower slit spreads the pattern more.
- Mixing the grating spacing and the number of lines.

## Practice problems

1. A grating has $400$ lines/mm; $\lambda = 600\ \text{nm}$: $d$ and the highest order.
2. Single slit $a = 0.2\ \text{mm}$, $\lambda = 500\ \text{nm}$: angle of the first minimum.

**Answers.** (1) $d = 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$; $m_{\max} = 4$. (2) $\sin\theta = 0.0025$, $\theta \approx 0.14^\circ$.
