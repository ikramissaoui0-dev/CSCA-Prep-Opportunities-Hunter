## What you will learn

- nuclear composition and the strong force;
- $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ decay and balanced nuclear equations;
- artificial transmutation and decay in a magnetic field.

## 1. The nucleus

A nucleus ${}^A_ZX$ has $Z$ protons and $N = A - Z$ neutrons ($A$ = mass number, $Z$ = proton/atomic number). **Isotopes** share $Z$ but differ in $N$. Nucleons are held together by the **strong force**: attractive, very short range ($\sim 10^{-15}\ \text{m}$), much stronger than the electric repulsion between protons. Atomic mass unit: $1\ \text{u} = 1.66 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$, $1\ \text{u}\cdot c^2 = 931.5\ \text{MeV}$.

## 2. Radioactive decay

Spontaneous, random, unaffected by temperature, pressure or chemical state. In every nuclear reaction **mass number** and **charge** are conserved.

| Decay | Emitted | Change | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | ${}^4_2\text{He}$ | $A - 4$, $Z - 2$ | most ionising, stopped by paper |
| $\beta^-$ | electron (+ antineutrino) | $A$ same, $Z + 1$ (neutron $\to$ proton) | stopped by a few mm of aluminium |
| $\beta^+$ | positron (+ neutrino) | $A$ same, $Z - 1$ | |
| $\gamma$ | photon | no change in $A$, $Z$ | least ionising, needs thick lead |

**Example 1.** ${}^{238}_{92}\text{U} \to {}^{234}_{90}\text{Th} + {}^4_2\text{He}$.

**Example 2.** ${}^{14}_6\text{C} \to {}^{14}_7\text{N} + {}^{\,0}_{-1}e + \bar\nu$.

**Example 3 (decay chain).** ${}^{232}_{90}\text{Th} \to {}^{208}_{82}\text{Pb}$: $\Delta A = 24$ so $6$ $\alpha$ decays; these lower $Z$ by $12$ but $Z$ only falls by $8$, so there are $4$ $\beta^-$ decays.

## 3. Artificial transmutation

- Rutherford (1919): ${}^{14}_7\text{N} + {}^4_2\text{He} \to {}^{17}_8\text{O} + {}^1_1\text{H}$ — discovery of the **proton**.
- Chadwick (1932): ${}^9_4\text{Be} + {}^4_2\text{He} \to {}^{12}_6\text{C} + {}^1_0n$ — discovery of the **neutron**.
- The Joliot-Curies produced the first artificial radioactive isotope; positrons were found in cosmic rays.

**Example 4.** Identify $X$ in ${}^9_4\text{Be} + {}^4_2\text{He} \to {}^{12}_6\text{C} + X$: mass $13 - 12 = 1$, charge $6 - 6 = 0$: a neutron.

## 4. Decay in a magnetic field

A stationary nucleus decays into two products with equal and opposite momenta. In a uniform field $r = \dfrac p{qB}$, so the product with the **smaller charge** has the larger circle. After $\alpha$ decay (two same-sign charges moving in opposite directions) the circles touch **externally**; after $\beta^-$ decay (opposite charges) they touch **internally**, the electron's circle being much larger.

## 5. Safety

Dose depends on activity, time, distance and shielding. $\gamma$ needs lead or thick concrete, $\beta$ a few millimetres of aluminium, $\alpha$ only paper (but is dangerous if inhaled or swallowed). Applications: medical imaging and radiotherapy, sterilisation, tracers, smoke detectors, dating.

## Common mistakes

- Not balancing both $A$ and $Z$.
- Confusing a $\beta$ particle (from the nucleus) with an orbital electron.
- Thinking decay can be sped up by heating.

## Practice problems

1. Complete ${}^{226}_{88}\text{Ra} \to {}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn} + ?$
2. After $\beta^-$ decay of ${}^{14}_6\text{C}$, what is the daughter nucleus?
3. ${}^{235}\text{U}$ to ${}^{207}\text{Pb}$ ($Z = 92 \to 82$): number of $\alpha$ and $\beta^-$ decays.

**Answers.** (1) ${}^4_2\text{He}$. (2) ${}^{14}_7\text{N}$. (3) $7$ $\alpha$ and $4$ $\beta^-$.
