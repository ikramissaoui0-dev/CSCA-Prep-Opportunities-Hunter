## What you will learn

- Bohr's postulates;
- the energy levels and radii of hydrogen;
- transitions, ionisation and excitation by photons and electrons.

## 1. Bohr's postulates

1. Electrons move in certain **stationary orbits** (定态) without radiating; each has a definite energy.
2. Radiation is emitted or absorbed only when an electron makes a **transition** (跃迁) between orbits: $hf = |E_m - E_n|$.
3. Allowed orbits have quantised angular momentum $L = n\dfrac h{2\pi}$.

## 2. Hydrogen levels

$$
E_n = \frac{E_1}{n^2} = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}, \qquad r_n = n^2r_1, \quad r_1 = 0.053\ \text{nm}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots
$$

The ground state ($n = 1$) has $E_1 = -13.6\ \text{eV}$; the **ionisation energy** is $13.6\ \text{eV}$ (to reach $E = 0$). The levels get closer together as $n$ increases. Excited states are unstable (lifetime about $10^{-8}\ \text{s}$).

| $n$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $\infty$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $E_n$ (eV) | $-13.6$ | $-3.40$ | $-1.51$ | $-0.85$ | $0$ |

**Example 1.** $n = 4 \to 2$: $E = -0.85 - (-3.40) = 2.55\ \text{eV}$, $\lambda = \dfrac{1240}{2.55} \approx 486\ \text{nm}$ (blue-green). $n = 3 \to 2$: $1.89\ \text{eV}$, $656\ \text{nm}$ (red).

## 3. Absorption and excitation

- A **photon** is absorbed only if its energy equals a gap exactly ($10.2$, $12.09$, $12.75\ \text{eV}$ for $n = 1 \to 2, 3, 4$) or exceeds the ionisation energy ($\ge 13.6\ \text{eV}$; the excess becomes the electron's kinetic energy).
- A colliding **electron** can excite the atom if its kinetic energy is at least the gap; it keeps the rest.

**Example 2.** Ground-state hydrogen is struck by a photon of $12\ \text{eV}$: not absorbed (no level gap $= 12$). By a $12\ \text{eV}$ electron: it can excite the atom to $n = 2$ (using $10.2\ \text{eV}$) and keep $1.8\ \text{eV}$.

**Example 3.** A $14\ \text{eV}$ photon ionises hydrogen; the freed electron has $0.4\ \text{eV}$.

**Number of lines.** A sample of atoms excited to level $n$ can emit $\dfrac{n(n - 1)}2$ different lines; one atom emits a single photon per transition. From $n = 4$: $6$ lines; from $n = 3$: $3$ lines.

## 4. Other quantities

The orbit speed and kinetic energy decrease as $n$ increases; the total energy rises toward $0$. For hydrogen-like ions ($\text{He}^+$, $\text{Li}^{2+}$): $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6Z^2}{n^2}\ \text{eV}$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the energies are **negative**.
- Thinking a photon can be partly absorbed.
- Counting emitted lines wrongly for a single atom.

## Practice problems

1. Energy emitted for $n = 3 \to 1$.
2. Wavelength of the photon emitted for $n = 3 \to 2$.
3. Number of different lines when atoms are excited to $n = 5$.

**Answers.** (1) $12.09\ \text{eV}$. (2) $656\ \text{nm}$. (3) $10$.
