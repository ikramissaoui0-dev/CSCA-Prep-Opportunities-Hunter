## What you will learn

- the wave and particle behaviour of light;
- the de Broglie wavelength;
- the uncertainty principle and probability waves.

## 1. Duality of light

Light shows **wave** behaviour (interference, diffraction, polarisation) and **particle** behaviour (photoelectric effect, Compton scattering). A photon has energy $E = hf$ and momentum

$$
p = \frac h\lambda.
$$

**Compton effect**: X-rays scattered by electrons come out with a longer wavelength; the photon loses energy and momentum to the electron, as in a particle collision.

**Example 1.** The momentum of a $500\ \text{nm}$ photon: $p = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{5 \times 10^{-7}} = 1.33 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg m/s}$.

## 2. Matter waves (de Broglie)

Every moving particle has a wavelength

$$
\lambda = \frac hp = \frac h{mv}.
$$

Electron diffraction by crystals (Davisson–Germer) confirmed it. For an electron accelerated through $U$ volts: $\lambda = \dfrac h{\sqrt{2meU}} \approx \dfrac{1.23}{\sqrt U}\ \text{nm}$.

**Example 2.** A $1\ \text{g}$ ball at $1\ \text{m/s}$: $\lambda \approx 7 \times 10^{-31}\ \text{m}$ — unobservable. An electron at $10^6\ \text{m/s}$: $\lambda \approx 7.3 \times 10^{-10}\ \text{m}$, comparable with atomic spacing, so diffraction is observable.

**Example 3.** An electron and a proton at the same speed: $\lambda \propto \dfrac1m$, so the electron's wavelength is about $1836$ times longer. At the same kinetic energy, $p = \sqrt{2mE_k}$ and the lighter particle again has the longer wavelength.

**Electron microscopes** beat optical ones because electrons have a much shorter wavelength (greater resolution).

## 3. Probability and uncertainty

Matter waves are **probability waves**: the square of the amplitude gives the probability of finding the particle at a place (single electrons build up a diffraction pattern one dot at a time). The **Heisenberg uncertainty principle**:

$$
\Delta x\,\Delta p \ge \frac h{4\pi}.
$$

You cannot know the position and momentum of a particle both with arbitrary precision. The same holds for energy and time.

## 4. Summary table

| Phenomenon | Shows |
|---|---|
| Interference, diffraction, polarisation of light | wave |
| Photoelectric effect, Compton effect | particle |
| Electron diffraction | wave behaviour of matter |
| Tracks in a cloud chamber | particle behaviour |

## Common mistakes

- Using $\lambda = h/(mv)$ for a photon (use $h/p$ with $p = E/c$).
- Thinking duality means a particle is sometimes a wave and sometimes a particle *at once* in a classical sense.
- Forgetting that everyday objects do have wavelengths, just immeasurably small.

## Practice problems

1. de Broglie wavelength of an electron at $2 \times 10^6\ \text{m/s}$ ($m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}$).
2. Photon momentum for $\lambda = 600\ \text{nm}$.

**Answers.** (1) $\dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.82 \times 10^{-24}} = 3.6 \times 10^{-10}\ \text{m}$. (2) $1.1 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg m/s}$.
