## What you will learn

- nuclear fission and the chain reaction;
- the parts of a fission reactor;
- fusion, its conditions and its advantages.

## 1. Fission

A heavy nucleus (such as ${}^{235}\text{U}$) absorbs a slow neutron and splits into two medium nuclei plus $2$–$3$ neutrons, releasing about $200\ \text{MeV}$:

$$
{}^{235}_{92}\text{U} + {}^1_0n \to {}^{141}_{56}\text{Ba} + {}^{92}_{36}\text{Kr} + 3\,{}^1_0n + \text{energy}.
$$

The released neutrons can split other nuclei: a **chain reaction**. A **critical mass** (volume) is needed so that, on average, at least one neutron per fission causes another fission. Uncontrolled: an atomic bomb. Controlled: a reactor.

**Example 1.** Find $x$ in ${}^{235}_{92}\text{U} + {}^1_0n \to {}^{136}_{54}\text{Xe} + {}^{90}_{38}\text{Sr} + x\,{}^1_0n$: mass numbers $236 = 226 + x$, so $x = 10$ (check charge: $92 = 54 + 38$ ✓).

## 2. Parts of a fission reactor

- **Fuel rods**: enriched uranium (or plutonium).
- **Moderator** (graphite, heavy or ordinary water): slows fast neutrons so that they are more likely to cause fission.
- **Control rods** (cadmium, boron): absorb neutrons; pushed in to slow or stop the reaction, pulled out to speed it up.
- **Coolant** (water, $\text{CO}_2$, sodium): carries heat to a heat exchanger and steam turbine.
- **Shielding**: thick concrete and steel against radiation.

**Issues**: radioactive waste (long half-lives, long storage), the risk of accidents, but no $\text{CO}_2$ emitted during operation. A reactor produces much more energy per kilogram of fuel than coal (about $3 \times 10^6$ times).

## 3. Fusion

Light nuclei combine into a heavier one, releasing energy:

$$
{}^2_1\text{H} + {}^3_1\text{H} \to {}^4_2\text{He} + {}^1_0n + 17.6\ \text{MeV}.
$$

Fusion needs temperatures of about $10^8\ \text{K}$ to overcome the Coulomb repulsion between nuclei, and **confinement**: magnetic (tokamak) or inertial (lasers). It powers the Sun (hydrogen to helium). Per unit mass, fusion releases more energy than fission; the fuel (deuterium from seawater) is plentiful and the waste is much less long-lived, but sustained controlled fusion is not yet commercial.

**Example 2.** ${}^2_1\text{H} + {}^2_1\text{H} \to {}^3_2\text{He} + {}^1_0n$: $\Delta m = 0.0035\ \text{u}$, giving $3.3\ \text{MeV}$.

## 4. Comparison

| | Fission | Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclei | heavy ($A$ large) | light ($A$ small) |
| Energy per reaction | $\approx 200\ \text{MeV}$ | $17.6\ \text{MeV}$ ($D$–$T$) |
| Energy per kg | high | higher |
| Conditions | slow neutrons, critical mass | $10^8\ \text{K}$ and confinement |
| Waste | long-lived radioactive | little long-lived |

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a moderator absorbs neutrons (it slows them; control rods absorb).
- Forgetting that mass number and charge balance in the equations.
- Saying fusion happens at low temperatures.
