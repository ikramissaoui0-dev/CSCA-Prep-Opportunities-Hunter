## What you will learn

- the equation of state $pV = nRT$;
- Boyle's, Charles's and Gay-Lussac's laws;
- pressure of an enclosed gas (pistons, liquid columns) and the units to use.

## 1. The equation of state

$$
pV = nRT = Nk_BT, \qquad R = 8.31\ \text{J/(mol K)}, \quad k_B = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\ \text{J/K}.
$$

**Always use kelvin** and consistent units ($\text{Pa}$, $\text{m}^3$) or ($\text{atm}$, $\text{L}$, $R = 0.0821$). For a fixed amount of gas:

$$
\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}.
$$

At STP ($273\ \text{K}$, $1\ \text{atm}$) one mole occupies $22.4\ \text{L}$.

## 2. The three special laws

| Fixed | Law | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| $T$ | Boyle (玻意耳) | $pV = $ const |
| $p$ | isobaric (盖–吕萨克 in China) | $\dfrac VT = $ const |
| $V$ | isochoric (查理 in China) | $\dfrac pT = $ const |

Names for the last two differ between textbooks; always read which variable is held fixed.

**Example 1.** $2\ \text{mol}$ of gas at $300\ \text{K}$ occupies $V = \dfrac{nRT}p$; at $1\ \text{atm}$: $49.2\ \text{L}$.

**Example 2.** A gas at $2\ \text{atm}$ occupies $4\ \text{L}$ at $300\ \text{K}$; isothermal expansion to $8\ \text{L}$: $p = 1\ \text{atm}$. Heated at constant volume to $600\ \text{K}$: $p = 4\ \text{atm}$.

**Example 3.** How many molecules in $5\ \text{L}$ at STP? $n = \dfrac5{22.4} = 0.223$; $N = 0.223 \cdot 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 1.34 \times 10^{23}$.

## 3. Pressure of an enclosed gas

- Gas under a movable piston of mass $m$ and area $S$ open to the air: $p = p_0 + \dfrac{mg}S$.
- A mercury column of length $h$ above the gas: $p = p_0 + h$ (in cmHg); if the gas is above the mercury (open end below): $p = p_0 - h$.
- In water at depth $h$: $p = p_0 + \rho gh$; at $20\ \text{m}$, $p \approx 3p_0$.

**Example 4 (bubble).** A bubble rises from $20\ \text{m}$ (about $3\ \text{atm}$) to the surface ($1\ \text{atm}$) at constant temperature: $V_2 = 3V_1$.

**Example 5 (tyre).** A tyre at gauge pressure $2\ \text{atm}$ (absolute $3\ \text{atm}$) and $27\ ^\circ\text{C}$ heated to $57\ ^\circ\text{C}$ at constant volume: $p = 3 \cdot \dfrac{330}{300} = 3.3\ \text{atm}$ absolute. **Always use the absolute pressure** in the gas law.

**Example 6 (mixtures).** Dalton's law: the total pressure of a gas mixture is the sum of the partial pressures.

## Common mistakes

- Using $^\circ\text{C}$ in the gas law.
- Using gauge pressure instead of absolute pressure.
- Mixing litres with $\text{m}^3$ without converting.

## Practice problems

1. A gas at $27\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $1\ \text{atm}$ is heated at constant volume to $127\ ^\circ\text{C}$: new pressure.
2. Amount of gas ($\text{mol}$) in $2\ \text{L}$ at $300\ \text{K}$ and $1.0 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$.
3. A gas at $1\ \text{atm}$, $2\ \text{L}$ is compressed isothermally to $0.5\ \text{L}$: pressure.

**Answers.** (1) $1.33\ \text{atm}$. (2) $\dfrac{10^5 \cdot 2 \times 10^{-3}}{8.31 \cdot 300} = 0.080\ \text{mol}$. (3) $4\ \text{atm}$.
