## What you will learn

- the microscopic meaning of temperature, pressure and internal energy;
- how each state variable changes in the common processes;
- first-law problems for an ideal gas.

## 1. Microscopic meaning

- **Temperature** (kelvin): proportional to the average molecular kinetic energy; two gases at the same temperature have the same average translational $E_k$.
- **Pressure**: determined by the number of molecules per unit volume and their average kinetic energy ($p = \tfrac23n_V\overline{E_k}$).
- **Internal energy** of an ideal gas: $U = \dfrac f2nRT$, depending **only on $T$**.

## 2. What changes in each process

| Process | $T$ | $n_V$ | $p$ | $U$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constant temperature (compression) | const | increases | increases | const |
| Constant volume (heating) | increases | const | increases | increases |
| Constant pressure (heating) | increases | decreases | const | increases |

**Example 1.** A gas is heated from $27$ to $127\ ^\circ\text{C}$ at constant volume: $p_2 = p_1 \cdot \dfrac{400}{300}$; $\overline{E_k}$ rises by $\dfrac43$.

**Example 2.** $2\ \text{mol}$ of monatomic ideal gas heated at constant volume from $300$ to $350\ \text{K}$: $\Delta U = Q = \tfrac32 \cdot 2 \cdot 8.31 \cdot 50 = 1.25\ \text{kJ}$.

**Example 3.** A fixed mass of ideal gas expands at constant temperature doing $200\ \text{J}$ of work on its surroundings: $\Delta U = 0$, so it must absorb $Q = 200\ \text{J}$.

**Example 4.** An insulated gas is compressed quickly with $500\ \text{J}$ of work: $Q = 0$, $\Delta U = +500\ \text{J}$, $T$ rises.

## 3. Explaining qualitatively

*Why does the pressure rise when a closed container is heated?* Molecules move faster, hitting the walls harder and more often. *Why does a bicycle pump get warm?* Rapid compression is adiabatic: work done on the gas raises its internal energy.

## 4. Using the first law with the ideal gas law

For $\Delta U$ use $nC_V\Delta T$ for any process; for $W$ use the $p$–$V$ relation of the process; then $Q = \Delta U + W_{\text{by}}$.

**Example 5.** $1\ \text{mol}$ of monatomic gas expands isobarically at $1\ \text{atm}$ from $22.4$ to $44.8\ \text{L}$: $W = p\Delta V = 2270\ \text{J}$; $T$ doubles from $273$ to $546\ \text{K}$; $\Delta U = \tfrac32 \cdot 8.31 \cdot 273 = 3403\ \text{J}$; $Q = 5673\ \text{J}$.

## Common mistakes

- Treating $U$ as depending on volume for an ideal gas.
- Using the isothermal formula for work in an adiabatic process.
- Leaving temperatures in Celsius.

## Practice problems

1. $2\ \text{mol}$ of monatomic gas heated by $100\ \text{K}$ at constant volume: $Q$.
2. An ideal gas absorbs $600\ \text{J}$ and its internal energy rises by $400\ \text{J}$: work done by the gas.

**Answers.** (1) $2.5\ \text{kJ}$. (2) $200\ \text{J}$.
