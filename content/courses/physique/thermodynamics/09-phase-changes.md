## What you will learn

- latent heat of fusion and vaporisation;
- heating curves, plateaus and slopes;
- mixing problems with phase change.

## 1. Latent heat

During a phase change the temperature stays constant while heat is absorbed or released:

$$
Q = mL, \qquad L_f(\text{water}) = 334\ \text{kJ/kg}, \quad L_v(\text{water}) = 2260\ \text{kJ/kg}.
$$

$L_f$ is the latent heat of fusion (melting/freezing); $L_v$ of vaporisation (boiling/condensing). The energy goes into overcoming intermolecular forces (potential energy), not kinetic energy.

## 2. Heating curve

A graph of temperature against heat added: **sloping** segments (single phase, $Q = mc\Delta T$, with a different $c$ for each phase: ice $2.1$, water $4.18$, steam $2.0\ \text{J/g K}$) and **plateaus** at the melting and boiling points ($Q = mL$). The slope is steeper for a smaller $c$, and the longer plateau is for vaporisation since $L_v > L_f$.

**Example 1.** Energy to change $500\ \text{g}$ of ice at $-10\ ^\circ\text{C}$ into steam at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$:

- warm ice: $500 \cdot 2.1 \cdot 10 = 10.5\ \text{kJ}$;
- melt: $500 \cdot 334 = 167\ \text{kJ}$;
- warm water: $500 \cdot 4.18 \cdot 100 = 209\ \text{kJ}$;
- boil: $500 \cdot 2260 = 1130\ \text{kJ}$.

Total $\approx 1517\ \text{kJ}$ (use $c_{\text{ice}}$ for the first step, not $c_{\text{water}}$).

## 3. Mixing with phase change

Compare the heat available from cooling the hot water to $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ with the heat needed to melt all the ice. If it is enough, all the ice melts and the final temperature is above $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$; if not, the final temperature is $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ with some ice left.

**Example 2.** $50\ \text{g}$ of ice at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ added to $200\ \text{g}$ of water at $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$: heat available $200 \cdot 4.18 \cdot 40 = 33.4\ \text{kJ}$; to melt the ice $50 \cdot 334 = 16.7\ \text{kJ}$: all melts. Then $16.7 + 50 \cdot 4.18T = 200 \cdot 4.18(40 - T) \Rightarrow T = 16\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

**Example 3.** $100\ \text{g}$ of ice at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ into $300\ \text{g}$ of water at $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$: available $62.7\ \text{kJ}$, needed $33.4\ \text{kJ}$; final $T = \dfrac{62.7 - 33.4}{4.18 \cdot 400} \cdot 1000 \approx 17.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## 4. Evaporation and boiling

**Evaporation** occurs at any temperature at the surface and cools the liquid (sweat, wet towels); **boiling** occurs throughout the liquid when the vapour pressure equals the external pressure, so a higher pressure raises the boiling point (pressure cooker) and a lower pressure lowers it (mountains). **Sublimation**: solid directly to gas (dry ice).

## Common mistakes

- Using $c_{\text{water}}$ for ice or steam.
- Using $mc\Delta T$ during a phase change.
- Assuming all the ice melts without checking.

## Practice problems

1. Heat to melt $200\ \text{g}$ of ice at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
2. Heat to turn $50\ \text{g}$ of water at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ into steam.
3. Energy to turn $1\ \text{kg}$ of ice at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ into water at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

**Answers.** (1) $66.8\ \text{kJ}$. (2) $113\ \text{kJ}$. (3) $334 + 83.6 = 417.6\ \text{kJ}$.
