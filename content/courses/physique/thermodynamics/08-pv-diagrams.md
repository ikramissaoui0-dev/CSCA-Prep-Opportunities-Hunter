## What you will learn

- how to read the processes on a $p$–$V$ diagram;
- work as an area; the net work of a cycle;
- comparing temperatures of states.

## 1. Reading a $p$–$V$ diagram

- A **horizontal** line is isobaric ($p$ constant); a **vertical** line is isochoric ($V$ constant).
- A **hyperbola** ($pV = $ const) is isothermal; an **adiabat** is steeper than the isotherm through the same point.
- Points farther from the axes (larger $pV$) have a higher temperature: $T \propto pV$.

## 2. Work

The work done by the gas is the **area under the curve**: $W = \int p\,dV$. It is positive when the gas expands (moving to the right), negative when compressed (moving left). For constant pressure: $W = p\Delta V$. For a straight line from $(V_1, p_1)$ to $(V_2, p_2)$: $W = \dfrac{p_1 + p_2}2(V_2 - V_1)$.

**Example 1.** A gas expands isobarically at $200\ \text{kPa}$ from $2\ \text{L}$ to $5\ \text{L}$: $W = 200\,000 \cdot 3 \times 10^{-3} = 600\ \text{J}$.

**Example 2.** A cycle traces a rectangle $2\ \text{atm}$ high and $4\ \text{L}$ wide: $W_{\text{net}} = 8\ \text{L atm} = 810\ \text{J}$; positive if clockwise.

## 3. Cycles

For a closed loop the net work is the enclosed area: **clockwise** loops have positive net work (heat engines); **anticlockwise** loops have negative net work (refrigerators). Over a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, so $W_{\text{net}} = Q_{\text{net}} = Q_H - Q_C$.

**Example 3.** A gas goes from $A(1\ \text{L}, 4\ \text{atm})$ isobarically to $B(3\ \text{L}, 4\ \text{atm})$, then isochorically to $C(3\ \text{L}, 2\ \text{atm})$. $p_AV_A = 4$, $p_BV_B = 12$, $p_CV_C = 6$: $T_B = 3T_A$ and $T_C = 1.5T_A$. $W_{AB} = 4 \cdot 2 = 8\ \text{L atm}$, $W_{BC} = 0$.

**Example 4.** On a $p$–$V$ graph a gas goes from $A(2 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}, 1\ \text{L})$ to $B(10^5\ \text{Pa}, 4\ \text{L})$ along a straight line: work $= \dfrac{3 \times 10^5}2 \cdot 3 \times 10^{-3} = 450\ \text{J}$; $p_AV_A = 200$ and $p_BV_B = 400$ show the temperature has doubled.

## 4. Identify the process

If $pV$ is constant: isothermal ($\Delta U = 0$). If the line is vertical: no work. If the graph is a straight line through the origin on a $p$–$T$ diagram: isochoric.

## Common mistakes

- Using the area from the wrong axis (pressure vs volume).
- Forgetting that work is negative for compression.
- Confusing the direction of a cycle with the sign of net work.

## Practice problems

1. A gas expands from $1\ \text{L}$ to $4\ \text{L}$ at constant $3 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$: $W$.
2. A clockwise rectangle $2 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$ high, $3\ \text{L}$ wide: net work and $Q_{\text{net}}$.

**Answers.** (1) $900\ \text{J}$. (2) $600\ \text{J}$ and $Q_{\text{net}} = 600\ \text{J}$ since $\Delta U = 0$.
