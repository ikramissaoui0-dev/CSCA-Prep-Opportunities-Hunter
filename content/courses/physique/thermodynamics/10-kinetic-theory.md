## What you will learn

- the assumptions of kinetic theory;
- pressure, temperature and the average kinetic energy;
- the rms speed and internal energy.

## 1. Assumptions

An ideal gas consists of a very large number of identical molecules that are point-like (negligible size), in continuous random motion, colliding elastically with each other and the walls, with no intermolecular forces except during collisions.

## 2. Results

$$
p = \frac13\rho v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac23n_V\overline{E_k}, \qquad \overline{E_k} = \frac32k_BT, \qquad v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3k_BT}m} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}M}.
$$

($n_V$ = number of molecules per unit volume; $\rho$ the density.) **Temperature is a measure of the average translational kinetic energy per molecule, independent of the gas.** At the same temperature lighter molecules move faster ($v_{\text{rms}} \propto M^{-1/2}$).

**Example 1.** Average translational kinetic energy at $300\ \text{K}$: $\tfrac32 \cdot 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \cdot 300 = 6.21 \times 10^{-21}\ \text{J}$.

**Example 2.** $v_{\text{rms}}$ of oxygen ($M = 0.032\ \text{kg/mol}$) at $300\ \text{K}$: $\sqrt{\dfrac{3 \cdot 8.31 \cdot 300}{0.032}} \approx 484\ \text{m/s}$. (Use $M$ in $\text{kg/mol}$.)

**Example 3.** Quadrupling $T$ doubles $v_{\text{rms}}$.

## 3. Pressure explained

Pressure comes from molecular impacts on the walls: raising the temperature gives faster, harder, more frequent impacts; increasing the density gives more impacts per second. Decreasing volume at constant temperature increases the impact rate (Boyle's law).

## 4. Internal energy

For an ideal gas $U$ is purely kinetic: $U = N\overline{E_k} = \dfrac f2nRT$ ($f = 3$ for monatomic). Doubling the absolute temperature doubles $U$. $U$ does not depend on volume.

**Example 4.** $2\ \text{mol}$ of monatomic gas at $300\ \text{K}$: $U = \tfrac32 \cdot 2 \cdot 8.31 \cdot 300 = 7.5\ \text{kJ}$; $24.9\ \text{kJ}$ at $1000\ \text{K}$.

**Example 5 (mixing).** In a mixture, lighter and heavier molecules at the same $T$ have the same $\overline{E_k}$ but different speeds.

## Common mistakes

- Using $g/\text{mol}$ instead of $\text{kg/mol}$ for $M$ in $v_{\text{rms}}$.
- Saying all molecules have the same speed.
- Using $^\circ\text{C}$ in $\overline{E_k} = \tfrac32kT$.

## Practice problems

1. $v_{\text{rms}}$ ratio of $\text{H}_2$ ($M = 2$) to $\text{O}_2$ ($M = 32$) at the same $T$.
2. By what factor does $\overline{E_k}$ change when $T$ goes from $27$ to $327\ ^\circ\text{C}$?

**Answers.** (1) $\sqrt{16} = 4$. (2) $\dfrac{600}{300} = 2$.
