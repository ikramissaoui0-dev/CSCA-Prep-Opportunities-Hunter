## What you will learn

- linear, area and volume expansion;
- bimetallic strips and thermal stress;
- the anomalous expansion of water.

## 1. Formulas

For a small temperature change $\Delta T$:

$$
\Delta L = \alpha L_0\Delta T, \qquad \Delta A = 2\alpha A_0\Delta T, \qquad \Delta V = \beta V_0\Delta T \approx 3\alpha V_0\Delta T.
$$

$\alpha$ is the coefficient of linear expansion (unit $^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ or $\text{K}^{-1}$); $\beta \approx 3\alpha$ for solids. Liquids expand more than solids (larger $\beta$). A hole in a plate expands like the plate material (the hole behaves as if filled with the same metal).

**Example 1.** A $2\ \text{m}$ steel rod ($\alpha = 12 \times 10^{-6}\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$) heated by $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $\Delta L = 12 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 2 \cdot 100 = 2.4\ \text{mm}$.

**Example 2.** A $100\ \text{m}$ steel bridge span with a $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$ swing: $\Delta L = 12 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 100 \cdot 40 = 4.8\ \text{cm}$ — hence expansion joints.

**Example 3.** An aluminium block of volume $0.001\ \text{m}^3$ ($\beta = 69 \times 10^{-6}$) heated by $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $\Delta V = 69 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 0.001 \cdot 100 = 6.9 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^3$.

## 2. Applications and consequences

- **Bimetallic strip**: two metals bonded together expand by different amounts, so the strip bends toward the metal with the smaller $\alpha$ when heated (and the other way when cooled). Used in thermostats and fire alarms.
- **Thermal stress**: if expansion is prevented, large forces arise (railway tracks buckle; hot glass cracks when cold water is poured on it).
- **Gaps** in bridges and railways; **rivets** shrunk by cooling; **glass thermometers** use the expansion of mercury or alcohol.
- Pyrex glass has a small $\alpha$, so it resists thermal shock.

## 3. Water

Water is anomalous: between $0$ and $4\ ^\circ\text{C}$ it contracts on heating, so its density is **maximum at $4\ ^\circ\text{C}$**. Ponds freeze from the top down, and the water at the bottom stays at $4\ ^\circ\text{C}$, protecting aquatic life. Ice is less dense than liquid water and floats.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting that the area coefficient is $2\alpha$ and the volume coefficient $3\alpha$.
- Using a $\Delta T$ in $\text{K}$ with the wrong sign.
- Thinking a hole shrinks when the plate is heated.

## Practice problems

1. A $10\ \text{m}$ aluminium rod ($\alpha = 23 \times 10^{-6}$) is heated by $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$: elongation.
2. Which side does a brass–iron strip bend toward when heated? ($\alpha_{\text{brass}} > \alpha_{\text{iron}}$.)

**Answers.** (1) $11.5\ \text{mm}$. (2) Toward the iron side (the iron is on the inside of the curve, the brass on the outside).
