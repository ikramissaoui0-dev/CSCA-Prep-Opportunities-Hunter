## What you will learn

- specific heat capacity and heat capacity;
- the principle of calorimetry (heat lost = heat gained);
- mixing problems and electrical heating.

## 1. Specific heat capacity

$$
Q = mc\,\Delta T = C\,\Delta T, \qquad C = mc.
$$

$c$ is the **specific heat capacity** (heat to raise $1\ \text{kg}$ by $1\ \text{K}$); water has $c = 4186\ \text{J/(kg K)}$ ($4.18\ \text{J/g}\,^\circ\text{C}$), unusually high, which makes water a good coolant and moderates climates. $C = mc$ is the heat capacity of the object.

**Example 1.** To warm $2\ \text{kg}$ of water from $20$ to $70\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $Q = 2 \cdot 4186 \cdot 50 = 418.6\ \text{kJ}$.

## 2. Principle of calorimetry

In an isolated system, **heat lost by hot bodies = heat gained by cold bodies**, so $\sum Q = 0$ and the final temperature $T_f$ is common.

**Example 2.** $200\ \text{g}$ of water at $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$ mixed with $300\ \text{g}$ at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $200(80 - T) = 300(T - 20) \Rightarrow T = 44\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

**Example 3.** $200\ \text{g}$ of iron ($c = 450$) at $300\ ^\circ\text{C}$ dropped into $500\ \text{g}$ of water ($c = 4186$) at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $0.2 \cdot 450(300 - T) = 0.5 \cdot 4186(T - 20) \Rightarrow T \approx 31.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

**Example 4 (unknown specific heat).** A $100\ \text{g}$ metal at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ is placed in $200\ \text{g}$ of water at $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$; the final temperature is $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $0.1c(75) = 0.2 \cdot 4186 \cdot 5 \Rightarrow c \approx 558\ \text{J/kg K}$.

## 3. Electrical heating

A heater of power $P$ (or $VI$) delivers $E = Pt = VIt$. A $500\ \text{W}$ heater for $200\ \text{s}$ warms $0.5\ \text{kg}$ of water by $\dfrac{100\,000}{0.5 \cdot 4186} \approx 48\ \text{K}$ with no losses; real results are lower because of heat lost to the surroundings and to the container.

**Calorimeter heat capacity.** A calorimeter cup with heat capacity $C_c$ takes up heat too: $\sum Q = 0$ includes $C_c\Delta T$.

## 4. Practical sources of error

Heat lost to the air (use insulation and a lid), heat absorbed by the container and the thermometer, incomplete mixing, thermometer calibration. These make the measured specific heat of a metal too high or the water temperature rise too low.

## Common mistakes

- Using Celsius vs Kelvin incorrectly (differences are the same).
- Writing $T_f - T_i$ with inconsistent signs; instead use positive heats: $Q_{\text{lost}} = Q_{\text{gained}}$.
- Mixing grams and kilograms with $c$ in $\text{J/(kg K)}$.

## Practice problems

1. Heat to warm $0.5\ \text{kg}$ of oil ($c = 2000$) by $30\ \text{K}$.
2. $100\ \text{g}$ of water at $90\ ^\circ\text{C}$ meets $50\ \text{g}$ at $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$: final $T$.
3. How long does a $2\ \text{kW}$ kettle take to heat $1\ \text{kg}$ of water by $50\ \text{K}$ (no losses)?

**Answers.** (1) $30\ \text{kJ}$. (2) $70\ ^\circ\text{C}$. (3) $\dfrac{209\,300}{2000} \approx 105\ \text{s}$.
