## What you will learn

- how to pick the right method for each mechanics question;
- a checklist that prevents the common errors.

## 1. Choosing the method

| Question asks for | Use |
|---|---|
| $v$, $x$, $t$ with constant $a$ | kinematic equations |
| force, acceleration | Newton's second law + free-body diagram |
| speed, height, distance (no time) | energy / work–energy theorem |
| collision, explosion, recoil | momentum conservation |
| circular motion | $F_c = \dfrac{mv^2}r$, naming the real force |
| orbit, satellites | $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = \dfrac{mv^2}r$ |

Aim for about $75$ seconds per question.

## 2. Checklist

1. **Sign convention first**; keep it for every vector.
2. **Free-body diagram**: all forces on the body, each named.
3. **Limiting cases**: $\theta = 0$ should give no acceleration; $\theta = 90^\circ$ free fall.
4. **Units**: convert (km/h to m/s, cm to m, rev/min to rad/s).
5. **Ratios**: for "twice the radius" type questions use ratios rather than numbers ($v \propto r^{-1/2}$, $T \propto r^{3/2}$).
6. **Whole system, then one part** (整体法与隔离法) for connected bodies.
7. **Estimation**: if $g = 10$ is allowed use it; check the order of magnitude.
8. **Wording**: "smooth" means no friction; "light" means massless; "just" or "exactly" means a limiting condition (e.g. $N = 0$, tension $= 0$).

## 3. Typical worked question

A block slides down a rough incline of angle $\theta$ and friction coefficient $\mu$: $a = g(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta)$; if the block moves at constant speed, $\mu = \tan\theta$.

A car braking from $10\ \text{m/s}$ stops in $5\ \text{m}$: $a = \dfrac{v^2}{2s} = 10\ \text{m/s}^2$, so $\mu = \dfrac ag = 1$.

Block $A$ ($2\ \text{kg}$) on a smooth table tied over a pulley to $B$ ($3\ \text{kg}$) hanging: $a = \dfrac{3g}5 = 6\ \text{m/s}^2$, $T = 2a = 12\ \text{N}$.

## 4. Traps

- Forgetting that a braking object stops.
- Centripetal force as an extra force.
- Using energy conservation in an inelastic collision.
- Using $r$ as altitude in gravitation.

## Quick self-test

1. A $0.1\ \text{kg}$ ball changes velocity from $10$ to $-8\ \text{m/s}$ in $0.01\ \text{s}$: force?
2. Speed at the bottom of a smooth $5\ \text{m}$ slide.
3. A satellite in a circular orbit at $r = 2R$: $v$ in terms of $\sqrt{gR}$.

**Answers.** (1) $180\ \text{N}$. (2) $10\ \text{m/s}$. (3) $\dfrac{\sqrt{gR}}{\sqrt2}$.
