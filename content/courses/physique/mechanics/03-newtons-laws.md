## What you will learn

- the three laws and the free-body diagram method;
- connected bodies, pulleys and the system method;
- equilibrium and apparent weight.

## 1. The three laws

1. **Inertia**: a body keeps its state of rest or uniform motion unless a net force acts on it.
2. **$\sum\vec F = m\vec a$** ($1\ \text{N} = 1\ \text{kg m/s}^2$).
3. **Action–reaction**: if $A$ exerts a force on $B$, $B$ exerts an equal and opposite force on $A$. The two forces act on *different* bodies, so they never cancel in one free-body diagram.

## 2. Method

1. Draw the body and **all** forces on it. 2. Choose axes. 3. Write $\sum F = ma$ per axis. 4. Solve and check units.

Common forces: weight $W = mg$; normal force $N$ (perpendicular to the surface; not always $mg$); tension $T$ (the same throughout an ideal rope/pulley); spring force $F = kx$.

**Example 1.** A $5\ \text{kg}$ box is pushed by $30\ \text{N}$ on a frictionless floor: $a = 6\ \text{m/s}^2$.

**Example 2 (incline).** A $2\ \text{kg}$ block on a frictionless $30^\circ$ incline: $a = g\sin30^\circ = 4.9\ \text{m/s}^2$; $N = mg\cos\theta$.

**Example 3 (elevator).** A $60\ \text{kg}$ person in an elevator accelerating up at $2\ \text{m/s}^2$ ($g = 10$): $N = m(g + a) = 720\ \text{N}$ (apparent weight); accelerating down, $N = 480\ \text{N}$; in free fall $N = 0$.

## 3. Connected bodies

Bodies connected by a taut rope share the acceleration. **Whole-system method** (整体法): treat all as one body to find $a$; **isolated-body method** (隔离法): isolate one body to find a tension.

**Example 4.** $3\ \text{kg}$ and $5\ \text{kg}$ blocks joined by a rope on a frictionless floor, with a $40\ \text{N}$ pull on the $5\ \text{kg}$ block: $a = \dfrac{40}8 = 5\ \text{m/s}^2$; $T = 3 \cdot 5 = 15\ \text{N}$.

**Example 5 (Atwood machine).** Masses $m_1 > m_2$:

$$
a = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)g}{m_1 + m_2}, \qquad T = \frac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1 + m_2}.
$$

With $3\ \text{kg}$ and $1\ \text{kg}$: $a = 5\ \text{m/s}^2$, $T = 15\ \text{N}$.

**Example 6.** A $3\ \text{kg}$ block on a smooth table is pulled by a hanging $1\ \text{kg}$ mass: $a = \dfrac{1 \cdot 10}4 = 2.5\ \text{m/s}^2$, $T = 7.5\ \text{N}$.

## 4. Equilibrium

$\sum\vec F = 0$. For three concurrent forces, use components. A $10\ \text{kg}$ lamp on two ropes each at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal: $2T\sin60^\circ = 100 \Rightarrow T \approx 57.7\ \text{N}$.

**Springs and strings.** A spring force cannot change instantly when a support is removed, whereas the tension in a rigid string can. At equilibrium $kx = mg$.

## Common mistakes

- Adding a "centrifugal" or "force of motion" to the diagram.
- Assuming $N = mg$ always.
- Treating action and reaction as cancelling.

## Practice problems

1. A $4\ \text{kg}$ block is pulled by $20\ \text{N}$ on a smooth surface; $a$?
2. Find the acceleration down a smooth $37^\circ$ slope ($\sin37^\circ = 0.6$, $g = 10$).
3. A $70\ \text{kg}$ person in an elevator accelerating down at $1\ \text{m/s}^2$: scale reading?

**Answers.** (1) $5\ \text{m/s}^2$. (2) $6\ \text{m/s}^2$. (3) $70 \cdot 9 = 630\ \text{N}$.
