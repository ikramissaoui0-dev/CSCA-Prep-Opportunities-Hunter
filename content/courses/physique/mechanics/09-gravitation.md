## What you will learn

- Newton's law of gravitation and surface gravity;
- Kepler's laws;
- satellites, orbital speed, period and escape velocity.

## 1. Kepler's laws

1. Planets move in **ellipses** with the Sun at one focus.
2. The line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps **equal areas in equal times** (a planet moves fastest at perihelion).
3. $\dfrac{a^3}{T^2} = k$ (or $\dfrac{T^2}{r^3}$ is constant) for all planets orbiting the same star.

## 2. Newton's law of gravitation

$$
F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}, \qquad G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2.
$$

Attractive, along the line of centres, inversely proportional to the square of the distance. Surface gravity: $g = \dfrac{GM}R^2$, hence $GM = gR^2$ (the "golden formula"); at height $h$: $g_h = \dfrac{gR^2}{(R + h)^2}$.

**Example 1.** A planet with twice Earth's mass and twice its radius has $g' = \dfrac24g = 0.5g$.

## 3. Satellites

For a circular orbit of radius $r$ about mass $M$, gravity provides the centripetal force:

$$
\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}r = m\omega^2r = m\frac{4\pi^2}{T^2}r = ma.
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}r}, \qquad \omega = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}, \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}, \qquad a = \frac{GM}{r^2}.
$$

**Higher orbit: slower and longer period.** Orbital quantities are independent of the satellite's mass.

**Example 2.** $P$ at $r = R$ and $Q$ at $r = 4R$: $v_Q = \tfrac12v_P$, $T_Q = 8T_P$, $a_Q = \tfrac1{16}a_P$.

**Example 3.** First cosmic velocity (orbit just above the surface): $v_1 = \sqrt{gR} \approx 7.9\ \text{km/s}$ (with $g = 10$, $R = 6.4 \times 10^6\ \text{m}$: $8\ \text{km/s}$). Escape velocity $v_2 = \sqrt2v_1 \approx 11.2\ \text{km/s}$.

**Example 4 (geostationary).** $T = 24\ \text{h}$, above the equator, at about $3.6 \times 10^4\ \text{km}$ above the surface.

**Weightlessness.** Objects in orbit are in free fall with the satellite; gravity is still acting (about $90\%$ of the surface value at $400\ \text{km}$).

**Changing orbit.** Firing thrusters to speed up moves the satellite onto a larger orbit, where its speed is *lower*.

## 4. Gravitational potential energy (extension)

$E_p = -\dfrac{GMm}r$ (zero at infinity); the total energy of a circular orbit is $-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$. Escape requires $E_k \ge \dfrac{GMm}R$.

## Common mistakes

- Using the height $h$ instead of the distance $r = R + h$ from the centre.
- Believing astronauts are beyond gravity.
- Thinking a heavier satellite orbits faster.

## Practice problems

1. Orbital speed at $r = 4R$ in terms of $v_1$.
2. $g$ at height $R$ above Earth's surface ($g_0 = 10$).
3. Period of a satellite in a circular orbit with $r = 4R$ compared with $T_1$ at $r = R$.

**Answers.** (1) $\dfrac{v_1}2$. (2) $\dfrac{g_0}4 = 2.5\ \text{m/s}^2$. (3) $8T_1$.
