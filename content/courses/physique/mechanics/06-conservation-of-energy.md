## What you will learn

- mechanical energy and when it is conserved;
- energy with friction and the heat produced;
- springs, vertical circles and multi-stage problems.

## 1. Mechanical energy

$E = E_k + E_p$. If only gravity and spring forces do work (a **conservative system**):

$$
E_{k1} + E_{p1} = E_{k2} + E_{p2}.
$$

With friction or other non-conservative forces (the work–energy form):

$$
W_{\text{other}} = E_2 - E_1, \qquad \text{heat produced } Q = f\cdot s_{\text{rel}}.
$$

**Example 1.** A ball released from rest at height $h$ on a smooth track: $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ at the bottom, independent of the shape. From $5\ \text{m}$: $v = 10\ \text{m/s}$.

**Example 2.** A $2\ \text{kg}$ block released from rest at $5\ \text{m}$ reaches the bottom of a rough slope at $8\ \text{m/s}$ ($g = 10$): energy lost $= 100 - 64 = 36\ \text{J}$ (to friction).

**Example 3.** A $2\ \text{kg}$ block slides down a smooth ramp $5\ \text{m}$ high onto a rough floor ($\mu = 0.5$): it stops after $s = \dfrac h\mu = 10\ \text{m}$.

## 2. Springs

For a block hitting a spring (no friction): $\tfrac12mv^2 = \tfrac12kx^2$ at maximum compression. With friction on the way: subtract the work done by friction first.

**Example 4.** A $2\ \text{kg}$ block from rest at $1.8\ \text{m}$, a rough flat section of $3\ \text{m}$ ($\mu = 0.2$), then a spring $k = 200\ \text{N/m}$. Energy at the start $36\ \text{J}$; friction takes $12\ \text{J}$; so $\tfrac12kx^2 = 24 \Rightarrow x \approx 0.49\ \text{m}$.

## 3. Vertical circle

At the top of a circle of radius $r$, a mass on a string needs $T + mg = \dfrac{mv^2}r$, so the minimum speed is $v_{\text{top}} = \sqrt{gr}$. Energy conservation gives $\tfrac12mv_{\text{bottom}}^2 = \tfrac12mv_{\text{top}}^2 + mg(2r)$, so $v_{\text{bottom}} = \sqrt{5gr}$ for the minimal case. On a track (not a string), the condition at the top is the same ($N \ge 0$), and a ball released from rest at height $2.5r$ just completes the loop.

**Example 5.** A ball released from rest at height $h$ above the bottom of a loop of radius $R$ completes it if $h \ge 2.5R$.

## 4. Choosing methods

| Question | Method |
|---|---|
| speed or height, no time asked | energy |
| time or acceleration | Newton + kinematics |
| collision | momentum first |
| friction on a curved path | work–energy: $mgh - fs = \tfrac12mv^2$ |

**Example 6 (multi-stage).** A box slides down a smooth $1\ \text{m}$-high ramp, then on a rough floor ($\mu = 0.5$) with a $1\ \text{m}$ flat section, then up a smooth ramp: $mgh_{\text{final}} = mg \cdot 1 - \mu mg \cdot 1 = 0.5mg$: it rises to $0.5\ \text{m}$.

**Heat**: the energy lost to friction becomes heat, $Q = fs_{\text{relative}}$; the total energy is conserved.

## Common mistakes

- Using conservation of mechanical energy with friction or in an inelastic collision.
- Forgetting to include all forms (kinetic, gravitational, elastic).
- Choosing a different reference level for initial and final states.

## Practice problems

1. A ball dropped from $20\ \text{m}$; speed at $5\ \text{m}$ above the ground ($g = 10$).
2. A pendulum of length $1\ \text{m}$ released from the horizontal: speed at the bottom.
3. A $0.5\ \text{kg}$ ball falls from $20\ \text{m}$ and rebounds to $5\ \text{m}$: energy lost.

**Answers.** (1) $v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 10 \cdot 15} \approx 17.3\ \text{m/s}$. (2) $\sqrt{2 \cdot 10 \cdot 1} = 4.5\ \text{m/s}$. (3) $0.5 \cdot 10 \cdot 15 = 75\ \text{J}$.
