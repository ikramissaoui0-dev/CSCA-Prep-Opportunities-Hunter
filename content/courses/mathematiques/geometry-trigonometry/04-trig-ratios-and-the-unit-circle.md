## What you will learn

- the trigonometric ratios in a right triangle (SOH-CAH-TOA);
- the unit-circle definition and the special angles;
- signs, reference angles and radians.

## 1. Right-triangle ratios

In a right triangle with an acute angle $\theta$:

$$
\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}.
$$

Reciprocals: $\csc\theta = \dfrac1{\sin\theta}$, $\sec\theta = \dfrac1{\cos\theta}$, $\cot\theta = \dfrac1{\tan\theta}$. Complementary angles: $\sin\theta = \cos(90^\circ - \theta)$.

**Special triangles.** The $45^\circ$–$45^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has sides $1 : 1 : \sqrt2$; the $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has sides $1 : \sqrt3 : 2$.

## 2. The unit circle

For any angle $\theta$ the point on the unit circle is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. For a point $P(x, y)$ at distance $r$ from the origin: $\sin = \dfrac yr$, $\cos = \dfrac xr$, $\tan = \dfrac yx$.

| $\theta$ | $0^\circ$ | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin$ | $0$ | $\dfrac12$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt2}2$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt3}2$ | $1$ |
| $\cos$ | $1$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt3}2$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt2}2$ | $\dfrac12$ | $0$ |
| $\tan$ | $0$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt3}3$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3$ | undefined |

**Signs (ASTC).** Quadrant I: all positive; II: only $\sin$; III: only $\tan$; IV: only $\cos$.

**Reference angle method**: find the acute angle the terminal side makes with the $x$-axis, evaluate it, and attach the sign.

**Example 1.** $\sin150^\circ$: reference angle $30^\circ$, sine positive in QII: $\dfrac12$.

**Example 2.** $\tan315^\circ$: reference angle $45^\circ$, tangent negative in QIV: $-1$.

**Example 3.** $\cos240^\circ = -\cos60^\circ = -\dfrac12$.

## 3. Radians

$180^\circ = \pi\ \text{rad}$. $240^\circ = \dfrac{4\pi}3$; $\dfrac{5\pi}6 = 150^\circ$. The most common angles: $\dfrac\pi6, \dfrac\pi4, \dfrac\pi3, \dfrac\pi2$ for $30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$.

## 4. Pythagorean relation and application

$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ gives the other ratio from one ratio and the quadrant. **Example 4.** If $\sin\alpha = \dfrac35$ and $\alpha$ is in quadrant II: $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac45$, $\tan\alpha = -\dfrac34$.

**Heights and distances.** A $30^\circ$ angle of elevation to the top of a tower from $50\ \text{m}$ away: height $= 50\tan30^\circ \approx 28.9\ \text{m}$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the sign in quadrants II–IV.
- Using degrees in a radian formula.
- Writing $\tan90^\circ$ as a number.

## Practice problems

1. Evaluate $\sin210^\circ$ and $\cos(-60^\circ)$.
2. $\cos\theta = -\dfrac5{13}$ with $\theta$ in QIII. Find $\sin\theta$.
3. Convert $\dfrac{7\pi}4$ to degrees.

**Answers.** (1) $-\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac12$. (2) $-\dfrac{12}{13}$. (3) $315^\circ$.
