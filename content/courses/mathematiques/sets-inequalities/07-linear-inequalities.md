## What you will learn

- the basic properties of inequalities, including the sign flip;
- how to solve linear and compound (double) inequalities;
- how to show the answer on a number line and in interval notation.

## 1. Basic properties

For real numbers $a, b, c$:

- $a < b \implies a + c < b + c$ (adding the same number keeps the direction).
- $a < b$ and $c > 0 \implies ac < bc$.
- $a < b$ and $c < 0 \implies ac > bc$ — **multiplying or dividing by a negative number flips the sign.**
- $0 < a < b \implies \dfrac1a > \dfrac1b$.
- $a < b$ and $c < d \implies a + c < b + d$, but you may **not** subtract inequalities.
- Transitivity: $a < b$ and $b < c \implies a < c$.

## 2. Solving a linear inequality

Treat it like an equation, with one critical rule: **flip the inequality sign when you multiply or divide by a negative number.** Steps: expand, collect $x$-terms on one side, constants on the other, divide by the coefficient of $x$.

**Example 1.** $4x - 7 \le 2x + 3 \Rightarrow 2x \le 10 \Rightarrow x \le 5$. Answer $(-\infty, 5]$.

**Example 2.** $-2(x + 1) > 6 \Rightarrow -2x - 2 > 6 \Rightarrow -2x > 8 \Rightarrow x < -4$ (flipped). Answer $(-\infty, -4)$.

**Example 3.** $3 - 2x \ge 7 \Rightarrow -2x \ge 4 \Rightarrow x \le -2$.

**Fractions.** Multiply by the (positive) common denominator first: $\dfrac{x - 3}{2} - 1 \le \dfrac x3$; multiply by $6$: $3(x - 3) - 6 \le 2x \Rightarrow x \le 15$.

## 3. Compound inequalities

- **"And"** (both must hold): intersection of the solution sets. $x + 3 > 5$ and $2x - 1 < 9$ gives $x > 2$ and $x < 5$: $(2, 5)$.
- **"Or"** (at least one): union. $x < -1$ or $x > 4$ is $(-\infty, -1) \cup (4, +\infty)$.
- **Double inequality** $a < f(x) \le b$: work on all three parts at once.

**Example 4.** $1 < 3x - 2 \le 10$: add 2 to all parts: $3 < 3x \le 12$; divide by 3: $1 < x \le 4$. Answer $(1, 4]$.

## 4. Number line and word problems

On a number line, an **open circle** marks an excluded endpoint and a **filled circle** an included one; this matches round and square brackets.

Translate "at least" to $\ge$, "at most" to $\le$, "more than" to $>$, "no more than" to $\le$. Define a variable, write the inequality, solve, then check that the answer makes sense (for example a number of people must be a non-negative integer).

**Example 5.** A plan costs 20 plus 0.5 per minute. For the bill to stay below 50: $20 + 0.5m < 50 \Rightarrow m < 60$.

## Common mistakes

- Not flipping the sign when dividing by a negative number.
- Using the wrong bracket type for a strict inequality.
- Writing a chain such as $5 < x < 2$, which describes no number.

## Exam tip

Write a reminder: "NEGATIVE ✕ or ÷ $\Rightarrow$ FLIP" and check one value of your answer in the original inequality.

## Practice problems

1. Solve $5 - 3x < 14$.
2. Solve $-3 \le 2x + 1 < 7$.
3. Solve $x - 2 > 1$ or $2x < -4$.

**Answers.** (1) $-3x < 9 \Rightarrow x > -3$. (2) $-4 \le 2x < 6 \Rightarrow -2 \le x < 3$. (3) $x > 3$ or $x < -2$: $(-\infty, -2) \cup (3, +\infty)$.
