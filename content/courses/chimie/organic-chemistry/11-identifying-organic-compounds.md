## What you will learn

- chemical tests for functional groups;
- distinguishing isomers;
- instrumental data: mass spectra and infrared.

## 1. Chemical tests

| Test | Observation | Indicates |
|---|---|---|
| Bromine water | orange $\to$ colourless | $\text{C=C}$ (alkene) |
| Acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ | purple $\to$ colourless | alkene, 1°/2° alcohol, aldehyde |
| Acidified $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$, warm | orange $\to$ green | 1°/2° alcohol, aldehyde |
| $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ / $\text{NaHCO}_3$ | $\text{CO}_2$ bubbles | carboxylic acid |
| Sodium metal | $\text{H}_2$ | –OH (alcohol or acid) |
| Tollens' reagent (warm) | silver mirror | aldehyde |
| Fehling's/Benedict's (warm) | red precipitate | aldehyde |
| $\text{NaOH}$, then $\text{HNO}_3$ and $\text{AgNO}_3$ | white, cream, yellow precipitate | chloro-, bromo-, iodo-alkane |
| Damp blue litmus | turns red | acid |

**Distinguishing**: an aldehyde from a ketone (both $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}$): Tollens' reagent — a silver mirror for propanal only. A carboxylic acid from an alcohol: carbonate. An alkene from an alkane: bromine water.

**Example 1.** An unknown liquid fizzes with sodium carbonate and turns blue litmus red: a carboxylic acid.

**Example 2.** A compound decolourises bromine water but does not fizz with sodium carbonate: an alkene (not an acid).

**Example 3.** A compound that is neutral, unaffected by sodium and carbonate, but hydrolysed by NaOH into an alcohol and a sodium salt of a carboxylic acid: an ester.

## 2. Formula determination

From combustion analysis ($\text{CO}_2$ gives C, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ gives H) find the empirical formula; the **mass spectrum**'s molecular ion peak ($M^+$) gives $M_r$, so the molecular formula follows. Fragments (e.g. $m/z = 29$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5^+$) give structural clues.

**Example 4.** $0.1\ \text{mol}$ of an alkene burns to give $8.8\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ ($0.2\ \text{mol}$): $2$ carbons, so $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$.

## 3. Infrared spectroscopy

Functional groups absorb at characteristic wavenumbers: $\text{C=O}$ near $1700\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (sharp, strong); broad $\text{O-H}$ near $3300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (alcohol) or $2500$–$3300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (carboxylic acid); $\text{C-H}$ near $2900\ \text{cm}^{-1}$.

## 4. Strategy for puzzles

1. Use the formula to find the unsaturation (alkane $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$; one double bond or ring removes $2\text{H}$).
2. Apply the tests in a sensible order (acid test, then oxidation, then bromine).
3. Draw all isomers, then use the observations to choose.

**Example 5.** A liquid that burns with a smoky flame and decolourises bromine water: an unsaturated hydrocarbon (high C : H ratio gives soot).

## Common mistakes

- Using bromine water to prove an aromatic ring.
- Concluding "alcohol" from sodium alone (acids also react).
- Forgetting that ketones are not oxidised by dichromate.

## Practice problems

1. A liquid gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Which functional group?
2. Name a test that separates ethanoic acid from ethanol.

**Answers.** (1) Aldehyde. (2) Sodium carbonate: the acid effervesces.
