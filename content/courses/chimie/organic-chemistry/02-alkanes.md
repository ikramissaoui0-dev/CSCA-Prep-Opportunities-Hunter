## What you will learn

- the structure and properties of alkanes;
- combustion and substitution reactions;
- trends in boiling point.

## 1. Structure

Alkanes are **saturated hydrocarbons** with only single covalent bonds: $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$. The first four: methane $\text{CH}_4$, ethane $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$, propane $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$, butane $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$. Each carbon is tetrahedral ($109.5^\circ$).

## 2. Physical properties

Non-polar, insoluble in water, poor conductors. **Boiling point increases with chain length** (more electrons, stronger London forces): methane $-162\ ^\circ\text{C}$, propane $-42$, hexane $69$. The first four are gases at room temperature; $\text{C}_5$–$\text{C}_{16}$ liquids; larger ones solids. **Branched** isomers boil lower than straight chains (less surface contact): 2-methylpropane $-12\ ^\circ\text{C}$, butane $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

**Example 1.** *Why does pentane boil higher than methane?* Larger molecules have stronger London forces.

## 3. Reactions

Alkanes are fairly unreactive (strong C–C and C–H bonds), but they burn and undergo substitution.

**Combustion**

- Complete: $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2} + \dfrac{3n + 1}2\text{O}_2 \to n\text{CO}_2 + (n + 1)\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Example: $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + 5\text{O}_2 \to 3\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- Incomplete (limited oxygen): $\text{CO}$ (toxic) and/or soot (carbon): $2\text{CH}_4 + 3\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{CO} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$; yellow, smoky flame.

**Substitution with halogens** (UV light): $\text{CH}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{UV}} \text{CH}_3\text{Br} + \text{HBr}$; further substitution gives a mixture. The mechanism is a **free-radical chain reaction**: initiation ($\text{Br}_2 \to 2\text{Br}\cdot$), propagation ($\text{CH}_4 + \text{Br}\cdot \to \cdot\text{CH}_3 + \text{HBr}$; $\cdot\text{CH}_3 + \text{Br}_2 \to \text{CH}_3\text{Br} + \text{Br}\cdot$), termination (two radicals combine).

**Example 2.** The product of methane and bromine in UV light is bromomethane and hydrogen bromide.

**Example 3.** Balanced equation for the complete combustion of butane: $2\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10} + 13\text{O}_2 \to 8\text{CO}_2 + 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

## 4. Uses

Fuels (methane/natural gas, LPG propane and butane, petrol, diesel), solvents, feedstock for the chemical industry, lubricants and wax (long chains).

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the UV light needed for substitution.
- Writing addition products for alkanes.
- Writing an unbalanced combustion equation.

## Practice problems

1. Formula of the alkane with $10$ carbons.
2. Products of the complete combustion of ethane.

**Answers.** (1) $\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22}$. (2) $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $2\text{C}_2\text{H}_6 + 7\text{O}_2 \to 4\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
