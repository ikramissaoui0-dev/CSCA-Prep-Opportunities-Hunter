## What you will learn

- what isomers are;
- the types of isomerism;
- a systematic strategy to draw all isomers.

## 1. Definition

**Isomers** have the **same molecular formula but different structural arrangements** (and so different properties). They matter because the arrangement of atoms determines physical and chemical behaviour.

## 2. Types

- **Chain isomers**: different carbon skeletons: butane and 2-methylpropane ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$).
- **Position isomers**: the same group in different places: propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}$).
- **Functional-group isomers**: different groups: ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) and methoxymethane ($\text{CH}_3\text{OCH}_3$); propanal and propanone ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}$).
- **Geometric (cis–trans)**: around a $\text{C=C}$ where each carbon has two different groups: but-2-ene.
- **Optical**: a carbon with four different groups (chiral centre) gives non-superimposable mirror images.

**Boiling points**: branched isomers boil lower than straight chains (smaller surface contact, weaker London forces): *methylpropane vs butane*.

## 3. Strategy for drawing isomers

1. Start with the longest straight chain.
2. Shorten the chain by one carbon and place it as a branch in every distinct position.
3. Repeat with more branches.
4. For functional groups, move the group along each skeleton.
5. Check for duplicates by naming each structure.

**Example 1.** $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}$ has **3** isomers: pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane.

**Example 2.** $\text{C}_4\text{H}_9\text{Br}$: **4** isomers: 1-bromobutane, 2-bromobutane, 1-bromo-2-methylpropane, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.

**Example 3.** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}$ alcohols: propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol (plus the ether methoxyethane).

**Example 4.** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}$ aldehyde or ketone: propanal and propanone.

**Example 5.** $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$ alkenes: but-1-ene, *cis*-but-2-ene, *trans*-but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene.

**Example 6.** Alcohols $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$: butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol: **4**.

## Common mistakes

- Drawing the same structure twice with a bent chain.
- Forgetting the functional-group isomers.
- Forgetting that cis/trans needs two different groups on each carbon.

## Practice problems

1. How many isomers does $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$ have?
2. How many alcohol isomers has $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}$?

**Answers.** (1) $2$. (2) $2$.
