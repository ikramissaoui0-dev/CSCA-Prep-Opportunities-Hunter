## What you will learn

- the structure of alkenes and why they are reactive;
- addition reactions and Markovnikov's rule;
- the test for unsaturation.

## 1. Structure

Alkenes are **unsaturated hydrocarbons** with a carbon–carbon double bond ($\text{C=C}$): $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$. The first three: ethene $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$, propene $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6$, butene $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$. The double bond is made of a $\sigma$ and a $\pi$ bond; the $\text{C=C}$ unit is **planar** with $120^\circ$ angles, and rotation about it is restricted (which allows *cis/trans* isomers).

## 2. Reactivity

The electron-rich $\pi$ bond makes alkenes much more reactive than alkanes. They undergo **addition reactions**: the double bond opens and atoms add on, with no other product (so the atom economy is $100\%$).

| Reagent | Product | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| $\text{H}_2$ | alkane | Ni catalyst, about $150\ ^\circ\text{C}$ |
| $\text{Br}_2$ | 1,2-dibromoalkane | room temperature |
| $\text{HBr}$, $\text{HCl}$ | haloalkane | room temperature |
| $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (steam) | alcohol | $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$, $300\ ^\circ\text{C}$, high pressure |

**Example 1.** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \to \text{CH}_2\text{BrCH}_2\text{Br}$ (1,2-dibromoethane).

**Example 2.** Hydration of propene: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)CH}_3$ (propan-2-ol, the major product).

**Example 3.** Hydrogenation of butene gives butane.

**Markovnikov's rule**: in the addition of $\text{HX}$ to an unsymmetrical alkene, H adds to the carbon that already has more hydrogens: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \to \text{CH}_3\text{CHBrCH}_3$ (major).

## 3. Test for unsaturation

Shake with **bromine water**: an alkene decolourises it (orange $\to$ colourless); an alkane does not. Alkenes also decolourise acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ (purple $\to$ colourless), which is an oxidation. *How would you tell an alkene from an alkane?* Add bromine water.

## 4. Combustion and polymerisation

Alkenes burn with a smoky flame (a higher carbon proportion). They are the monomers for **addition polymers** (next lessons). Ethene is made industrially by **cracking** and used to make ethanol and poly(ethene).

**Example 4.** *Mechanism idea*: **electrophilic addition** — the $\pi$ electrons attack the electrophile; a carbocation forms; the nucleophile then adds. A more stable (more substituted) carbocation explains Markovnikov's rule.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a substitution product for an alkene.
- Using the wrong colour change for the bromine test.
- Forgetting to write both products in unsymmetrical additions (major and minor).

## Practice problems

1. Product of ethene + HBr.
2. What does bromine water show for hex-1-ene?

**Answers.** (1) Bromoethane, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$. (2) It decolourises: the compound is unsaturated.
