## What you will learn

- the structure and weak-acid behaviour of carboxylic acids;
- their reactions with metals, carbonates and alkalis;
- esters: formation, naming, hydrolysis and uses.

## 1. Structure and properties

Carboxylic acids contain the **carboxyl group** –COOH: $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{COOH}$. First three: methanoic acid $\text{HCOOH}$, ethanoic acid $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ (vinegar), propanoic acid. They form hydrogen bonds and have high boiling points. They are **weak acids**: $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{H}^+$ (only partially ionised), but they still react like acids.

## 2. Reactions as acids

- **With metals**: $2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{Mg} \to (\text{CH}_3\text{COO})_2\text{Mg} + \text{H}_2$.
- **With carbonates**: effervescence of $\text{CO}_2$ (the test that separates carboxylic acids from alcohols).
- **With alkalis**: $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{NaOH} \to \text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (sodium ethanoate).

**Example 1.** *Why does ethanoic acid react more slowly with magnesium than HCl of the same concentration?* It is a weak acid: its $[\text{H}^+]$ is much lower.

**Preparation**: oxidation of a primary alcohol with excess oxidant under reflux.

## 3. Esters

**Esterification**: a carboxylic acid + an alcohol $\rightleftharpoons$ ester + water (conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ catalyst, heated under reflux):

$$
\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}.
$$

**Naming**: the alcohol part first (ending *-yl*), then the acid part (*-oate*): ethyl ethanoate; methyl propanoate (propanoic acid + methanol); ethyl methanoate (methanoic acid + ethanol). Esters are volatile, with pleasant fruity smells (flavourings, perfumes, solvents). Fats and oils are esters of glycerol with fatty acids.

**Hydrolysis**: with dilute acid the reverse equilibrium; with alkali (NaOH) it goes to completion giving the alcohol and the sodium salt of the acid (**saponification**: fat + NaOH $\to$ glycerol + soap).

**Example 2.** Name the ester from methanoic acid and ethanol: ethyl methanoate.

**Example 3.** The conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is a catalyst and also absorbs water, pushing the equilibrium toward the ester.

## 4. Other points

Carboxylic acids are used in food (vinegar, citric acid), preservatives, polymers (polyesters) and pharmaceuticals (aspirin).

## Common mistakes

- Calling carboxylic acids strong acids.
- Naming an ester with the acid part first.
- Forgetting that esterification is reversible.

## Practice problems

1. Equation for ethanoic acid with sodium carbonate (products).
2. Ester formed from propanoic acid and methanol.

**Answers.** (1) Sodium ethanoate, water, carbon dioxide. (2) Methyl propanoate.
