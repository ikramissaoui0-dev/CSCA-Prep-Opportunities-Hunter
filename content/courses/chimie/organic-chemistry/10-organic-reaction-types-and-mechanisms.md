## What you will learn

- the reaction types: addition, substitution, elimination, oxidation, reduction, condensation, hydrolysis;
- reagents and conditions for common conversions;
- mechanism vocabulary (radicals, electrophiles, nucleophiles).

## 1. Reaction types

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Addition** | two molecules combine into one; $\text{C=C}$ becomes C–C | $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{Br}_2$ |
| **Substitution** | an atom or group is replaced | $\text{CH}_4 + \text{Cl}_2 \to \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}$ |
| **Elimination** | a small molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, HX) is removed to form $\text{C=C}$ | $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ |
| **Oxidation** | gain of O / loss of H | alcohol $\to$ aldehyde $\to$ acid |
| **Reduction** | gain of H | alkene $+ \text{H}_2 \to$ alkane |
| **Condensation** | two molecules join with loss of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | esterification, amide formation |
| **Hydrolysis** | reaction with water breaking a bond | ester $\to$ acid + alcohol |

**Addition** reactions have $100\%$ atom economy (no atoms left over); **substitution** and **elimination** make a by-product.

## 2. Haloalkanes

- $\text{RX} + \text{OH}^-$ (**aqueous** NaOH, heat) $\to$ alcohol (**substitution**): $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Br} + \text{OH}^- \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{Br}^-$.
- $\text{RX} + \text{OH}^-$ (**ethanolic** NaOH, heat) $\to$ alkene (**elimination**).

*The choice of solvent decides: aqueous gives substitution, ethanolic gives elimination.*

## 3. Mechanism vocabulary

- **Free-radical substitution** (alkane + halogen, UV): initiation, propagation, termination.
- **Electrophilic addition** (alkene + $\text{Br}_2$, HBr): the electron-rich $\text{C=C}$ attacks an electron-poor species (an **electrophile**); a carbocation intermediate forms; Markovnikov's rule follows from the more stable carbocation.
- **Nucleophilic substitution** (haloalkane + $\text{OH}^-$): a **nucleophile** (a species with a lone pair) attacks the $\delta^+$ carbon and displaces the halide.

Curly arrows show the movement of an electron pair.

## 4. Conversion map

alkane $\xrightarrow{\text{Cl}_2/\text{UV}}$ haloalkane $\xrightarrow{\text{NaOH(aq)}}$ alcohol $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}]}$ aldehyde/ketone $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}]}$ carboxylic acid $\xrightarrow{\text{ROH, H}^+}$ ester; alkene $\xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}}$ alcohol; alcohol $\xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4}$ alkene; haloalkane $\xrightarrow{\text{NaOH(ethanol)}}$ alkene.

**Example 1.** *Bromine water + ethene*: electrophilic addition, giving 1,2-dibromoethane (colourless).

**Example 2.** *Ethanol warmed with acidified potassium dichromate under reflux*: oxidation to ethanoic acid; orange $\to$ green.

**Example 3.** *Ethanoic acid + ethanol with a little conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (warm)*: condensation (esterification) giving ethyl ethanoate and water.

**Example 4.** Route from ethene to ethyl ethanoate: ethene $\to$ ethanol (steam, catalyst) $\to$ ethanoic acid (oxidation) $\to$ ester with another ethanol.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing elimination (ethanolic NaOH) and substitution (aqueous NaOH).
- Giving a reaction without conditions.
- Calling an addition reaction a substitution.

## Practice problems

1. Reagents to convert bromoethane into ethene and into ethanol.
2. Classify $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.

**Answers.** (1) NaOH in ethanol (heat); NaOH(aq) (heat). (2) Elimination (dehydration).
