## What you will learn

- addition polymerisation and the repeat unit;
- common polymers and their uses;
- condensation polymers and the environmental problems of plastics.

## 1. Addition polymerisation

A **polymer** is a very large molecule made from many small **monomers**. In **addition polymerisation** unsaturated monomers (alkenes) join end to end by opening the double bond; no other product forms (the polymer's formula is a multiple of the monomer's).

$$
n\,\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2 \to \bigl(\text{–CH}_2\text{–CH}_2\text{–}\bigr)_n.
$$

To find the **repeat unit** from a monomer, open the double bond; to find the monomer from a polymer, choose the repeat unit and restore the double bond.

| Polymer | Monomer | Repeat unit | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poly(ethene) | $\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CH}_2$ | $-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2-$ | bags, bottles |
| Poly(propene) | $\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CHCH}_3$ | $-\text{CH}_2\text{CH(CH}_3)-$ | ropes, crates |
| Poly(chloroethene), PVC | $\text{CH}_2{=}\text{CHCl}$ | $-\text{CH}_2\text{CHCl}-$ | pipes, cable insulation |
| PTFE | $\text{CF}_2{=}\text{CF}_2$ | $-\text{CF}_2\text{CF}_2-$ | non-stick pans |
| Polystyrene | styrene | | packaging, insulation |

**Example 1.** Repeat unit of poly(propene): $-\text{CH}_2\text{CH(CH}_3)-$. **Example 2.** Monomer for PVC: chloroethene.

## 2. Condensation polymers

Monomers with two functional groups join with loss of a small molecule (usually $\text{H}_2\text{O}$):

- **Polyesters** (PET): a diacid + a diol, with ester links –COO–.
- **Polyamides** (nylon, proteins): a diacid + a diamine, with amide links –CONH–.
- Natural polymers: starch and cellulose (glucose units), proteins (amino acids), DNA.

## 3. Properties and structure

Addition polymers have **C–C backbones**: chemically inert, strong, light, insulating and cheap. Properties depend on chain length and cross-linking (thermoplastics soften on heating; thermosets do not).

## 4. Environmental issues

Addition polymers are **not biodegradable**: poly(ethene) bags cause litter, landfill problems and marine pollution; burning them can release toxic gases (PVC gives HCl). Responses: **reuse and recycle**, incineration with energy recovery, **biodegradable alternatives** (starch-based, PLA), reduced use.

**Example 3.** *Why do poly(ethene) bags cause environmental problems?* They do not rot because the C–C bonds are strong and microorganisms cannot break them down.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a polymer with the C=C double bond still present.
- Forgetting the $n$ and the brackets.
- Calling an addition polymer biodegradable.

## Practice problems

1. Repeat unit of poly(chloroethene).
2. Name the type of polymerisation that forms nylon.

**Answers.** (1) $-\text{CH}_2\text{CHCl}-$. (2) Condensation polymerisation (a polyamide).
