## What you will learn

- the pollutants formed when fuels burn;
- the harm each causes;
- the methods used to reduce pollution.

## 1. Products of combustion

| Product | Source | Harm |
|---|---|---|
| $\text{CO}_2$ | complete combustion | greenhouse gas: global warming |
| $\text{CO}$ | incomplete combustion | toxic: binds to haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen, reducing oxygen transport |
| Soot (C) and particulates | incomplete combustion | respiratory problems, global dimming, smog |
| Unburnt hydrocarbons | engines | smog (with $\text{NO}_x$ and sunlight) |
| $\text{SO}_2$ | sulfur impurities in coal and oil | acid rain: $\text{SO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{H}_2\text{SO}_3$ |
| $\text{NO}_x$ | $\text{N}_2 + \text{O}_2$ at the high temperature of engines | acid rain, photochemical smog, respiratory problems |

**Example 1.** *Why does burning coal produce $\text{SO}_2$ but burning pure methane does not?* Coal contains sulfur compounds; pure methane contains only C and H.

**Example 2.** *How is carbon monoxide harmful?* It binds to haemoglobin (carboxyhaemoglobin), so blood carries less oxygen.

## 2. Effects

- **Acid rain** damages buildings (limestone, metals), forests and lakes.
- **Global warming**: $\text{CO}_2$ and other greenhouse gases trap infrared radiation.
- **Photochemical smog**: $\text{NO}_x$ and hydrocarbons react in sunlight to form ozone and irritants.
- **Ozone layer**: CFCs release chlorine radicals that destroy ozone.

## 3. Reducing pollution

- **Catalytic converters**: Pt/Rh catalysts turn $\text{CO}$, $\text{NO}_x$ and unburnt hydrocarbons into $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{N}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $2\text{CO} + 2\text{NO} \to 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{N}_2$.
- **Desulfurisation** of fuels and **flue-gas desulfurisation** with $\text{CaO}$ or $\text{CaCO}_3$: $\text{SO}_2 + \text{CaO} \to \text{CaSO}_3$.
- Cleaner fuels (natural gas, biofuels, hydrogen), electric vehicles, public transport, energy efficiency, renewable energy, carbon capture.
- CFC replacements; legislation.

## 4. Problems with solutions

**Example 3.** *How does a catalytic converter reduce pollution?* The catalyst provides a surface on which $\text{CO}$ and $\text{NO}$ react to form harmless $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{N}_2$, and hydrocarbons are fully oxidised.

**Example 4.** Environmental problems of fossil fuels in vehicles: $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{NO}_x$, particulates, unburnt hydrocarbons.

## Common mistakes

- Blaming $\text{CO}_2$ for acid rain (it is $\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{NO}_x$).
- Saying nitrogen oxides come from the fuel (they come from air at high temperature).
- Forgetting that complete combustion still produces $\text{CO}_2$.

## Practice problems

1. Which gas causes acid rain: $\text{CO}_2$ or $\text{SO}_2$?
2. How does a catalytic converter treat $\text{CO}$ and $\text{NO}$?

**Answers.** (1) $\text{SO}_2$ (and $\text{NO}_x$). (2) $2\text{CO} + 2\text{NO} \to 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{N}_2$.
