## What you will learn

- what crude oil is and how fractional distillation separates it;
- the main fractions and their properties;
- cracking and why it is important.

## 1. Crude oil

Crude oil is a **finite (non-renewable) resource** formed over millions of years from the remains of marine organisms. It is a **mixture** of hydrocarbons (mostly alkanes) of many chain lengths, with small amounts of sulfur compounds.

## 2. Fractional distillation

The oil is heated and the vapour enters a tall column that is **hot at the bottom and cooler at the top**. Each hydrocarbon rises until it reaches a level cooler than its boiling point, then condenses and is drawn off. Fractions are mixtures with similar boiling points.

| Fraction (top to bottom) | Chain length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Refinery gases | $\text{C}_1$–$\text{C}_4$ | bottled gas, fuel |
| Petrol (gasoline) | $\text{C}_5$–$\text{C}_{10}$ | car fuel |
| Naphtha | $\text{C}_7$–$\text{C}_{14}$ | feedstock for chemicals |
| Kerosene | $\text{C}_{10}$–$\text{C}_{16}$ | jet fuel |
| Diesel | $\text{C}_{14}$–$\text{C}_{20}$ | lorries, trains |
| Fuel oil | $\text{C}_{20}$–$\text{C}_{50}$ | ships, heating |
| Bitumen | $> \text{C}_{50}$ | road surfaces |

**Trends going down the column**: boiling point, viscosity and flame sootiness increase; flammability and volatility decrease. Small molecules (top) have weaker London forces: low boiling points, volatile, ignite easily, burn cleanly. **Example 1.** Kerosene condenses lower than petrol because its larger molecules have higher boiling points.

## 3. Cracking

Large, less useful hydrocarbons are broken into smaller, more useful ones: heat with a **zeolite catalyst** (about $500\ ^\circ\text{C}$) or **steam cracking** at higher temperature. Products: shorter alkanes and **alkenes**.

$$
\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22} \to \text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_4, \qquad \text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{26} \to \text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_4.
$$

Check that carbon and hydrogen balance ($12 = 10 + 2$, $26 = 22 + 4$). Cracking is **economically important** because the demand for petrol and alkenes (for polymers) exceeds their supply from distillation, and long-chain fractions are less in demand.

## 4. Fuels and the environment

Fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas) provide most energy; burning them releases $\text{CO}_2$ (global warming) and pollutants. Alternatives: biofuels (ethanol, biodiesel), hydrogen, solar, wind, nuclear.

**Example 2.** *Why does petrol vaporise more easily than diesel?* Its smaller molecules have weaker intermolecular forces.

## Common mistakes

- Saying fractional distillation involves chemical reactions (it is a physical separation).
- Mixing up the direction of the temperature gradient.
- Saying cracking joins small molecules.

## Practice problems

1. Which fraction has the highest boiling point?
2. Complete: $\text{C}_{14}\text{H}_{30} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + ?$

**Answers.** (1) Bitumen. (2) $\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{26}$.
