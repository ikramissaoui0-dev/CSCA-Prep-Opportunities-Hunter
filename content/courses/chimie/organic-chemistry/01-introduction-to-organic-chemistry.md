## What you will learn

- what organic chemistry is and why carbon is special;
- homologous series and functional groups;
- types of formula and basic naming.

## 1. Why carbon?

**Organic chemistry** is the study of carbon compounds (other than a few simple ones like $\text{CO}$, $\text{CO}_2$, carbonates). Carbon forms **four covalent bonds**, bonds strongly to itself in **chains, branches and rings**, and forms single, double and triple bonds, so a vast number of compounds exist.

## 2. Homologous series

A **homologous series** is a family with the same general formula and the same functional group; consecutive members differ by $\text{CH}_2$; their physical properties change gradually (boiling point rises with chain length) while the chemistry stays similar.

| Series | General formula | Functional group |
|---|---|---|
| Alkanes | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$ | C–C single bonds only |
| Alkenes | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ | $\text{C=C}$ |
| Alcohols | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{OH}$ | –OH |
| Carboxylic acids | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{COOH}$ | –COOH |
| Haloalkanes | $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{X}$ | –Cl, –Br, –I |
| Esters | $\text{RCOOR'}$ | –COO– |

**Saturated** compounds contain only single bonds; **unsaturated** compounds have double or triple bonds.

## 3. Formulae

- **Molecular formula**: the number of each atom ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$).
- **Structural (condensed) formula**: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$.
- **Displayed formula**: every atom and bond drawn.
- **Skeletal formula**: zig-zag lines with carbon at each corner.
- **Empirical formula**: simplest ratio.

## 4. Naming

The stem shows the number of carbons in the longest chain: meth- (1), eth- (2), prop- (3), but- (4), pent- (5), hex- (6). The ending shows the series: -ane, -ene, -anol, -anoic acid. Branches are named as prefixes with the position number (the lowest possible): $\text{CH}_3\text{CH(CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$ is 2-methylbutane.

**Example 1.** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$ is propane. The molecular formula of butene is $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8$. The displayed formula of ethanol shows a $\text{C-C}$ bond, an $\text{O-H}$ bond and five $\text{C/H}$ bonds.

**Example 2.** Name $\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CHCH}_3$: but-2-ene. Name $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$: propan-1-ol.

**Example 3.** The general formula shows an alkane with $6$ carbons has $14$ hydrogens: hexane $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{14}$.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a chain that is not the longest.
- Giving higher locant numbers than necessary.
- Confusing the molecular and empirical formulae.

## Practice problems

1. Formula of an alkane with $8$ carbons and of an alkene with $5$.
2. Name $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$.

**Answers.** (1) $\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18}$ and $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}$. (2) Propanoic acid.
