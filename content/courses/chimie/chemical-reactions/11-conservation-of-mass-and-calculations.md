## What you will learn

- the law of conservation of mass and apparent changes;
- reacting-mass calculations, limiting reagent and percentage yield;
- atom economy.

## 1. Conservation of mass

In a closed system the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants: atoms are neither created nor destroyed, only rearranged.

**Why mass seems to change in an open vessel**: a **decrease** means a gas escaped (e.g. $\text{CO}_2$ from $\text{CaCO}_3$ + acid); an **increase** means a gas was taken from the air. *A student heats magnesium in a crucible and finds a mass increase*: $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{MgO}$; $4.8\ \text{g}$ of Mg gives $8.0\ \text{g}$ of MgO, the extra $3.2\ \text{g}$ being oxygen.

## 2. Reacting-mass calculations

mass $\to$ moles ($n = m/M$) $\to$ ratio from the equation $\to$ moles of the other substance $\to$ mass.

**Example 1.** Mass of water formed when $8\ \text{g}$ of methane burns completely: $\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \to \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $n(\text{CH}_4) = 0.5$; $n(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 1.0$; mass $18\ \text{g}$.

**Example 2.** $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$: $50\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ gives $0.5\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{CaO}$, i.e. $28\ \text{g}$.

**Limiting reagent**: compare moles divided by coefficients; the smaller value limits the product. **Example 3.** $4\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{H}_2$ and $3\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{O}_2$ in $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $\tfrac42 = 2 < \tfrac31 = 3$, so $\text{H}_2$ is limiting and $4\ \text{mol}$ of water forms.

## 3. Yield and atom economy

$$
\text{percentage yield} = \frac{\text{actual yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \times 100\%, \qquad \text{atom economy} = \frac{M_r(\text{desired product})}{\sum M_r(\text{reactants})} \times 100\%.
$$

The yield is below $100\%$ because of incomplete reaction, side reactions, mechanical loss and impure product. **Atom economy** (原子利用率) measures how much of the reactant mass ends up in the useful product, independent of the practical yield.

**Example 4.** A student expected $14\ \text{g}$ and obtained $9.9\ \text{g}$: yield $70.7\%$.

**Example 5.** $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$ with CaO wanted: $\dfrac{56}{100} = 56\%$. Hydration of ethene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$): $100\%$. $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Br} + \text{NaOH} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaBr}$: $\dfrac{46}{149} = 31\%$.

## 4. Gas and solution links

At STP $1\ \text{mol}$ of gas is $22.4\ \text{L}$; $c = \dfrac nV$. For example, $2\text{Al} + 6\text{HCl} \to 2\text{AlCl}_3 + 3\text{H}_2$: $2.7\ \text{g}$ of Al ($0.1\ \text{mol}$) gives $0.15\ \text{mol}$ $\text{H}_2$, i.e. $3.36\ \text{L}$ at STP.

## Common mistakes

- Using masses rather than moles in the ratio.
- Confusing yield with atom economy.
- Forgetting that a limiting reagent controls the product.

## Practice problems

1. Mass of $\text{MgO}$ from $12\ \text{g}$ of Mg.
2. $\text{H}_2$ volume (STP) from $13\ \text{g}$ Zn with excess acid.

**Answers.** (1) $0.5\ \text{mol}$ Mg gives $20\ \text{g}$ MgO. (2) $0.2\ \text{mol}$, $4.48\ \text{L}$.
