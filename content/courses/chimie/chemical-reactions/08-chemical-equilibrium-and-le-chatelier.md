## What you will learn

- reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium;
- the equilibrium constant and ICE tables;
- Le Chatelier's principle and industrial compromises.

## 1. Reversible reactions and equilibrium

In a closed system a **reversible reaction** ($\rightleftharpoons$) reaches **dynamic equilibrium** when the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal: the concentrations are constant (not necessarily equal) and the reaction has not stopped.

**Recognising equilibrium (化学平衡状态的判断)**: $v_{\text{forward}} = v_{\text{reverse}}$ for the same substance; constant concentrations, colour, mass or pressure; any property that changes as the reaction proceeds has stopped changing. A property that never changes (e.g. total pressure when the gas moles are equal on both sides) cannot show equilibrium.

## 2. Equilibrium constant

For $a\text{A} + b\text{B} \rightleftharpoons c\text{C} + d\text{D}$:

$$
K_c = \frac{[\text{C}]^c[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a[\text{B}]^b}.
$$

Pure solids and liquids are omitted. **$K$ depends only on temperature.** A large $K$ means the products dominate. If $Q < K$ the reaction goes forward.

**Example 1 (ICE table).** $2\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{N}_2$ and $6\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{H}_2$ in a $2\ \text{L}$ vessel reach equilibrium after $1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{N}_2$ has reacted. Change: $-1, -3, +2$; at equilibrium $\text{N}_2$ $1$, $\text{H}_2$ $3$, $\text{NH}_3$ $2\ \text{mol}$, i.e. $0.5, 1.5, 1.0\ \text{mol/L}$: $K = \dfrac{1.0^2}{0.5 \cdot 1.5^3} \approx 0.59$. Conversion of $\text{N}_2$: $\dfrac12 = 50\%$ (divide by the *starting* amount).

## 3. Le Chatelier's principle

If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to oppose the change.

| Change | Shift |
|---|---|
| more reactant / remove product | forward |
| higher pressure | toward fewer gas moles |
| higher temperature | toward the endothermic direction |
| lower temperature | toward the exothermic direction |
| catalyst | no shift (equilibrium reached faster) |
| inert gas at constant volume | no shift |

**Example 2.** $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$: adding $\text{SO}_2$ shifts forward (more $\text{SO}_3$); the conversion of $\text{O}_2$ rises, but that of $\text{SO}_2$ falls.

**Example 3.** Opening a fizzy drink lowers the pressure, so $\text{CO}_2(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{CO}_2(g)$ shifts toward gas: bubbles.

**Example 4.** $2\text{NO}_2\ (\text{brown}) \rightleftharpoons \text{N}_2\text{O}_4\ (\text{colourless})$, exothermic forward: cooling makes the mixture paler; reducing the volume shifts toward $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4$ (fewer moles) although the colour first darkens (higher concentration).

## 4. Industrial compromise

The **Haber process** $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ ($\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ}$): high pressure favours $\text{NH}_3$; low temperature favours the exothermic direction but is too slow, so about $450\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $200\ \text{atm}$, iron catalyst. The **Contact process** is exothermic yet runs at about $450\ ^\circ\text{C}$ because lower temperatures are too slow even with the catalyst; the yield is already about $99\%$ at atmospheric pressure.

## Common mistakes

- Saying equilibrium means the concentrations are equal.
- Including solids or liquids in $K$.
- Saying a catalyst changes the yield.

## Practice problems

1. $\text{PCl}_5 \rightleftharpoons \text{PCl}_3 + \text{Cl}_2$ ($\Delta H > 0$): effect on $\text{PCl}_3$ of higher pressure and of higher temperature.
2. Write $K_c$ for $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$.

**Answers.** (1) Less (more gas moles on the right); more (endothermic forward). (2) $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3}$.
