## What you will learn

- the reactivity series and how it is determined;
- displacement reactions and reactions with water and acid;
- extraction of metals and corrosion.

## 1. The reactivity series

From most to least reactive: **K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, (C), Zn, Fe, (H), Cu, Ag, Au**. (The Chinese order, 金属活动性顺序: 钾钙钠镁铝，锌铁锡铅(氢)，铜汞银铂金 — K, Ca, Na, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb, (H), Cu, Hg, Ag, Pt, Au.)

Reactivity is judged from:

- reaction with **water** and **dilute acid**: metals above hydrogen displace $\text{H}_2$ from dilute acids;
- **displacement** from salt solutions: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one;
- the stability of the compounds (the more reactive the metal, the harder it is to extract).

**Example 1.** Can iron displace copper from copper(II) sulfate? Yes: $\text{Fe} + \text{CuSO}_4 \to \text{FeSO}_4 + \text{Cu}$ (a brown deposit forms, the blue solution fades).

**Example 2.** Will zinc react with dilute HCl? Yes: $\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2$. Will copper? No.

**Example 3.** Order from reactions: X reacts with cold water, Y only with steam, Z only with acid: $\text{X} > \text{Y} > \text{Z}$.

## 2. Extraction of metals

The method depends on the position of the metal relative to carbon:

- **Below carbon** (Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu): **reduction with carbon or carbon monoxide**. In the blast furnace, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \to 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$ (CO is the main reducing agent).
- **Above carbon** (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al): **electrolysis** of the molten compound. Aluminium cannot be reduced by carbon because it holds oxygen more strongly; $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ is electrolysed dissolved in molten cryolite.
- **Unreactive** metals (Au, Ag) occur native or are easily reduced.

Extraction methods are chosen also for cost and environmental impact (recycling aluminium saves about $95\%$ of the energy).

## 3. Corrosion and its prevention

**Rusting** of iron needs both **oxygen and water**; salt speeds it up. Protection: paint, grease or plastic coating (barrier); **galvanising** (zinc coating, which also protects when scratched because zinc, being more reactive, corrodes first — sacrificial protection); **sacrificial anodes** (Mg blocks on ship hulls); **alloying** (stainless steel with Cr and Ni).

## 4. Metals and their uses

Aluminium (light, protected by an oxide layer: aircraft, cans); copper (excellent conductor: wires); iron/steel (strong: construction); gold (unreactive: jewellery, electronics); alloys are harder than pure metals.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting copper to give hydrogen with dilute acids.
- Forgetting that iron forms iron(II) with dilute acid.
- Saying the carbon in the furnace reduces iron ore directly (CO does most of it).

## Practice problems

1. Predict whether magnesium will displace zinc from $\text{ZnSO}_4$.
2. Why is aluminium extracted by electrolysis but iron by carbon?

**Answers.** (1) Yes: $\text{Mg} + \text{ZnSO}_4 \to \text{MgSO}_4 + \text{Zn}$. (2) Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide.
