## What you will learn

- what a catalyst is and how it works;
- types of catalyst, enzymes and industrial examples;
- common exam questions on catalysts.

## 1. What a catalyst does

A **catalyst** speeds up a reaction without being used up. It provides an **alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy**, so that more collisions are effective. It:

- lowers $E_a$ for both the forward and the reverse reaction equally;
- does **not** change $\Delta H$, the equilibrium position, the equilibrium constant $K$ or the yield (it only helps equilibrium to be reached sooner);
- remains chemically unchanged at the end (it may be physically altered or "poisoned").

## 2. Types

- **Heterogeneous**: a different phase from the reactants (a solid catalyst for gases): iron in the Haber process, $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ in the Contact process, platinum/rhodium in catalytic converters and the Ostwald process, nickel in hydrogenation.
- **Homogeneous**: the same phase as the reactants (acid catalyst in esterification).
- **Biological catalysts — enzymes**: proteins, very specific, work at about body temperature and optimum pH, denatured by high temperature; e.g. amylase, catalase (breaks down $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$), yeast enzymes in fermentation.

## 3. Examples

- $2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{MnO}_2} 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2$.
- $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{V}_2\text{O}_5} 2\text{SO}_3$.
- $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Fe}} 2\text{NH}_3$.
- Catalytic converters: $2\text{CO} + 2\text{NO} \to 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{N}_2$ on Pt/Rh.

**Example 1.** *A student adds $\text{MnO}_2$ to hydrogen peroxide and sees rapid oxygen production; after the reaction the $\text{MnO}_2$ is recovered unchanged and weighs the same.* This shows it is a catalyst.

**Example 2.** *Sketch the energy profiles with and without a catalyst for an exothermic reaction*: same start and end levels (same $\Delta H$); the catalysed curve has a lower peak.

**Example 3.** *Why is iron used as a catalyst in the Haber process even though it does not change the percentage of $\text{NH}_3$ at equilibrium?* It lets equilibrium be reached quickly at a moderate temperature (which also favours a higher equilibrium yield for the exothermic reaction).

## 4. Why use catalysts industrially?

They allow lower temperatures and pressures, saving energy and cost, and improve selectivity. They are reused, so little is needed.

## Common mistakes

- Saying a catalyst "takes part and is used up".
- Saying a catalyst shifts the equilibrium or raises the yield.
- Saying it changes $\Delta H$.

## Practice problems

1. What happens to $E_a$ and $\Delta H$ when a catalyst is added?
2. Name the catalyst in the Contact process.

**Answers.** (1) $E_a$ falls; $\Delta H$ is unchanged. (2) Vanadium(V) oxide, $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$.
