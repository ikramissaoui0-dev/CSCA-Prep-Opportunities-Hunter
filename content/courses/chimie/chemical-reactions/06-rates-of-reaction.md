## What you will learn

- how to measure and calculate a rate;
- collision theory;
- the factors that affect rate.

## 1. Measuring rate

The **rate** is how fast reactants are used up or products formed. In a closed vessel of volume $V$ (化学反应速率):

$$
v = \frac{\Delta c}{\Delta t} \ \text{in mol/(L}\cdot\text{s)}, \qquad \frac{v_A}a = \frac{v_B}b = \frac{v_C}c = \frac{v_D}d \ \text{for } a\text{A} + b\text{B} \to c\text{C} + d\text{D}.
$$

Measure by gas volume, mass loss, colour change or turbidity. On a graph the rate is the **slope**; it is steepest at the start and falls to zero as reactants are used up.

**Example 1.** $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ in a $2\ \text{L}$ vessel: after $10\ \text{s}$, $0.4\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{N}_2$ has reacted. $v(\text{N}_2) = \dfrac{0.4/2}{10} = 0.02\ \text{mol/(L s)}$; $v(\text{H}_2) = 0.06$; $v(\text{NH}_3) = 0.04$.

## 2. Collision theory (有效碰撞)

Reaction needs **collisions** between particles with energy $\ge E_a$ and the correct orientation (effective collisions). A reaction speeds up if there are **more frequent** or **more energetic** collisions.

## 3. Factors

| Factor | Effect | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration (or gas pressure) | faster | more collisions per second |
| Temperature | much faster (about $\times2$ per $10\ ^\circ\text{C}$) | more particles have $E \ge E_a$; more frequent collisions |
| Surface area (powder vs lump) | faster | more exposed particles |
| Catalyst | faster | alternative route, lower $E_a$ |
| Light (photochemical reactions) | faster | supplies energy |
| Stirring | faster (in solutions/heterogeneous) | brings reactants into contact |

**Example 2.** Powdered $\text{CaCO}_3$ reacts faster with acid than large chips because the exposed surface is bigger; the **total gas** collected at the end is the same (same mass), but the initial slope is steeper.

**Example 3.** *Why does a small temperature rise greatly increase the rate?* The fraction of molecules with $E \ge E_a$ (the tail of the energy distribution) grows rapidly, and collisions are more frequent.

**Example 4.** Reading graphs: a steeper curve = faster; a curve that levels at the same height = the same amount of product; a curve levelling higher = more reactant used.

## 4. Heterogeneous reactions and everyday examples

Dust explosions (flour), fast food spoilage at high temperature, refrigeration to slow bacteria, pressure cookers.

## Common mistakes

- Saying a catalyst increases the yield.
- Confusing the rate with the total amount of product.
- Forgetting that concentration changes the number of collisions *per second*, not their energy.

## Practice problems

1. A reaction $2\text{A} \to \text{B}$: $v(\text{A}) = 0.06\ \text{mol/(L s)}$: $v(\text{B})$.
2. Why does a higher concentration increase the rate?

**Answers.** (1) $0.03\ \text{mol/(L s)}$. (2) More particles per unit volume: more frequent collisions.
