## What you will learn

- definitions of acids and bases;
- strong and weak acids and bases;
- pH, indicators and neutralisation.

## 1. Definitions

- **Arrhenius**: an acid gives $\text{H}^+$ in water; a base gives $\text{OH}^-$. An **alkali** is a soluble base.
- **Brønsted–Lowry**: an acid is a **proton donor**; a base is a **proton acceptor**. Conjugate pairs: $\text{NH}_4^+/\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{OH}^-$, $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}/\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$.

**Strong acids** ($\text{HCl}$, $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $\text{HNO}_3$) ionise **completely**; **weak acids** ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$, $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$) only partly. **Strong bases**: NaOH, KOH, $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$; weak: $\text{NH}_3$. "Strong" and "concentrated" are different ideas.

## 2. The pH scale

$$
\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+], \qquad \text{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\text{OH}^-], \qquad \text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14 \ (25\ ^\circ\text{C}), \qquad K_w = [\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-] = 10^{-14}.
$$

pH $< 7$ acidic, $= 7$ neutral, $> 7$ alkaline. Each unit of pH changes $[\text{H}^+]$ by a factor of $10$.

**Example 1.** $0.01\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl: $\text{pH} = 2$. $0.01\ \text{mol/L}$ NaOH: $\text{pOH} = 2$, $\text{pH} = 12$. $0.005\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$: $[\text{OH}^-] = 0.01$, $\text{pH} = 12$.

**Example 2.** A strong acid diluted ten times has its pH increased by $1$.

**Example 3.** Mixing $50\ \text{mL}$ of $0.10\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl with $50\ \text{mL}$ of $0.06\ \text{mol/L}$ NaOH: excess $\text{H}^+ = 0.005 - 0.003 = 0.002\ \text{mol}$ in $0.1\ \text{L}$: $0.02\ \text{mol/L}$, $\text{pH} = 1.7$.

## 3. Indicators

| Indicator | Acid | Neutral | Alkali |
|---|---|---|---|
| Litmus | red | purple | blue |
| Methyl orange | red | orange | yellow (changes near pH 3.5) |
| Phenolphthalein | colourless | colourless | pink (changes near pH 9) |
| Universal indicator | red/orange | green | blue/purple |

## 4. Neutralisation

Acid + base $\to$ salt + water; $\text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^- \to \text{H}_2\text{O}$; exothermic. **Example 4.** $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{KOH} \to \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

**Example 5.** *Why is ethanoic acid called weak while hydrochloric acid is strong?* Ethanoic acid is only partially ionised in water ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{H}^+$); hydrochloric acid is fully ionised.

**Example 6.** At the same concentration a strong acid has a lower pH and conducts better than a weak acid, but both neutralise the same amount of alkali.

**Titration.** At the end point $n_{\text{acid}}$ and $n_{\text{base}}$ are in the ratio of the equation: $25.0\ \text{mL}$ of NaOH neutralised by $20.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.10\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl: $c = \dfrac{0.0020}{0.0250} = 0.080\ \text{mol/L}$.

## Common mistakes

- Using $\text{pH} = -\log c$ for a weak acid.
- Confusing strong with concentrated.
- Forgetting the volume change when mixing solutions.

## Practice problems

1. pH of $0.001\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl and of $0.01\ \text{mol/L}$ NaOH.
2. Ionic equation for $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{KOH}$.

**Answers.** (1) $3$ and $12$. (2) $\text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^- \to \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
