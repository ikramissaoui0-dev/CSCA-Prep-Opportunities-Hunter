## What you will learn

- exothermic and endothermic changes;
- energy profile diagrams and activation energy;
- bond energy calculations.

## 1. Definitions

- **Exothermic**: heat is released to the surroundings; the temperature of the surroundings **rises**; $\Delta H < 0$. Examples: combustion, neutralisation, respiration, many oxidations.
- **Endothermic**: heat is absorbed from the surroundings; the temperature **falls**; $\Delta H > 0$. Examples: thermal decomposition, photosynthesis, dissolving ammonium nitrate, citric acid + sodium bicarbonate.

Bond **breaking** absorbs energy (endothermic); bond **making** releases energy (exothermic).

## 2. Energy profiles

Plot energy against reaction progress. The **activation energy** $E_a$ is the energy barrier from reactants to the top of the curve.

- Exothermic: products lie **below** reactants; $\Delta H$ is the (negative) height difference.
- Endothermic: products lie **above** reactants.
- A **catalyst** lowers $E_a$ (for both directions) without changing $\Delta H$.

For a reverse reaction: $E_a(\text{reverse}) = E_a(\text{forward}) - \Delta H$. With $\Delta H = +50\ \text{kJ}$ and $E_a = 120\ \text{kJ}$: $E_a(\text{reverse}) = 70\ \text{kJ}$.

## 3. Bond energies

$$
\Delta H = \sum(\text{bonds broken}) - \sum(\text{bonds formed}).
$$

**Example 1.** $\text{H}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{HCl}$ with $\text{H-H} = 436$, $\text{Cl-Cl} = 243$, $\text{H-Cl} = 432\ \text{kJ/mol}$: $\Delta H = 436 + 243 - 2(432) = -185\ \text{kJ}$ (exothermic).

**Example 2.** The bonds broken in a reaction need $800\ \text{kJ}$ and the bonds made release $1200\ \text{kJ}$: $\Delta H = -400\ \text{kJ}$, exothermic.

**Example 3.** A student mixes citric acid and sodium bicarbonate in water: the temperature drops — endothermic.

## 4. Measuring energy changes

Calorimetry: $Q = mc\Delta T$ for the solution, then $\Delta H = -\dfrac Qn$ per mole of the limiting reagent. Heat losses make the measured value smaller in magnitude than the true one.

**Example 4.** $50\ \text{mL}$ of $1.0\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl + $50\ \text{mL}$ of $1.0\ \text{mol/L}$ NaOH, $\Delta T = 6.8\ ^\circ\text{C}$: $Q = 100 \cdot 4.18 \cdot 6.8 = 2842\ \text{J}$; $n = 0.050$; $\Delta H = -56.8\ \text{kJ/mol}$.

## 5. Applications

Hand warmers and self-heating cans (exothermic); cold packs (endothermic); fuels (combustion); energy storage in food.

## Common mistakes

- Reversing the sign of $\Delta H$.
- Saying "bond breaking releases energy".
- Believing a catalyst changes $\Delta H$.

## Practice problems

1. Is $\text{N}_2 + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{NO}$ exothermic if breaking needs $945 + 498$ and forming releases $2 \cdot 631\ \text{kJ}$?
2. How does a catalyst change the energy profile?

**Answers.** (1) $\Delta H = 1443 - 1262 = +181\ \text{kJ}$: endothermic. (2) It lowers the activation energy (a lower peak) but not $\Delta H$.
