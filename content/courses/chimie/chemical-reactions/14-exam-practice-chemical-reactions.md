## What you will learn

- the question types and the method for each;
- worked exam-style questions with answers.

## 1. Question types

| Type | Method |
|---|---|
| Balancing equations | coefficients only; check atoms and charge |
| Reaction identification | combination, decomposition, displacement, neutralisation, redox |
| Acid reactions | acid + metal/oxide/hydroxide/carbonate |
| Energy calculations | bond energies, $Q = mc\Delta T$, energy profiles |
| Rate questions | collision theory, graph slope |
| Reacting-mass calculations | mass $\to$ moles $\to$ ratio $\to$ mass; limiting reagent; yield |
| Redox | oxidation numbers; oxidising/reducing agents; half-equations |
| Equilibrium | Le Chatelier; $K$; ICE table |

## 2. Worked questions

**Q1.** Calculate the mass of water produced when $8\ \text{g}$ of methane burns. *Answer*: $\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \to \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $0.5\ \text{mol}$ gives $1\ \text{mol}$ of water: $18\ \text{g}$.

**Q2.** Explain why raising the temperature increases the rate of the reaction between magnesium and acid. *Answer*: particles move faster, so collisions are more frequent, and a greater fraction have energy $\ge E_a$: more effective collisions per second.

**Q3.** Iron displaces copper from copper sulfate: write the ionic equation and identify the oxidising agent. *Answer*: $\text{Fe} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \to \text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{Cu}$; $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ is the oxidising agent.

**Q4.** $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$, $\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ}$: explain the conditions. *Answer*: high pressure favours the side with fewer gas moles; a moderate $450\ ^\circ\text{C}$ is a compromise because low temperature favours the product but is too slow; an iron catalyst speeds up the approach to equilibrium.

**Q5.** A $0.1\ \text{mol/L}$ solution of a weak acid has a higher pH than $0.1\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl. Why? *Answer*: the weak acid is only partially ionised, so $[\text{H}^+]$ is lower.

**Q6.** $50\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ is heated. Find the volume of $\text{CO}_2$ at STP. *Answer*: $0.5\ \text{mol}$; $11.2\ \text{L}$.

**Q7.** In $\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2$ identify the oxidised substance. *Answer*: zinc ($0 \to +2$).

**Q8.** Predict the products at the electrodes for molten $\text{PbBr}_2$. *Answer*: lead at the cathode, bromine at the anode.

**Q9.** $0.2\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{N}_2$ and $0.5\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{H}_2$ form $\text{NH}_3$: limiting reagent and maximum product. *Answer*: with $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3$, compare moles divided by coefficient: $\text{N}_2$: $\dfrac{0.2}1 = 0.2$; $\text{H}_2$: $\dfrac{0.5}3 = 0.167$. The smaller value belongs to $\text{H}_2$, so it is limiting and the maximum is $\dfrac23 \cdot 0.5 = 0.333\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{NH}_3$.

**Q10.** A reaction has $\Delta H = -120\ \text{kJ}$ and $E_a = 60\ \text{kJ}$: $E_a$ for the reverse. *Answer*: $180\ \text{kJ}$.

## 3. Quick reminders

$\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}^+]$; $c_1V_1 = c_2V_2$; $\text{yield} = \dfrac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}}$; $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{products}]}{[\text{reactants}]}$; catalysts never change $\Delta H$, $K$ or the yield.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the limiting reagent by mass instead of moles divided by coefficients.
- Mixing up the signs of $\Delta H$.
- Forgetting the physical state in ionic equations.

## Practice problem

$25.0\ \text{mL}$ of NaOH is neutralised by $20.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.10\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl: $c(\text{NaOH})$.

**Answer.** $0.080\ \text{mol/L}$.
