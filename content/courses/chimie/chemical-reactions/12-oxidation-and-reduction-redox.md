## What you will learn

- oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen, hydrogen, electrons and oxidation number;
- oxidising and reducing agents;
- half-equations and balancing.

## 1. Definitions

| | Oxidation | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | gain | loss |
| Hydrogen | loss | gain |
| Electrons | **loss** | **gain** |
| Oxidation number | increases | decreases |

**OIL RIG** — Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons). In Chinese: "升失氧，降得还" — oxidation number rises, electrons lost, oxidised; oxidation number falls, electrons gained, reduced.

Both always occur together (a **redox** reaction). The **oxidising agent** is reduced (it takes electrons); the **reducing agent** is oxidised (it gives electrons).

## 2. Oxidation numbers

Rules: elements $= 0$; monatomic ions = their charge; $\text{O} = -2$ (except peroxides $-1$); $\text{H} = +1$ (except metal hydrides); the sum equals the overall charge.

**Example 1.** Mn in $\text{KMnO}_4$: $+1 + x - 8 = 0$, $x = +7$. Cr in $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$: $+6$. S in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: $+6$.

**Example 2.** $2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{C} \to 4\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$: Fe $+3 \to 0$ (reduced; $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ is the oxidising agent); C $0 \to +4$ (oxidised; C is the reducing agent).

**Example 3.** $\text{Mg} + \text{Cl}_2 \to \text{MgCl}_2$: half-equations $\text{Mg} \to \text{Mg}^{2+} + 2e^-$ and $\text{Cl}_2 + 2e^- \to 2\text{Cl}^-$.

**Example 4.** Is $\text{NaOH} + \text{HCl} \to \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ redox? No oxidation number changes.

**Example 5.** $\text{MnO}_2 + 4\text{HCl(conc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{MnCl}_2 + \text{Cl}_2\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$: Mn $+4 \to +2$ (oxidising agent $\text{MnO}_2$); Cl $-1 \to 0$ for **two of the four** HCl; the other two act as an acid.

## 3. Balancing with half-equations

1. Write the two half-equations. 2. Balance atoms other than O and H; O with $\text{H}_2\text{O}$; H with $\text{H}^+$. 3. Balance charge with electrons. 4. Multiply to equalise electrons and add.

**Example 6.** $\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5e^- \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{Fe}^{2+} \to \text{Fe}^{3+} + e^-$ ($\times5$):

$$
\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5\text{Fe}^{2+} \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{Fe}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}.
$$

The purple solution turns almost colourless.

## 4. Special types

**Disproportionation**: one species is both oxidised and reduced: $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$; $2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2$.

**Common agents**: oxidising — $\text{O}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{KMnO}_4$, $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, conc. $\text{HNO}_3$, $\text{Fe}^{3+}$; reducing — metals, $\text{H}_2$, C, CO, $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, $\text{I}^-$, $\text{SO}_2$.

## Common mistakes

- Calling the substance that is reduced the reducing agent.
- Forgetting to balance the charge in half-equations.
- Treating an oxidation number as a real charge.

## Practice problems

1. Oxidation number of S in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ and in $\text{SO}_2$.
2. In $2\text{Al} + \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \to \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{Fe}$ name the reducing agent.

**Answers.** (1) $+6$ and $+4$. (2) Aluminium.
