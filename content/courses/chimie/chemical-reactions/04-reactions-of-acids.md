## What you will learn

- the four general reactions of acids;
- naming the salts formed;
- how to test the gases and prepare a salt.

## 1. General reactions

1. **Acid + metal $\to$ salt + hydrogen** (only for metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series): $\text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{H}_2$; $\text{Mg} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{MgCl}_2 + \text{H}_2$. Iron gives iron(II). Copper does not react with dilute acids.
2. **Acid + metal oxide $\to$ salt + water**: $\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{CuSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (a black solid dissolves to a blue solution).
3. **Acid + metal hydroxide (or alkali) $\to$ salt + water**: $\text{NaOH} + \text{HCl} \to \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
4. **Acid + carbonate $\to$ salt + water + carbon dioxide**: $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{HNO}_3 \to 2\text{NaNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$; effervescence.

(Acid + ammonia gives an ammonium salt: $\text{NH}_3 + \text{HCl} \to \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$.)

## 2. Naming salts

The first part names the metal (or ammonium); the second part comes from the acid: hydrochloric $\to$ **chloride**; sulfuric $\to$ **sulfate**; nitric $\to$ **nitrate**; carbonic $\to$ carbonate; ethanoic $\to$ ethanoate; phosphoric $\to$ phosphate.

**Example 1.** Zinc + sulfuric acid: zinc sulfate and hydrogen.

**Example 2.** Copper(II) oxide + hydrochloric acid: copper(II) chloride (blue-green) and water.

**Example 3.** Sodium carbonate + nitric acid: sodium nitrate, water and carbon dioxide.

## 3. Tests

- **Hydrogen**: lighted splint, squeaky pop.
- **Carbon dioxide**: limewater turns milky.

## 4. Making a soluble salt

From an insoluble base, carbonate or metal: add the solid to the warm acid until in excess (no more dissolves), filter off the excess, evaporate part of the water, cool to crystallise, filter and dry. From a soluble base (alkali): find the exact volume by titration with an indicator, then repeat without the indicator. **Example 4.** Pure copper(II) sulfate crystals: add excess CuO to warm dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, filter, crystallise.

**Example 5.** *Why does ethanoic acid react more slowly with magnesium than hydrochloric acid of the same concentration?* It is a weak acid with a lower $[\text{H}^+]$.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting copper to react with dilute acids.
- Writing iron(III) from iron + acid (it is iron(II)).
- Forgetting water in the carbonate reaction.

## Practice problems

1. Equation for magnesium oxide + hydrochloric acid.
2. Products of calcium carbonate + hydrochloric acid.

**Answers.** (1) $\text{MgO} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{MgCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. (2) Calcium chloride, water, carbon dioxide.
