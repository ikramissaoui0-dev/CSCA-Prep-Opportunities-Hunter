## What you will learn

- the structures of diamond, graphite, silica and graphene;
- how structure explains properties;
- uses and model answers.

## 1. What is a giant covalent structure?

A huge network of atoms joined by strong covalent bonds throughout the crystal. Typical properties: **very high melting points** (many strong bonds must be broken), usually **insoluble**, **hard** (except graphite), and **non-conducting** (except graphite).

## 2. Diamond

Each carbon is bonded to **four** others in a rigid **tetrahedral** network. Extremely hard, very high melting point ($>3500\ ^\circ\text{C}$), **does not conduct** (no free electrons). Uses: cutting and drilling tools, jewellery.

## 3. Graphite

Each carbon is bonded to **three** others in **hexagonal layers**; the fourth outer electron is **delocalised** between the layers. Layers are held by weak forces, so they **slide over each other** (soft, lubricant, pencil leads); the delocalised electrons make graphite a good **conductor** (electrodes). Very high melting point.

## 4. Silicon dioxide and graphene

**Silicon dioxide** (silica, sand, quartz): each Si bonded to four O, each O to two Si; hard, high melting point ($1700\ ^\circ\text{C}$), insulator. **Graphene**: a single layer of graphite — very strong, flexible, transparent and conducting: suggested for flexible touch screens, composites and batteries. **Fullerenes** ($\text{C}_{60}$) are simple molecular, not giant.

## 5. Model answers

**Example 1.** *Explain why diamond is hard but graphite is soft.* In diamond each carbon forms four strong covalent bonds in a rigid network that resists deformation; in graphite the layers are held only by weak forces, so they slide.

**Example 2.** *Why does graphite conduct but diamond does not?* Graphite has delocalised electrons that move between the layers; in diamond all four electrons per carbon are in bonds.

**Example 3.** *Why is silicon dioxide's melting point so high compared with $\text{CO}_2$?* $\text{SiO}_2$ is a giant covalent network (strong bonds must be broken); $\text{CO}_2$ is simple molecular with weak intermolecular forces.

**Example 4.** Compare sodium chloride and diamond: NaCl — ionic lattice, conducts when molten/dissolved, brittle, soluble; diamond — covalent network, never conducts, very hard, insoluble.

## Common mistakes

- Saying graphite conducts because of ions.
- Saying diamond conducts when molten.
- Calling a giant covalent structure a molecule.

## Practice problems

1. Number of bonds per carbon in diamond and graphite.
2. Why is graphite used as a lubricant and as an electrode?

**Answers.** (1) $4$ and $3$. (2) Layers slide (lubricant); delocalised electrons conduct (electrode).
