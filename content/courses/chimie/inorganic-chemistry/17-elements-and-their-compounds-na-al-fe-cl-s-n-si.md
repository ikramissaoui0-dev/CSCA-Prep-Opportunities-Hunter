## What you will learn

- the key reactions, compounds and tests of seven elements;
- how the industrial processes link to the chemistry;
- quantitative problems on these elements.

## 1. Sodium

Soft, stored under oil; $2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$; burns in air with a yellow flame to give $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$ (and $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$). **$\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$** reacts with water and $\text{CO}_2$ releasing oxygen: $2\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{NaOH} + \text{O}_2$; $2\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2 \to 2\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{O}_2$ (oxygen source in submarines; $1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$ transfers $1\ \text{mol}$ of electrons). $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (soda ash, stable) and $\text{NaHCO}_3$ (baking soda, $2\text{NaHCO}_3 \to \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$): distinguish by heating, by the speed of fizzing with acid, or with $\text{CaCl}_2$ (only $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ gives a precipitate).

## 2. Aluminium

Reactive but protected by a thin tough $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ layer. **Amphoteric** $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ and $\text{Al(OH)}_3$: they dissolve in acid and in strong alkali: $\text{Al(OH)}_3 + 3\text{H}^+ \to \text{Al}^{3+} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $\text{Al(OH)}_3 + \text{OH}^- \to [\text{Al(OH)}_4]^-$. In excess aqueous ammonia it does **not** redissolve. Extracted by electrolysis of alumina in molten cryolite at about $950\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Thermite: $2\text{Al} + \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \to \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{Fe}$.

**Example 1.** NaOH added to $50\ \text{mL}$ of $2\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{AlCl}_3$ ($0.1\ \text{mol}$ $\text{Al}^{3+}$): maximum precipitate $0.1\ \text{mol}$ ($7.8\ \text{g}$) needs $0.3\ \text{mol}$ NaOH; excess dissolves it (needs $0.1\ \text{mol}$ more). A precipitate of $3.9\ \text{g}$ ($0.05\ \text{mol}$) can come from $0.15\ \text{mol}$ NaOH or from $0.35\ \text{mol}$ (partly redissolved): **two answers**.

## 3. Iron

Extracted in the **blast furnace** with coke, limestone and hot air (CO is the main reducing agent: $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \to 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$; limestone forms slag). $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ (pale green) $\to$ $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ (yellow/brown) is easy: $2\text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2\text{Cl}^-$; $2\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{Fe} \to 3\text{Fe}^{2+}$ (so $\text{FeSO}_4$ solutions are stored with iron nails). $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ + KSCN: blood-red. Etching: $2\text{FeCl}_3 + \text{Cu} \to 2\text{FeCl}_2 + \text{CuCl}_2$.

## 4. Chlorine

Yellow-green toxic gas. Lab: $\text{MnO}_2 + 4\text{HCl(conc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{MnCl}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; industrially by the chlor-alkali process. With water: $\text{Cl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{HCl} + \text{HClO}$ ($\text{HClO}$ bleaches and disinfects). With cold dilute NaOH: bleach ($\text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO}$). Test: turns damp blue litmus red, then bleaches it; displaces bromine and iodine.

## 5. Sulfur

Yellow solid ($\text{S}_8$). **$\text{SO}_2$**: choking gas; decolourises acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ and bromine water (a reducing agent); bleaches temporarily; causes acid rain; $\text{SO}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{HCl}$. **Sulfuric acid** (Contact process: $\text{S} \to \text{SO}_2 \to \text{SO}_3$ with $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$, $450\ ^\circ\text{C}$). Concentrated acid is **dehydrating** (turns sugar black), a hot **oxidising** agent ($\text{Cu} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{CuSO}_4 + \text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, half acting as acid and half as oxidant) and a **drying agent** (not for $\text{NH}_3$). Add acid to water.

## 6. Nitrogen

$\text{N}_2$ is inert (triple bond). **Ammonia**: Haber process; lab: $2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, collected by **downward delivery** (lighter than air), dried with $\text{CaO}$ (not $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{CaCl}_2$); very soluble (the ammonia fountain turns phenolphthalein red). **Nitric acid** (Ostwald process): a strong oxidising acid; with copper, dilute gives $\text{NO}$ (turns brown in air), concentrated gives $\text{NO}_2$ (brown). Fertilisers; excess runoff causes eutrophication. Test for $\text{NH}_4^+$: warm with NaOH.

## 7. Silicon

A semiconductor; from $\text{SiO}_2 + 2\text{C} \to \text{Si} + 2\text{CO}$. $\text{SiO}_2$ is a giant covalent network (sand, quartz, glass); $\text{SiO}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (so alkali is not stored in glass-stoppered bottles); $\text{SiO}_2 + 4\text{HF} \to \text{SiF}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (HF etches glass). Uses: solar cells, chips, silicones, ceramics, cement.

## Common mistakes

- Drying ammonia with concentrated sulfuric acid.
- Forgetting that $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ is amphoteric but $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$ is not.
- Giving the same product for dilute and concentrated nitric acid with copper.

## Practice problems

1. A white precipitate forms with NaOH and dissolves in excess: which ions?
2. Two functions of limestone in the blast furnace.

**Answers.** (1) $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ or $\text{Pb}^{2+}$. (2) It decomposes to CaO, which removes $\text{SiO}_2$ as slag; the $\text{CO}_2$ formed is reduced to CO.
