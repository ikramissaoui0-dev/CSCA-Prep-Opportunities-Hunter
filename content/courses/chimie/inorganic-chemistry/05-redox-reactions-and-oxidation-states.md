## What you will learn

- oxidation states and how to assign them;
- oxidising and reducing agents;
- half-equations and balancing redox reactions in acid solution.

## 1. Definitions

**OIL RIG**: oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain. In Chinese textbooks: "升失氧，降得还". The **oxidising agent** is reduced; the **reducing agent** is oxidised.

## 2. Oxidation states

Rules: elements $= 0$; simple ions = their charge; $\text{O} = -2$ (peroxides $-1$); $\text{H} = +1$ (metal hydrides $-1$); halogens $-1$ in halides; the sum equals the overall charge. Typical: Group 1 $+1$; Group 2 $+2$; Al $+3$; S $-2, 0, +4, +6$; N from $-3$ ($\text{NH}_3$) to $+5$ ($\text{HNO}_3$); Fe $+2, +3$; Mn $+2, +4, +7$; Cr $+3, +6$.

**Example 1.** S in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: $+6$; in $\text{SO}_2$: $+4$; in $\text{H}_2\text{S}$: $-2$.

**Example 2.** $2\text{Al} + \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \to \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{Fe}$: Al $0 \to +3$ is oxidised (the reducing agent); Fe $+3 \to 0$ is reduced.

## 3. Half-equations

**Method** (acid solution): balance atoms other than O and H; add $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ for O; add $\text{H}^+$ for H; add electrons for charge; multiply to equalise electrons; add.

**Example 3.** $\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5e^- \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (reduction) and $\text{Fe}^{2+} \to \text{Fe}^{3+} + e^-$ ($\times 5$ for the oxidation):

$$
\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5\text{Fe}^{2+} \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{Fe}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}.
$$

**Example 4.** $3\text{Cu} + 8\text{HNO}_3(\text{dilute}) \to 3\text{Cu(NO}_3)_2 + 2\text{NO} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$: Cu loses $2e^-$, N gains $3e^-$; LCM $6$.

## 4. Types and agents

**Disproportionation**: $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$; $2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2$. **Comproportionation**: $\text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{S} \to 3\text{S} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

Common oxidising agents: $\text{KMnO}_4$ (acid: $\text{Mn}^{2+}$, almost colourless), $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ ($\text{Cr}^{3+}$, green), $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, conc. $\text{HNO}_3$, $\text{Fe}^{3+}$. Reducing agents: metals, $\text{H}_2$, C, CO, $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, $\text{I}^-$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$.

**Redox test**: a reaction is redox if an oxidation number changes. $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$ is not redox; concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ oxidises copper: $\text{Cu} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{CuSO}_4 + \text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

## Common mistakes

- Treating oxidation numbers as true charges.
- Reversing oxidising and reducing agents.
- Not balancing the charge in half-equations.

## Practice problems

1. Oxidation state of N in $\text{NH}_4^+$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$.
2. Is $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \to \text{H}_2\text{O} + \tfrac12\text{O}_2$ redox? What kind?

**Answers.** (1) $-3$ and $+5$. (2) Yes: disproportionation of oxygen ($-1 \to -2$ and $0$).
