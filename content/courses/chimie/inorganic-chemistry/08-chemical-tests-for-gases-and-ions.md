## What you will learn

- tests for the common gases;
- tests for cations and anions;
- order of tests and interference.

## 1. Gases

| Gas | Test and result |
|---|---|
| $\text{H}_2$ | lighted splint: squeaky pop |
| $\text{O}_2$ | glowing splint relights |
| $\text{CO}_2$ | limewater turns milky (clears with excess) |
| $\text{NH}_3$ | damp red litmus turns blue; white smoke with HCl |
| $\text{Cl}_2$ | damp blue litmus turns red then bleaches; yellow-green |
| $\text{SO}_2$ | decolourises acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ or bromine water; choking smell |
| $\text{HCl}$ | misty fumes; white smoke with $\text{NH}_3$ |
| $\text{NO}_2$ | brown gas ($\text{NO}$ turns brown in air) |
| Water vapour | turns anhydrous $\text{CuSO}_4$ blue (or cobalt chloride pink) |

A burning splint does **not** identify $\text{CO}_2$ ($\text{N}_2$ also extinguishes it).

## 2. Cations (add NaOH, a few drops then excess)

| Ion | Result |
|---|---|
| $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ | pale-blue precipitate, insoluble in excess |
| $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ | green precipitate, turning red-brown in air |
| $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ | red-brown precipitate |
| $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$, $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ | white precipitate, **dissolves in excess** NaOH |
| $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ | white precipitate, insoluble in excess |
| $\text{NH}_4^+$ | warm with NaOH: $\text{NH}_3$ gas |

With aqueous ammonia: $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ redissolves in excess, $\text{Al}^{3+}$ does not. $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ with $\text{SCN}^-$: blood-red. **Flame tests**: Li red, Na yellow, K lilac, Ca orange-red, Cu blue-green.

## 3. Anions

| Ion | Test |
|---|---|
| $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ | add dilute acid: $\text{CO}_2$ turns limewater milky |
| $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ | add dilute HCl then $\text{BaCl}_2$: white precipitate $\text{BaSO}_4$ |
| $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{Br}^-$, $\text{I}^-$ | add dilute $\text{HNO}_3$ then $\text{AgNO}_3$: white, cream, yellow precipitates |
| $\text{NO}_3^-$ | warm with NaOH and aluminium foil: $\text{NH}_3$ released |
| $\text{OH}^-$ | turns red litmus blue (pH $> 7$) |

**Order matters**: acidify first, to remove carbonate (which would otherwise give white $\text{BaCO}_3$ or $\text{Ag}_2\text{CO}_3$) before adding $\text{BaCl}_2$ or $\text{AgNO}_3$. Test for carbonate before halide.

## 4. Worked questions

**Example 1.** A solution gives a white precipitate with NaOH that quickly turns grey-green then red-brown: it contains $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ (white $\text{Fe(OH)}_2$ rapidly oxidised in air).

**Example 2.** Test for sulfate: add dilute HCl (removes carbonate), then $\text{BaCl}_2$ solution: a white precipitate of $\text{BaSO}_4$ confirms sulfate.

**Example 3.** An unknown solid fizzes with acid and the gas turns limewater milky: a carbonate.

**Example 4.** A white solid gives a yellow flame and its solution a white precipitate with $\text{AgNO}_3$ (after $\text{HNO}_3$) that dissolves in dilute ammonia: sodium chloride.

**Example 5.** A white precipitate with NaOH that dissolves in excess NaOH: $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ or $\text{Pb}^{2+}$; add excess ammonia: $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ redissolves, $\text{Al}^{3+}$ does not.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ (check excess NaOH).
- Adding $\text{BaCl}_2$ or $\text{AgNO}_3$ without acidifying.
- Concluding sulfate from a white precipitate alone.

## Practice problems

1. Test to distinguish $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ from $\text{Fe}^{3+}$.
2. How would you confirm that a gas is $\text{NH}_3$?

**Answers.** (1) NaOH: green vs red-brown precipitate. (2) Damp red litmus turns blue; white smoke with conc. HCl.
