## What you will learn

- the predictions at each electrode for various electrolytes;
- half-equations and quantitative electrolysis;
- applications: refining, plating and the chlor-alkali process.

## 1. Setting up

An electrolyte with **mobile ions** (molten or aqueous) is decomposed. The **cathode** is negative (reduction); the **anode** is positive (oxidation). Inert electrodes (graphite, platinum) or **active** electrodes (the anode metal may dissolve).

## 2. Molten compounds

Half-equations for molten $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$: cathode $\text{Al}^{3+} + 3e^- \to \text{Al}$; anode $2\text{O}^{2-} \to \text{O}_2 + 4e^-$ (the oxygen burns the carbon anodes). Molten NaCl: cathode $\text{Na}^+ + e^- \to \text{Na}$; anode $2\text{Cl}^- \to \text{Cl}_2 + 2e^-$.

## 3. Aqueous solutions

Water contributes $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{OH}^-$.

- **Cathode**: the less reactive of the metal ion and $\text{H}^+$ is reduced: $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \text{Cu}$; for reactive metals $2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \to \text{H}_2$ (or $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \to \text{H}_2 + 2\text{OH}^-$).
- **Anode**: a halide is oxidised in preference to $\text{OH}^-$ (in concentrated solution); otherwise $4\text{OH}^- \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2 + 4e^-$.

**Example 1.** Brine (concentrated NaCl): cathode $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \to \text{H}_2 + 2\text{OH}^-$; anode $2\text{Cl}^- \to \text{Cl}_2 + 2e^-$; NaOH is left in solution.

**Example 2.** $\text{CuSO}_4(aq)$ with inert electrodes: Cu at the cathode, $\text{O}_2$ at the anode; the solution becomes acidic. With **copper electrodes** the anode dissolves and the cathode grows (refining/plating).

**Example 3.** Dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ in the ratio $2 : 1$.

## 4. Quantitative

$$
Q = It, \qquad n(e^-) = \frac Q{96\,500}, \qquad n(\text{metal}) = \frac{n(e^-)}z.
$$

**Example 4.** $2.0\ \text{A}$ for $30\ \text{min}$ through $\text{CuSO}_4$: $Q = 3600\ \text{C}$, $n(e^-) = 0.0373$, $n(\text{Cu}) = 0.0187$, $m = 1.19\ \text{g}$; oxygen at the anode: $\dfrac{0.0373}4 = 0.00933\ \text{mol} = 0.209\ \text{L}$ at STP.

**Example 5.** Silver-plating a spoon: spoon = cathode, silver = anode, $\text{AgNO}_3$ electrolyte; $0.54\ \text{g}$ of Ag needs $n = 0.005\ \text{mol}$, $Q = 483\ \text{C}$, e.g. $0.5\ \text{A}$ for $966\ \text{s}$.

## 5. Industrial uses

**Chlor-alkali** (membrane cell): $2\text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2 + \text{Cl}_2$ (products in the ratio $2 : 1 : 1$); **aluminium** extraction; **copper refining** (anode mud holds Ag and Au); **electroplating** (the object is the cathode).

## Common mistakes

- Swapping cathode and anode.
- Forgetting water in aqueous solutions.
- Mixing up electrolytic cell and galvanic cell polarities.

## Practice problems

1. Products when molten $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ is electrolysed.
2. Moles of electrons for $965\ \text{C}$.

**Answers.** (1) Al (cathode), $\text{O}_2$ (anode). (2) $0.01\ \text{mol}$.
