## What you will learn

- the particle model of solids, liquids and gases;
- changes of state in terms of energy and forces;
- heating curves, diffusion and sublimation.

## 1. Particle model

- **Solid**: particles close together in fixed positions, vibrating; fixed shape and volume; not compressible.
- **Liquid**: particles close but free to move past each other; fixed volume, takes the shape of the container.
- **Gas**: particles far apart, moving fast and randomly; no fixed shape or volume; compressible; pressure from collisions with the walls.

## 2. Changes of state

Melting, freezing, boiling/evaporation, condensation, **sublimation** (solid to gas: iodine, dry ice) and deposition. During a change of state the **temperature stays constant** while the energy supplied (or released) changes the **potential energy** of the particles by overcoming (or forming) intermolecular forces.

**Example 1.** *Describe what happens to particles when ice melts.* Heating increases the vibration until the particles gain enough energy to break out of fixed positions; they can then move past each other, but remain in contact.

**Example 2.** *Explain the flat section of the heating curve at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for water.* The energy is used to overcome the hydrogen bonds between molecules (boiling), not to raise the average kinetic energy, so the temperature is constant.

**Example 3.** *Why can gases be compressed but solids cannot?* Gas particles are far apart; solid particles are touching.

## 3. Heating curves

On a plot of temperature against time (constant heating): sloping segments (single phase), plateaus at the melting and boiling points; the boiling plateau is longer than the melting plateau (more energy is needed to separate the particles completely). **Cooling curves** are similar in reverse. A pure substance has a **sharp** melting point; an impure one melts over a range and at a lower temperature.

## 4. Diffusion

Particles mix spontaneously by random motion. Diffusion is faster in gases than liquids, faster at higher temperatures, and faster for lighter particles (Graham's law: rate $\propto \dfrac1{\sqrt M}$): $\text{NH}_3$ ($M = 17$) diffuses faster than $\text{HCl}$ ($M = 36.5$), so in a glass tube the white $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ ring forms closer to the HCl end.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the temperature rises during melting.
- Saying molecules themselves expand when a substance expands.
- Confusing boiling with evaporation.

## Practice problems

1. What happens to the particles during sublimation?
2. Why does a pure substance have a sharp melting point?

**Answers.** (1) They gain enough energy to go directly from a lattice to separate gas particles. (2) All the particles are identical, so the same energy is needed to overcome all the bonds.
