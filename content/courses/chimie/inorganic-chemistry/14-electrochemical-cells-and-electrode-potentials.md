## What you will learn

- galvanic (primary) cells and the salt bridge;
- standard electrode potentials and cell EMF;
- electrolytic versus galvanic cells; practical cells.

## 1. Galvanic cells

A **galvanic (voltaic) cell** (原电池) turns the energy of a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy. Two half-cells are joined by a wire and a **salt bridge** (it completes the circuit with moving ions and keeps each half-cell neutral).

- **Anode** (negative): **oxidation**. **Cathode** (positive): **reduction**. Electrons flow in the wire from anode to cathode.
- **Daniell cell**: $\text{Zn}\,|\,\text{Zn}^{2+}\,\|\,\text{Cu}^{2+}\,|\,\text{Cu}$: anode $\text{Zn} \to \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$; cathode $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \text{Cu}$; overall $\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \to \text{Zn}^{2+} + \text{Cu}$.
- Chinese curriculum: the more reactive metal is the negative electrode and dissolves. For Zn/Cu in dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (no salt bridge): $\text{Zn} \to \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$ (negative), $2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \to \text{H}_2$ at the copper (positive).

## 2. Standard electrode potentials

$E^\circ$ is measured against the **standard hydrogen electrode** ($\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2$, $0.00\ \text{V}$) under standard conditions. A more positive $E^\circ$ means a stronger oxidising agent.

| Half-reaction | $E^\circ / \text{V}$ |
|---|---|
| $\text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \text{Zn}$ | $-0.76$ |
| $\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \text{Fe}$ | $-0.44$ |
| $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \text{Cu}$ | $+0.34$ |
| $\text{Ag}^+ + e^- \to \text{Ag}$ | $+0.80$ |
| $\text{Cl}_2 + 2e^- \to 2\text{Cl}^-$ | $+1.36$ |
| $\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5e^- \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | $+1.51$ |

$$
E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} > 0 \ \Rightarrow \ \text{the reaction is spontaneous}.
$$

**Do not multiply $E^\circ$ by the number of electrons.**

**Example 1.** Zn/Cu: $E^\circ = 0.34 - (-0.76) = +1.10\ \text{V}$.

**Example 2.** Will Cu reduce $\text{Ag}^+$? $E^\circ = 0.80 - 0.34 = +0.46\ \text{V} > 0$: yes, $\text{Cu} + 2\text{Ag}^+ \to \text{Cu}^{2+} + 2\text{Ag}$.

**Concentration effects (Nernst)**: $E = E^\circ - \dfrac{0.0592}n\log Q$ at $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

## 3. Electrolytic vs galvanic

| | Galvanic | Electrolytic |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | chemical to electrical | electrical to chemical |
| Anode sign | **negative** | **positive** |
| Cathode sign | positive | negative |
| Reaction | spontaneous | forced |

In both, oxidation is at the anode and reduction at the cathode.

## 4. Practical cells

Dry and alkaline cells; the **lead–acid** battery (rechargeable: $\text{Pb}$ and $\text{PbO}_2$ in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ give $\text{PbSO}_4$ on discharge); **fuel cells** ($\text{H}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ giving water, with no $\text{CO}_2$ at the point of use). To raise the current: more reactive electrodes, a more concentrated electrolyte, a larger electrode area.

**Example 3 (Faraday).** $2\ \text{A}$ for $965\ \text{s}$ deposits $0.64\ \text{g}$ of copper ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$, $M = 63.5$): $n(e^-) = 0.02$, $n(\text{Cu}) = 0.01$.

## Common mistakes

- Calling the anode of a galvanic cell positive.
- Doubling $E^\circ$ for a doubled equation.
- Reversing the subtraction in $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$.

## Practice problems

1. $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ for $\text{Zn}|\text{Zn}^{2+}\|\text{Ag}^+|\text{Ag}$.
2. Will $\text{Fe}$ reduce $\text{Cu}^{2+}$?

**Answers.** (1) $0.80 - (-0.76) = 1.56\ \text{V}$. (2) Yes: $0.34 - (-0.44) = +0.78\ \text{V}$.
