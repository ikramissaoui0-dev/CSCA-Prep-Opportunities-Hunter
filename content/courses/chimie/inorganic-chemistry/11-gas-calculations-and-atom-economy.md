## What you will learn

- molar volume of gases and gas stoichiometry;
- percentage yield and atom economy;
- the principles of green chemistry.

## 1. Gas calculations

At STP ($0\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $101\ \text{kPa}$): $V_m = 22.4\ \text{L/mol}$; at room temperature $\approx 24\ \text{L/mol}$. So $V = n \times V_m$. In general use $pV = nRT$.

**Example 1.** Volume of $\text{H}_2$ at STP when $4.8\ \text{g}$ of Mg reacts with excess acid: $n = 0.2$, $V = 4.48\ \text{L}$.

**Example 2.** Volumes of reacting gases at the same $T$ and $p$ are in the ratio of the coefficients: $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3$, $1 : 3 : 2$.

**Example 3.** Combustion of $10\ \text{mL}$ of $\text{CH}_4$: needs $20\ \text{mL}$ of $\text{O}_2$ and gives $10\ \text{mL}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ (the water is liquid).

## 2. Yield

$$
\text{percentage yield} = \frac{\text{actual mass}}{\text{theoretical mass}} \times 100\%.
$$

**Example 4.** A student expected $14\ \text{g}$ of product and obtained $9.9\ \text{g}$: $70.7\%$. Causes of a low yield: incomplete reaction, side reactions, loss during transfer and filtration, impure product.

## 3. Atom economy (原子利用率)

$$
\text{atom economy} = \frac{M_r(\text{desired product})}{\sum M_r(\text{all reactants})} \times 100\%.
$$

It is independent of the practical yield. **Addition and combination** reactions can have $100\%$; **substitution, elimination and decomposition** make by-products.

**Example 5.** $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$ with CaO wanted: $\dfrac{56}{100} = 56\%$.

**Example 6.** $\text{C} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CO} + \text{H}_2$ with $\text{H}_2$ wanted: $\dfrac2{30} = 6.7\%$.

**Example 7.** Which route to ethanol is greener? Hydration of ethene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$): $100\%$. From bromoethane ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Br} + \text{NaOH} \to \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaBr}$): $\dfrac{46}{149} = 31\%$.

**Example 8.** Iron: $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \to 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$: $\dfrac{112}{160 + 84} = 45.9\%$.

## 4. Green chemistry

Prevent waste; high atom economy; use safer solvents and reagents; energy efficiency (catalysts, ambient conditions); renewable feedstocks; design degradable products; real-time monitoring; recycle unreacted reagents.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing yield with atom economy.
- Dividing by the mass of one reactant only.
- Using $22.4\ \text{L}$ at room temperature.

## Practice problems

1. Volume of $\text{CO}_2$ (STP) from $10\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$ with excess acid.
2. Atom economy for making $\text{NH}_3$ from $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2$.

**Answers.** (1) $0.1\ \text{mol}$, $2.24\ \text{L}$. (2) $100\%$.
