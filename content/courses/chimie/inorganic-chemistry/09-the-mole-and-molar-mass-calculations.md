## What you will learn

- the mole, molar mass and Avogadro's constant;
- reacting masses and limiting reagents;
- empirical formulae and hydrates.

## 1. The mole

The **mole** (物质的量, $n$) contains $N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ particles (阿伏加德罗常数). The **molar mass** $M$ (摩尔质量, g/mol) equals $M_r$ numerically:

$$
n = \frac mM = \frac N{N_A} = \frac V{V_m} = cV.
$$

**Example 1.** $11\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$: $n = \dfrac{11}{44} = 0.25\ \text{mol}$ (that is $1.5 \times 10^{23}$ molecules).

## 2. Reacting masses

Convert mass to moles, apply the **mole ratio** from the equation, convert back.

**Example 2.** Mass of MgO from $4.8\ \text{g}$ of Mg: $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{MgO}$; $n(\text{Mg}) = 0.2$; $n(\text{MgO}) = 0.2$; $m = 0.2 \cdot 40 = 8.0\ \text{g}$.

**Example 3.** Limiting reagent: $2\ \text{g}$ of Mg and $50\ \text{mL}$ of $2.0\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl: $n(\text{Mg}) = 0.0823$ needs $0.165\ \text{mol}$ HCl, only $0.100\ \text{mol}$ is present: HCl limits; $\text{H}_2$ formed $= 0.050\ \text{mol}$.

## 3. Empirical and molecular formulae

Divide each mass (or %) by $A_r$, then by the smallest. **Example 4.** $40\%\ \text{C}$, $6.7\%\ \text{H}$, $53.3\%\ \text{O}$: $3.33 : 6.7 : 3.33 = 1 : 2 : 1$, so $\text{CH}_2\text{O}$; with $M_r = 180$, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$.

## 4. Hydrates (water of crystallisation)

Heating $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$ drives off water (blue $\to$ white). If $2.50\ \text{g}$ loses $0.90\ \text{g}$: $n(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 0.050$; $n(\text{CuSO}_4) = \dfrac{1.60}{160} = 0.010$; $x = 5$. Another: $\text{MgSO}_4\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with $M_r = 246$: $246 - 120 = 126 = 18x$, $x = 7$.

## 5. Purity and mixtures

**Percentage purity** $= \dfrac{\text{pure mass}}{\text{sample mass}} \times 100\%$. $10.0\ \text{g}$ of impure marble gives $2.0\ \text{L}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ at room temperature ($24.5\ \text{L/mol}$): $n = 0.0816$, $m(\text{CaCO}_3) = 8.16\ \text{g}$, purity $81.6\%$. For a mixture of two compounds, set up two unknowns and two equations (total mass and total product).

## Common mistakes

- Using the mass ratio instead of the mole ratio.
- Using $22.4\ \text{L}$ outside STP.
- Forgetting water of crystallisation in $M_r$.

## Practice problems

1. Moles in $34\ \text{g}$ of $\text{NH}_3$.
2. Mass of $\text{Fe}$ from $320\ \text{t}$ of pure $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$.

**Answers.** (1) $2\ \text{mol}$. (2) $224\ \text{t}$.
