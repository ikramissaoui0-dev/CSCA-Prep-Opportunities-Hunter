## What you will learn

- the physical methods for separating mixtures;
- how to choose the right method;
- step-by-step procedures and chromatography.

## 1. Choosing the method

| Mixture | Method | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Insoluble solid and liquid | **filtration** | particle size |
| Soluble solid from a solution | **evaporation** / **crystallisation** | solvent evaporates; cooling a hot saturated solution |
| Pure solvent from a solution | **simple distillation** | boiling-point difference |
| Miscible liquids | **fractional distillation** | different boiling points |
| Immiscible liquids | **separating funnel** (分液) | different density |
| Solute in two solvents | **solvent extraction** (萃取) | different solubility |
| Coloured substances | **chromatography** | different affinity for stationary and mobile phase |
| A solid that sublimes ($\text{I}_2$, $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$) | sublimation | sublimes on heating |
| A magnetic solid (Fe) | magnet | magnetism |

## 2. Procedures

**Pure salt from rock salt** (NaCl and sand): dissolve in water, filter off the sand, evaporate part of the filtrate, cool to crystallise (or evaporate to dryness), filter and dry. **Evaporative crystallisation** (蒸发结晶) suits NaCl (solubility barely changes with temperature); **cooling crystallisation** (冷却结晶) suits $\text{KNO}_3$ (much more soluble when hot).

**Distillation**: the thermometer bulb level with the **side arm**; add **boiling chips**; cooling water enters the condenser at the **lower** end and leaves at the top. Ethanol ($78\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and water ($100\ ^\circ\text{C}$) need **fractional** distillation (a fractionating column).

**Solvent extraction**: shake with an immiscible solvent in a separating funnel; the solute dissolves preferentially in the solvent in which it is more soluble. *Bromine from bromine water is extracted with $\text{CCl}_4$* (bromine is far more soluble in it; $\text{CCl}_4$ is denser than water and forms the lower layer).

**Chromatography**: a spot of mixture on the baseline of paper, solvent rising up. $R_f = \dfrac{\text{distance moved by the spot}}{\text{distance moved by the solvent front}}$ (a spot at $4.2\ \text{cm}$ with the front at $8.4\ \text{cm}$ gives $R_f = 0.50$). The same substance has the same $R_f$ in the same solvent; the baseline is in pencil.

**Example 1.** Separate a mixture of sand, salt and iodine: sublime the iodine; add water and filter the sand; evaporate the filtrate for salt.

**Example 2.** Separate iron filings and sulfur: use a magnet; or dissolve the sulfur in a suitable solvent.

**Example 3.** Obtain pure water from sea water: simple distillation.

## 3. Choosing

Ask: solid and liquid? use filtration. Dissolved solid? evaporate/crystallise. Two liquids? density (immiscible) or boiling point (miscible). Dyes? chromatography.

## Common mistakes

- Using simple distillation for liquids with close boiling points.
- Putting the thermometer in the liquid in distillation.
- Drawing the chromatography baseline in ink.

## Practice problems

1. How would you obtain pure dry NaCl from rock salt?
2. Calculate $R_f$ for a spot that moved $3.0\ \text{cm}$ when the solvent moved $7.5\ \text{cm}$.

**Answers.** (1) Dissolve, filter off sand, evaporate, crystallise, dry. (2) $0.40$.
