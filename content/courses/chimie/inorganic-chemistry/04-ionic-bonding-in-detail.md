## What you will learn

- the formation of ionic compounds in terms of electron transfer;
- the structure and properties of ionic lattices;
- lattice energy and what controls melting point.

## 1. Formation

Ionic bonding (离子键) is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in a **giant ionic lattice**. A metal atom transfers electrons to a non-metal atom; both obtain full outer shells.

**Describe the formation of MgO**: each Mg atom ($2, 8, 2$) loses two electrons to form $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ($2, 8$); each O atom ($2, 6$) gains two to form $\text{O}^{2-}$ ($2, 8$); the oppositely charged ions attract in a lattice. Show square brackets and charges in a dot-and-cross diagram (the Chinese 电子式 convention writes the electrons around the symbol and the charge outside the brackets).

**Formula**: balance the charges: $\text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^- \to \text{CaCl}_2$; $\text{Al}^{3+} + 3\text{Cl}^- \to \text{AlCl}_3$ (but this one is covalent in practice).

## 2. Structure and properties

In NaCl each $\text{Na}^+$ is surrounded by six $\text{Cl}^-$ and vice versa. Properties:

- **High melting and boiling points**: many strong ionic bonds to overcome.
- **Hard but brittle**: a blow shifts a layer so like charges face each other and repel.
- **Soluble in water** (many): water molecules surround the ions.
- **Conduct electricity only when molten or dissolved**: the ions are then free to move.

**Example 1.** *Why does NaCl conduct when dissolved but not as a solid?* In solution the ions are free to move and carry charge; in the solid they are fixed in the lattice.

## 3. Lattice energy and melting point

The **lattice energy** is the energy released when one mole of the ionic solid forms from gaseous ions (a large negative value). It is greater (more negative) for **higher ionic charges** and **smaller ions**. This explains why **MgO** ($2852\ ^\circ\text{C}$, ions $2+$ and $2-$) melts much higher than **NaCl** ($801\ ^\circ\text{C}$, ions $1+$ and $1-$). (Lattice energy is beyond the core CSCA syllabus, but the qualitative argument is useful.)

**Example 2.** Order the melting points of $\text{NaCl}$, $\text{MgO}$ and $\text{CO}_2$: $\text{CO}_2 < \text{NaCl} < \text{MgO}$.

**Example 3.** Compare $\text{NaF}$ and $\text{MgO}$: MgO has doubly charged ions, so a higher melting point.

## 4. Comparing bonding types

| | Ionic | Covalent (simple) | Metallic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Particles | ions | molecules | cations + electrons |
| Melting point | high | low | usually high |
| Conducts | molten/aqueous | no | solid and liquid |

## Common mistakes

- Drawing shared pairs in an ionic diagram.
- Forgetting charges or brackets.
- Saying ionic solids conduct.

## Practice problems

1. Formula of magnesium nitride ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{N}^{3-}$).
2. Why is an ionic crystal brittle?

**Answers.** (1) $\text{Mg}_3\text{N}_2$. (2) Layers shift so that like charges align and repel, cleaving the crystal.
