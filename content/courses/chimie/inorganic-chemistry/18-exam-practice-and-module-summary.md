## What you will learn

- how to answer structure-and-bonding, quantitative and element-chemistry questions;
- a summary of the formulas of the module;
- worked exam questions with answers.

## 1. Strategy

- **Structure to property**: identify the structure type, state what is overcome on melting, and whether charge carriers are mobile.
- **Titrations**: balanced equation $\to$ moles of the standard $\to$ ratio $\to$ concentration; remember dilution factors and units ($\text{mol/dm}^3 = \text{mol/L}$).
- **Enthalpy**: reversing a reaction flips the sign; use $\sum\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{reactants})$.
- **Cells**: $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}$; positive means spontaneous.
- **Elements**: learn each by its key reaction, industrial process and test.

## 2. Formula sheet

$$
c = \frac nV, \quad \Delta H = \sum\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{prod}) - \sum\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{react}), \quad E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cat}} - E^\circ_{\text{an}}, \quad \text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}, \quad K_{sp} = [\text{M}]^a[\text{X}]^b.
$$

$\text{yield} = \dfrac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}}$; $\text{atom economy} = \dfrac{M_r(\text{product})}{\sum M_r(\text{reactants})}$; $Q = It$; $n(e^-) = \dfrac Q{96\,500}$.

## 3. Worked questions

**Q1.** Compare the structure and properties of sodium chloride and diamond. *Answer*: NaCl — a giant ionic lattice; high melting point; brittle; conducts when molten or dissolved; soluble. Diamond — a giant covalent network of carbon atoms, each bonded to four others; very high melting point; extremely hard; does not conduct; insoluble.

**Q2.** $25.0\ \text{mL}$ of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ needs $20.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.200\ \text{mol/L}$ NaOH for neutralisation: concentration of the acid. *Answer*: $n(\text{NaOH}) = 0.00400$; $n(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) = 0.00200$; $c = 0.080\ \text{mol/L}$.

**Q3.** Use $\Delta H_f^\circ$: $\text{CO}_2 = -394$, $\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} = -286$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH(l)} = -278$ to find $\Delta H_c$ of ethanol. *Answer*: $[2(-394) + 3(-286)] - (-278) = -1368\ \text{kJ/mol}$.

**Q4.** Which has the higher melting point, MgO or NaF? *Answer*: MgO: ions of charge $2+$ and $2-$ give a much stronger lattice.

**Q5.** A buffer has $0.20\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ and $0.10\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$ ($\text{p}K_a = 4.76$): pH. *Answer*: $4.76 + \log0.5 = 4.46$.

**Q6.** What volume of $0.100\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{KMnO}_4$ reacts with $25.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.200\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{Fe}^{2+}$? *Answer*: $n(\text{Fe}^{2+}) = 0.00500$; ratio $1 : 5$ gives $0.00100\ \text{mol}$ $\text{MnO}_4^-$: $10.0\ \text{mL}$.

**Q7.** Describe how to use chromatography to identify an amino acid. *Answer*: spot the unknown and known amino acids on a pencil baseline, develop in a solvent, spray with a locating agent (ninhydrin), compare the $R_f$ values; a match in the same solvent identifies it.

**Q8.** Explain why graphite conducts but diamond does not. *Answer*: graphite has delocalised electrons between its layers; in diamond all four outer electrons are used in bonds.

## 4. Module checklist

Bonding types and properties; redox and oxidation states; separation methods; tests for gases and ions; mole, concentration and titration; gas volumes and atom economy; electrolysis; enthalpy and Hess's law; cells and $E^\circ$; acids, bases and buffers; salts and $K_{sp}$; the chemistry of Na, Al, Fe, Cl, S, N, Si.

## Common mistakes

- Not converting units.
- Incomplete bonding answers (say what is overcome, and state the type of particle).
- Mixing up the cathode and anode in cells.

## Practice problem

Calculate the atom economy for making $\text{Fe}$ from $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{112}{160 + 84} = 45.9\%$.
