## What you will learn

- three definitions of acids and bases;
- pH of strong and weak acids and $K_a$;
- buffer solutions and salt hydrolysis.

## 1. Definitions

- **Arrhenius**: acids give $\text{H}^+$, bases give $\text{OH}^-$ in water.
- **Brønsted–Lowry**: acids are proton donors, bases are proton acceptors; conjugate pairs ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}/\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$, $\text{NH}_4^+/\text{NH}_3$).
- **Lewis**: acids accept an electron pair, bases donate one ($\text{BF}_3 + \text{NH}_3$).

**Strong** acids and bases ionise completely; **weak** ones only partially. Electrolytes (电解质) are strong or weak by degree of ionisation; sugar and ethanol are non-electrolytes.

## 2. pH

$\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}^+]$; $\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$ at $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$. **Example 1.** $0.05\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$: $[\text{OH}^-] = 0.10$, $\text{pOH} = 1$, $\text{pH} = 13$. **Example 2.** $25\ \text{mL}$ of $0.1\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$ ($0.005\ \text{mol}$ $\text{OH}^-$) is neutralised by $25\ \text{mL}$ of $0.2\ \text{mol/L}$ HCl.

For a weak acid $\text{HA} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{A}^-$:

$$
K_a = \frac{[\text{H}^+][\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}, \qquad [\text{H}^+] \approx \sqrt{K_ac}.
$$

**Example 3.** $0.10\ \text{mol/L}$ ethanoic acid ($K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$): $[\text{H}^+] \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-3}$, $\text{pH} = 2.9$.

## 3. Buffers

A **buffer** resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added. It contains a **weak acid and its conjugate base** (e.g. $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}/\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$) or a weak base and its salt.

$$
\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]} \quad (\text{Henderson–Hasselbalch}).
$$

Added $\text{H}^+$ is removed by $\text{A}^-$ ($\text{A}^- + \text{H}^+ \to \text{HA}$); added $\text{OH}^-$ by $\text{HA}$ ($\text{HA} + \text{OH}^- \to \text{A}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}$). A buffer works best at $\text{pH} \approx \text{p}K_a \pm 1$ and has a finite capacity. Blood is buffered at $7.4$ by $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3/\text{HCO}_3^-$.

**Example 4.** Equal concentrations of $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ and $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$ ($\text{p}K_a = 4.76$): $\text{pH} = 4.76$. With $0.20$ and $0.10\ \text{mol/L}$: $\text{pH} = 4.76 + \log\dfrac{0.10}{0.20} = 4.46$.

## 4. Salt hydrolysis (盐类水解)

"The strong one decides": salt of a strong acid and strong base ($\text{NaCl}$): neutral; weak acid + strong base ($\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$, $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$): alkaline ($\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{OH}^-$); strong acid + weak base ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$, $\text{CuSO}_4$): acidic.

**Conductivity**: adding $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$ to $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ lowers the conductivity to nearly zero at neutralisation ($\text{BaSO}_4$ precipitates, water forms), then it rises again.

## Common mistakes

- Using $\text{pH} = -\log c$ for a weak acid.
- Treating a buffer capacity as unlimited.
- Forgetting that salts can be acidic or alkaline.

## Practice problems

1. pH of a buffer with $0.1\ \text{mol/L}$ of each of $\text{HA}$ ($\text{p}K_a = 5$) and $\text{A}^-$.
2. Is a solution of $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ acidic, neutral or alkaline?

**Answers.** (1) $5$. (2) Alkaline.
