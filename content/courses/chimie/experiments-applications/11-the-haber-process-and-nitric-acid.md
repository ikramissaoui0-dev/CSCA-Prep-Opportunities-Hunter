## What you will learn

- the Haber process for ammonia and the reasons for its conditions;
- the Ostwald process for nitric acid;
- equilibrium and mass calculations.

## 1. The Haber process

$$
\text{N}_2(g) + 3\text{H}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3(g), \qquad \Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ/mol}.
$$

- **Sources**: $\text{N}_2$ from the air; $\text{H}_2$ from natural gas by steam reforming ($\text{CH}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CO} + 3\text{H}_2$).
- **Conditions**: about $400$–$450\ ^\circ\text{C}$, $150$–$250\ \text{atm}$, **iron catalyst**.
- **Why**: *high pressure* shifts the equilibrium toward the side with fewer gas moles ($4 \to 2$), but costs more and needs stronger equipment; *low temperature* favours the exothermic forward reaction but is too slow, so a compromise temperature is used; the **catalyst** speeds up the approach to equilibrium without changing the percentage yield; $\text{NH}_3$ is **condensed and removed**, and unreacted $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{H}_2$ are **recycled**. The single-pass conversion is only about $15$–$20\%$.
- **Uses**: fertilisers (about $80\%$), nitric acid, explosives, plastics, refrigerants.

**Example 1.** *Why does the iron catalyst not change the percentage of $\text{NH}_3$ at equilibrium?* It speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so it does not alter the position of equilibrium, only the time to reach it.

**Example 2.** $2\ \text{mol}$ $\text{N}_2$ and $6\ \text{mol}$ $\text{H}_2$ reach equilibrium after $1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{N}_2$ has reacted: $\text{N}_2$ $1\ \text{mol}$, $\text{H}_2$ $3\ \text{mol}$, $\text{NH}_3$ $2\ \text{mol}$; total gas falls from $8$ to $6\ \text{mol}$, so the pressure drops to $\tfrac34$ (constant $V$ and $T$); $\text{NH}_3$ is $\tfrac26 = 33\%$ of the mixture; conversion of $\text{N}_2$ is $\tfrac12 = 50\%$ (divide by what you started with, not by the total gas).

## 2. The Ostwald process

1. **Catalytic oxidation**: $4\text{NH}_3 + 5\text{O}_2 \to 4\text{NO} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Pt/Rh gauze, about $800\ ^\circ\text{C}$).
2. $2\text{NO} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{NO}_2$.
3. **Absorption** in water: $3\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{HNO}_3 + \text{NO}$ (the NO is recycled), or $4\text{NO}_2 + \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{HNO}_3$.

Overall, with recycling of NO, $\text{NH}_3 \to \text{HNO}_3$ is $1 : 1$. **Example 3.** $51\ \text{t}$ of $\text{NH}_3$ ($M = 17$) is $3 \times 10^6\ \text{mol}$ and gives $3 \times 10^6\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{HNO}_3$, i.e. $189\ \text{t}$.

Uses of nitric acid: ammonium nitrate fertiliser, explosives (TNT), dyes. It is a strong **oxidising acid**: with copper, dilute acid gives $\text{NO}$, concentrated gives $\text{NO}_2$.

## 3. Environmental notes

Excess fertiliser runoff causes **eutrophication** (algae bloom, oxygen depletion); nitrogen oxides contribute to acid rain; high energy use in the Haber process.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the catalyst increases the yield.
- Dividing by the total gas rather than the starting amount for conversion.
- Forgetting that NO is recycled in the Ostwald process.

## Practice problems

1. Name the catalysts in the Haber and Ostwald processes.
2. Why is a pressure of about $200\ \text{atm}$ used?

**Answers.** (1) Iron; platinum–rhodium. (2) It raises the yield by shifting the equilibrium toward fewer gas moles, as a compromise with cost and safety.
