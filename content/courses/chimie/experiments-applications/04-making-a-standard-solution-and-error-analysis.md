## What you will learn

- the steps for preparing a solution of exact concentration;
- dilution with a pipette and volumetric flask;
- how each mistake changes the concentration (error analysis).

## 1. Preparing a standard solution

A **standard solution** has an accurately known concentration. To make $250.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.100\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$:

1. **Calculate** (计算): $m = c \cdot V \cdot M = 0.100 \cdot 0.2500 \cdot 106 = 2.65\ \text{g}$.
2. **Weigh** (称量) the solid accurately in a weighing bottle (by difference).
3. **Dissolve** (溶解) in a beaker with less than $250\ \text{mL}$ of distilled water; stir with a glass rod. If the process gives off heat, **let the solution cool to room temperature**.
4. **Transfer** (转移) into the volumetric flask with the help of the glass rod and funnel; **rinse** (洗涤) the beaker, rod and funnel into the flask (the **washings**).
5. **Make up to the mark** (定容): add water until about $1\ \text{cm}$ below the mark, then use a dropper so that the **bottom of the meniscus touches the mark**.
6. **Stopper** and **invert** several times to mix.

**Example 1.** Mass of NaOH for $500\ \text{mL}$ of $0.20\ \text{mol/L}$: $0.5 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 40 = 4.0\ \text{g}$ (NaOH absorbs water and $\text{CO}_2$, so it is not a primary standard; its concentration is found by titration).

## 2. Dilution

$c_1V_1 = c_2V_2$. To dilute concentrated $18\ \text{mol/L}$ $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ to $500\ \text{mL}$ of $2\ \text{mol/L}$: $V_1 = \dfrac{500 \cdot 2}{18} = 55.6\ \text{mL}$; measure with a measuring cylinder (or pipette for exact volumes), **add the acid slowly to water**, cool, then transfer to the flask. For exact dilution use a pipette and a volumetric flask.

## 3. Error analysis

Effect on the concentration of the prepared solution:

| Mistake | Effect on $c$ |
|---|---|
| Not transferring the washings | **too low** (some solute lost) |
| Water added **above** the mark | **too low** (volume too large) |
| Looking up when making up to the mark | volume too large: **too low** |
| Looking down when making up to the mark | volume too small: **too high** |
| Transferring a **warm** solution and making up to the mark | when it cools the volume shrinks, so $c$ is **too high** |
| Flask wet with distilled water before use | no effect |
| Weighing with the balance reading too high | amount of solute too large: $c$ too high |
| Solute left in the weighing bottle | too low |

Also: if after mixing the level falls slightly below the mark, **do not add more water** (it would dilute the solution).

**Example 2.** A student transfers a warm NaOH solution straight into the flask and makes it up to the mark: on cooling the solution contracts below the mark, so the true volume is smaller and the concentration is **too high**.

**Example 3.** *If the washings are not transferred*, the amount of solute is lower than calculated, so $c$ is too low; a titration with this standard would give a calculated concentration of the unknown that is too high (the titre of the standard would be too large).

## 4. Random and systematic errors

**Systematic** errors (consistent bias): uncalibrated balance, impure reagent, wrong indicator. **Random** errors: parallax, end-point judgement; reduced by repeating and averaging. **Percentage uncertainty** $= \dfrac{\text{absolute uncertainty}}{\text{measurement}} \times 100\%$.

## Common mistakes

- Making up the solution in a beaker.
- Adding the solute directly to the volumetric flask.
- Not mixing thoroughly.

## Practice problems

1. Mass of $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ for $250\ \text{mL}$ of $0.200\ \text{mol/L}$.
2. Effect of water above the mark on $c$.

**Answers.** (1) $5.30\ \text{g}$. (2) Concentration too low.
