## What you will learn

- how to choose the collection method from density and solubility;
- drying agents and washing bottles ("long in, short out");
- absorbing and handling tail gases safely.

## 1. Collection methods

- **Over water** (displacement of water): for gases that are **insoluble or slightly soluble** in water: $\text{H}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{NO}$, $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$, $\text{CO}$. Gives a gas that is not dry but pure.
- **Upward delivery** (downward displacement of air): for gases **denser than air** ($M > 29$): $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{NO}_2$, $\text{HCl}$.
- **Downward delivery** (upward displacement of air): for gases **lighter than air**: $\text{H}_2$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{CH}_4$.

*Collecting $\text{SO}_2$ ($M = 64$, fairly soluble in water)*: upward delivery, not over water. $\text{NO}$ reacts with air, so it must be collected over water. $\text{Cl}_2$ can also be collected over saturated brine (very little dissolves).

## 2. Two-hole bottles

For **washing or drying** a gas the gas enters the **long tube** (it bubbles through the liquid) and leaves through the short tube: "**long in, short out**". For **collecting in an empty bottle by air displacement**: a dense gas enters the long tube (reaching the bottom and pushing air out of the short tube); a light gas enters the short tube (collecting at the top and pushing air out of the long tube) — "short in, long out" for light gases.

## 3. Drying and purifying

| Gas | Drying agent |
|---|---|
| $\text{H}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{HCl}$ | concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ |
| $\text{NH}_3$ | $\text{CaO}$ or soda lime (never $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{CaCl}_2$) |

An **acidic** gas needs an acidic or neutral drying agent; an **alkaline** gas needs an alkaline one.

**Order for dry, pure $\text{CO}_2$ from marble and HCl**: (1) saturated $\text{NaHCO}_3$ solution (removes HCl; it does not absorb much $\text{CO}_2$); (2) concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (removes water vapour); (3) collect by upward delivery. **Drying must come last**, because the gas picks up water in each aqueous wash. For $\text{Cl}_2$: first water or saturated brine (removes HCl), then conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

## 4. Tail-gas treatment

Toxic acidic gases ($\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{HCl}$, $\text{NO}_2$) are absorbed by **NaOH solution**: $\text{SO}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$; $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{NaCl} + \text{NaClO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. $\text{CO}$ and $\text{H}_2$ are burned or collected in a bag. Very soluble gases ($\text{NH}_3$, $\text{HCl}$) need a **suck-back** guard: an inverted funnel just touching the surface of the water, or an empty buffer bottle.

**Example 1.** An empty two-tube bottle is used to collect $\text{H}_2$: hydrogen is lighter than air, so it enters through the short tube and air leaves by the long tube.

**Example 2.** Collect $\text{NH}_3$: downward delivery with a plug of cotton wool at the mouth; dry with $\text{CaO}$.

## Common mistakes

- Collecting $\text{NH}_3$ over water or by upward delivery.
- Drying $\text{NH}_3$ with concentrated sulfuric acid.
- Drying the gas before the washing step.

## Practice problems

1. Collection method for $\text{CO}_2$ and for $\text{NO}$.
2. How to absorb the tail gas from $\text{Cl}_2$ preparation.

**Answers.** (1) Upward delivery; over water. (2) Pass it into NaOH solution.
