## What you will learn

- the blast furnace and the reactions in it;
- the Hall–Héroult electrolysis of alumina;
- calculations and common questions.

## 1. Extraction of iron: the blast furnace

Raw materials: **iron ore** ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, haematite), **coke** (carbon), **limestone** ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and **hot air**.

1. Coke burns: $\text{C} + \text{O}_2 \to \text{CO}_2$ (heat).
2. $\text{CO}_2 + \text{C} \to 2\text{CO}$.
3. **Reduction**: $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \to 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$. **CO is the main reducing agent** (not coke itself).
4. **Slag**: limestone decomposes, $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$, and $\text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \to \text{CaSiO}_3$ (slag floats on the molten iron).

Molten iron (**pig iron**, about $4\%$ carbon, brittle) is tapped from the bottom; it is made into **steel** by removing carbon and impurities and adding other metals.

**Example 1.** $320\ \text{t}$ of pure $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ ($M = 160$) is $2 \times 10^6\ \text{mol}$ and gives $4 \times 10^6\ \text{mol}$ of Fe, i.e. $224\ \text{t}$.

**Example 2.** *Why is limestone added?* It decomposes to CaO, which combines with acidic $\text{SiO}_2$ impurities to form slag; the $\text{CO}_2$ formed is reduced to CO.

## 2. Extraction of aluminium

Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ **cannot be reduced by carbon**: the oxide is **electrolysed**.

- **Purification**: bauxite is treated with NaOH to give pure alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$).
- **Electrolysis (Hall–Héroult)**: alumina is dissolved in **molten cryolite** ($\text{Na}_3\text{AlF}_6$), which lowers the melting point from over $2000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to about $950\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (saving energy) and improves conductivity. Carbon electrodes.
- **Cathode**: $\text{Al}^{3+} + 3e^- \to \text{Al}$ (molten aluminium collects at the bottom).
- **Anode**: $2\text{O}^{2-} \to \text{O}_2 + 4e^-$; the oxygen **burns the carbon anodes** to $\text{CO}_2$, so they must be replaced regularly.

**Cryolite is a solvent, not a reactant.** The process uses a very large amount of electricity (about $13$–$15\ \text{MWh}$ per tonne), so **recycling** aluminium (saves about $95\%$ of the energy) is important. Producing $1\ \text{t}$ of Al needs $\dfrac{10^6}{27} \cdot 3 \approx 1.1 \times 10^5\ \text{mol}$ of electrons.

**Example 3.** *Why is aluminium not extracted by heating $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ with carbon as iron is?* Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot take the oxygen from it.

## 3. Comparison

| | Iron | Aluminium |
|---|---|---|
| Method | reduction by CO in a furnace | electrolysis of the molten oxide |
| Reason | Fe is below C in the reactivity series | Al is above C |
| Energy | heat from coke | electricity |

## Common mistakes

- Calling coke the main reducing agent.
- Treating cryolite as a reactant.
- Forgetting that the carbon anodes are consumed.

## Practice problems

1. Equation of the main reduction in the blast furnace.
2. What happens at the anode in aluminium extraction?

**Answers.** (1) $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \to 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$. (2) $\text{O}^{2-}$ is oxidised to $\text{O}_2$, which reacts with the carbon anode.
