## What you will learn

- how to read the meniscus correctly;
- how to read a burette and calculate a titre;
- the effect of reading errors (looking down or up).

## 1. The meniscus

The surface of a liquid in a glass tube is curved. For water-like liquids it curves **downward** (a concave meniscus): read the **lowest point** of the curve with your **eye level** with it. For mercury the meniscus is convex: read the top.

Eye above the level is "looking down" (俯视); eye below it is "looking up" (仰视). The effect depends on where the zero of the scale is:

| Instrument | Looking down (俯视) | Looking up (仰视) |
|---|---|---|
| Measuring cylinder (zero at the bottom) | reading **too high** | reading **too low** |
| Burette (zero at the top) | reading **too low** | reading **too high** |

The simplest rule: always put your eye level with the bottom of the meniscus.

## 2. The burette

- It reads from $0$ at the **top** to $50$ at the bottom: **volume delivered = final reading $-$ initial reading**. Initial $3.20\ \text{mL}$, final $23.45\ \text{mL}$: titre $20.25\ \text{mL}$.
- Reading to **$\pm 0.05\ \text{mL}$**: two decimal places, the last digit $0$ or $5$.
- Before use: rinse with the solution; **fill the tip** (no air bubble); remove the funnel; adjust to near zero and record the initial reading.
- Near the end point add dropwise and wash the flask walls with distilled water.

**Example 1.** A burette reads $0.00$ at the start and $21.40$ at the end: titre $21.40\ \text{mL}$.

**Example 2.** Initial $0.80\ \text{mL}$, final $21.15\ \text{mL}$: titre $20.35\ \text{mL}$.

## 3. Titre and repeating

Do a rough titration, then repeat until **two or more concordant titres** (within $0.10\ \text{mL}$); use the mean of the concordant results, never an outlier.

## 4. Effect of errors on the titre

| Error | Titre |
|---|---|
| Final reading taken looking **up** | too **large** |
| Final reading taken looking **down** | too **small** |
| Initial reading taken looking **down** | too **large** (the initial value is read too low) |
| Air bubble in the tip that disappears during the titration | too **large** |
| Overshooting the end point | too **large** |
| Rinsing the conical flask with the solution | too **large** (more moles in the flask) |

**Example 3.** In a titration the initial reading is taken correctly but the final reading is taken looking up: the final value is too high, so the titre and the calculated concentration of the unknown are too high.

**Example 4.** A student pours liquid into a measuring cylinder until the scale reads $30\ \text{mL}$ **looking down**: the reading is too high, so the true volume is **less** than $30\ \text{mL}$.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the top of the meniscus.
- Reading a burette to $0.1\ \text{mL}$ only.
- Forgetting to remove the funnel before the initial reading.

## Practice problems

1. Initial $1.05\ \text{mL}$, final $22.30\ \text{mL}$: the titre.
2. Why must the burette tip be filled before the initial reading?

**Answers.** (1) $21.25\ \text{mL}$. (2) An air bubble would be expelled during the titration and be counted as delivered liquid, making the titre too large.
