## What you will learn

- the four principles for removing an impurity;
- common reagent choices;
- the order of additions in salt purification.

## 1. Principles

When removing a small impurity from a substance:

1. **Add no new impurity** (the reagent and the by-products must not contaminate the product).
2. **Do not lose the wanted substance.**
3. Make the **separation easy** (the impurity should become a gas, a precipitate or a different phase).
4. Make the **recovery easy** (the product should be easy to isolate).

An **excess** of reagent is often used to remove all of the impurity; the **excess must then be removed** too.

## 2. Common examples

| Main substance | Impurity | Reagent / method |
|---|---|---|
| NaCl solution | $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ | add $\text{BaCl}_2$, filter |
| $\text{CO}_2$ | HCl | pass through saturated $\text{NaHCO}_3$ solution |
| $\text{CO}_2$ | $\text{CO}$ | pass over hot $\text{CuO}$ |
| $\text{CO}$ | $\text{CO}_2$ | pass through NaOH solution |
| $\text{Cu}$ | $\text{Fe}$ | add dilute acid, filter |
| $\text{CaO}$ | $\text{CaCO}_3$ | heat strongly |
| $\text{Cl}_2$ | HCl | pass through saturated brine |
| $\text{KNO}_3$ solution | $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$ | add exactly $\text{Ba(NO}_3)_2$, filter |

**Example 1.** *Remove a little HCl from $\text{CO}_2$*: saturated $\text{NaHCO}_3$ solution reacts with HCl but does not absorb $\text{CO}_2$ (NaOH would absorb the $\text{CO}_2$ too).

**Example 2.** *Purify $\text{KNO}_3$ solution containing a little $\text{K}_2\text{SO}_4$*: add exactly the required volume of $\text{Ba(NO}_3)_2$ solution and filter off $\text{BaSO}_4$: no new impurity enters (using $\text{BaCl}_2$ would add chloride).

## 3. Purifying crude salt

Crude salt contains $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$. Add, in a suitable order: excess $\text{BaCl}_2$ (removes sulfate), excess NaOH (removes magnesium), excess $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ (removes calcium **and** the excess barium), then **filter** off all precipitates, then add dilute HCl until neutral (removes excess $\text{OH}^-$ and $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$), and evaporate.

Rules for the order: $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ must come **after** $\text{BaCl}_2$ (to remove the excess $\text{Ba}^{2+}$); HCl must come **after filtering** (otherwise it would redissolve $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$, $\text{CaCO}_3$ and $\text{BaCO}_3$); NaOH can come anywhere before the filtration.

## 4. Gas purification

Remove acidic impurities with alkali or saturated bicarbonate; remove water last with a suitable drying agent; remove oxygen by passing over heated copper.

## Common mistakes

- Using a reagent that introduces a new impurity.
- Adding HCl before filtering.
- Using NaOH to remove HCl from $\text{CO}_2$.

## Practice problems

1. Remove $\text{Fe}$ filings from copper powder.
2. Purify $\text{CO}$ containing a little $\text{CO}_2$.

**Answers.** (1) Add dilute HCl (or use a magnet) and filter. (2) Pass through NaOH solution.
