## What you will learn

- the rules for heating solids and liquids safely;
- how to handle gases (hydrogen, toxic gases) and fires;
- first aid and waste.

## 1. General rules

Wear safety goggles and a lab coat; tie back long hair; no eating or drinking; read the hazard labels; never taste chemicals; smell by wafting; work in a fume cupboard with toxic gases ($\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{NO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{SO}_2$).

## 2. Heating

- **Use the outer (hottest) part of the flame**; the blue flame (air hole open) is the hottest; a safety flame (yellow, air hole closed) is used when not heating.
- **Heating a liquid in a test tube**: no more than $\tfrac13$ full, tilted at about $45^\circ$, mouth pointed away from everyone, moved gently so that it heats evenly.
- **Heating a solid that gives off water or a gas**: tilt the mouth **slightly downward** so that condensed water cannot run back onto the hot glass and crack it.
- **Flammable liquids** (ethanol, ether): never heat directly; use a **water bath** or hot plate.
- Add **boiling chips** to a liquid being boiled to prevent bumping; use **reflux** to prevent loss of volatile liquids.
- Heat a test tube evenly first, then concentrate the flame at the bottom; **never point at anyone** and never heat a closed vessel.

## 3. Collecting gas over water from a heated tube

At the end **remove the delivery tube from the water before extinguishing the flame**; otherwise water is sucked back into the hot tube (suck-back) and cracks it.

## 4. Hydrogen and other flammable gases

Test for **purity (验纯)** before igniting or heating anything in it: collect a test tube of gas and bring it to a flame: a quiet "pop" means pure; a sharp bang means air is mixed in. When reducing CuO with $\text{H}_2$ or CO: pass the gas for a while **first** to drive out the air; heat; afterwards stop heating first and keep passing gas until the tube cools (to stop hot copper re-oxidising).

## 5. Fires and spills

- **Alkali metals (Na, K)**: cover with dry sand; **never water or a $\text{CO}_2$ extinguisher** (they react with both). *If a small piece of potassium catches fire and a student reaches for a $\text{CO}_2$ extinguisher, that is wrong.*
- **Oil and ethanol fires**: smother with a damp cloth or sand.
- **Acid or alkali on skin**: rinse with plenty of water for several minutes; then dilute sodium hydrogencarbonate for acid or dilute boric acid for alkali; eyes: flush with water and seek help.
- **Mercury**: cover spilt mercury with sulfur powder.
- **Dilution of sulfuric acid**: add the **acid slowly to water**, stirring (never water to acid: the dilution is exothermic and the water would boil and spit).

## 6. Waste

Pour waste into the correct containers; do not return unused reagent to the stock bottle; do not pour organic solvents or heavy-metal solutions down the sink.

## Common mistakes

- Heating a flammable liquid directly with a flame.
- Igniting hydrogen without testing for purity.
- Extinguishing the burner before removing the delivery tube.

## Practice problems

1. Why must the acid be added to the water, and not the water to the acid, when diluting concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$?
2. Why tilt the mouth of a tube downward when heating a hydrated salt?

**Answers.** (1) The dilution releases a lot of heat; adding water to the acid would make it spit. (2) So that condensed water cannot run back and crack the hot glass.
