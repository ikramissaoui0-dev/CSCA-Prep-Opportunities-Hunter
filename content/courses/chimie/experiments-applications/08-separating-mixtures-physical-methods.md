## What you will learn

- the physical separation methods and the correct set-up;
- crystallisation, distillation, extraction and chromatography;
- worked separation problems.

## 1. Methods and principles

| Mixture | Method | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Insoluble solid + liquid | **filtration** | particle size |
| Soluble solid from solution | **evaporative** or **cooling crystallisation** | solvent evaporates / solubility falls on cooling |
| Pure liquid from a solution | **simple distillation** | boiling-point difference |
| Miscible liquids | **fractional distillation** | different boiling points |
| Immiscible liquids | **separating funnel** (分液) | different density |
| Solute in two solvents | **solvent extraction** (萃取) | solubility |
| Dyes and pigments | **chromatography** | affinity for stationary/mobile phase |
| Sublimable solid | sublimation | $\text{I}_2$, $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$, dry ice |
| Magnetic solid | magnet | iron |

## 2. Crystallisation

**Evaporative crystallisation** (蒸发结晶) suits NaCl (solubility barely changes with temperature): evaporate until crystals begin to appear, then stop heating and let the rest evaporate by residual heat. **Cooling crystallisation** (冷却结晶) suits $\text{KNO}_3$ (much more soluble when hot): make a hot saturated solution and cool it. Filter, wash with a little cold distilled water and dry.

**Example 1.** *Obtain pure dry NaCl from rock salt (NaCl + sand)*: dissolve in water, filter off the sand, evaporate the filtrate, crystallise, filter, dry.

## 3. Distillation

- **Simple distillation**: the thermometer bulb **level with the side arm** (measuring the vapour); add **boiling chips**; cooling water enters the condenser at the **lower** end and leaves at the top; fill the flask no more than $\tfrac23$.
- **Fractional distillation** with a fractionating column separates liquids with close boiling points: ethanol ($78\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and water ($100\ ^\circ\text{C}$).

**Example 2.** Pure water from sea water: simple distillation.

## 4. Extraction and separation

**Example 3.** *Extract bromine from bromine water*: shake with an immiscible solvent such as $\text{CCl}_4$ in a separating funnel: bromine is much more soluble in the organic layer (the lower layer, since $\text{CCl}_4$ is denser than water). Open the stopper, run off the lower layer through the tap, pour the upper layer out of the top.

**Chromatography**: a pencil baseline, spots of the samples, solvent below the baseline. $R_f = \dfrac{\text{distance moved by the spot}}{\text{distance moved by the solvent front}}$; the same substance has the same $R_f$ in the same solvent. A spot at $4.2\ \text{cm}$ with the front at $8.4\ \text{cm}$: $R_f = 0.50$. For colourless substances (amino acids) spray with a locating agent such as ninhydrin.

**Example 4.** Separate sand, salt and iodine: sublime the iodine; dissolve the rest in water; filter the sand; evaporate for the salt.

## Common mistakes

- Using simple distillation for liquids with close boiling points.
- Putting the thermometer in the liquid.
- Drawing the baseline in ink.

## Practice problems

1. How would you separate ethanol from water?
2. Calculate $R_f$ for a spot that moved $3.0\ \text{cm}$ when the solvent front moved $7.5\ \text{cm}$.

**Answers.** (1) Fractional distillation. (2) $0.40$.
