minutes: 10

## What you will learn

- the tests for the common gases;
- how to distinguish gases that give similar results;
- strategies for puzzle questions.

## 1. Tests

| Gas | Test and result |
|---|---|
| $\text{H}_2$ | lighted splint: squeaky "pop" (burns with a pale-blue flame producing only water) |
| $\text{O}_2$ | glowing splint relights |
| $\text{CO}_2$ | limewater turns milky ($\text{CaCO}_3$); clears with excess |
| $\text{NH}_3$ | damp red litmus turns blue; white smoke with conc. HCl |
| $\text{Cl}_2$ | yellow-green; damp blue litmus turns red then bleaches; turns starch–iodide paper blue-black |
| $\text{SO}_2$ | choking smell; decolourises acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$, bromine water or magenta solution |
| $\text{HCl}$ | misty fumes in moist air; white smoke with $\text{NH}_3$ |
| $\text{NO}_2$ | brown gas; $\text{NO}$ turns brown on contact with air |
| $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ | rotten-egg smell; blackens lead(II) ethanoate paper |
| Water vapour | turns anhydrous $\text{CuSO}_4$ from white to blue |

A **burning splint does not confirm $\text{CO}_2$**; use limewater. Test for **water vapour first** before passing a gas through any aqueous solution.

## 2. Distinguishing similar gases

- **$\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{SO}_2$** both turn limewater milky: $\text{SO}_2$ also decolourises $\text{KMnO}_4$ or bromine water and has a choking smell; $\text{CO}_2$ has no smell and does not.
- **$\text{H}_2$, $\text{CO}$ and $\text{CH}_4$** all burn with a blue flame: the products differ — $\text{H}_2$ gives only water; $\text{CO}$ gives $\text{CO}_2$ only (limewater); $\text{CH}_4$ gives both water and $\text{CO}_2$.
- **$\text{O}_2$, $\text{N}_2$, $\text{CO}_2$**: a glowing splint relights only in $\text{O}_2$; limewater distinguishes $\text{CO}_2$; $\text{N}_2$ does neither.
- **$\text{Cl}_2$ and $\text{HCl}$**: $\text{Cl}_2$ bleaches damp litmus; $\text{HCl}$ only turns it red.

## 3. Puzzle questions

**Example 1.** A colourless gas with a choking smell turns limewater milky: $\text{SO}_2$ (add acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ to confirm; it decolourises).

**Example 2.** A gas burns with a blue flame: $\text{H}_2$, $\text{CO}$ or $\text{CH}_4$: identify by the products.

**Example 3.** Give two independent tests for $\text{NH}_3$: damp red litmus turns blue; white smoke with a rod dipped in conc. HCl.

**Example 4.** A gas relights a glowing splint, then limewater is unaffected: $\text{O}_2$.

**Example 5.** A gas that turns brown on contact with air: $\text{NO}$.

## Common mistakes

- Concluding $\text{CO}_2$ from a splint test.
- Concluding $\text{CO}_2$ from limewater when $\text{SO}_2$ could be present.
- Forgetting to use *damp* litmus.

## Practice problems

1. How to distinguish $\text{CO}_2$ from $\text{SO}_2$.
2. How to show the product of burning hydrogen.

**Answers.** (1) $\text{SO}_2$ decolourises acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$; $\text{CO}_2$ does not. (2) It turns anhydrous $\text{CuSO}_4$ from white to blue (water).
