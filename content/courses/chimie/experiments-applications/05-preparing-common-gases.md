## What you will learn

- how to choose a generator from the reactants and conditions;
- the laboratory preparation of $\text{O}_2$, $\text{H}_2$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{SO}_2$;
- why certain reagents are chosen.

## 1. Choosing the generator

The type of apparatus follows from the **state of the reactants** and **whether heating is needed**:

- **solid + liquid, no heating**: a flask with a dropping funnel (or a Kipp's apparatus for $\text{H}_2$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$);
- **solid + solid, heating**: a test tube with its mouth **slightly downward** (so condensed water cannot run back onto hot glass);
- **solid + liquid, heating**: a flask with a delivery tube (e.g. $\text{Cl}_2$).

## 2. The gases

| Gas | Preparation |
|---|---|
| $\text{O}_2$ | $2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{MnO}_2} 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2$ (solid + liquid, no heat); or $2\text{KClO}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{MnO}_2, \Delta} 2\text{KCl} + 3\text{O}_2$; $2\text{KMnO}_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{K}_2\text{MnO}_4 + \text{MnO}_2 + \text{O}_2$ |
| $\text{H}_2$ | $\text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{H}_2$ |
| $\text{CO}_2$ | $\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$ |
| $\text{Cl}_2$ | $\text{MnO}_2 + 4\text{HCl(conc.)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{MnCl}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ |
| $\text{NH}_3$ | $2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ |
| $\text{SO}_2$ | $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{SO}_2$ |

## 3. Reasons for the choices

**Example 1.** *Which generator for $\text{O}_2$ from $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ with $\text{MnO}_2$, and why not the $\text{KMnO}_4$ set-up?* A solid-liquid, no-heat generator (flask with a dropping funnel): the reaction needs no heating; $\text{MnO}_2$ is a catalyst and is not consumed.

**Example 2.** *Why use $\text{HCl}$, not $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, with marble?* Calcium sulfate is only slightly soluble; it coats the marble and stops the reaction.

**Example 3.** *Why is $\text{NH}_3$ not made by heating $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ alone?* It decomposes to $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{HCl}$, which recombine on the cool part of the tube to give $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ again; calcium hydroxide traps the $\text{HCl}$.

**Example 4.** *Why is dilute (not concentrated) acid used with zinc for hydrogen?* Concentrated sulfuric acid is an oxidising acid and gives $\text{SO}_2$ rather than $\text{H}_2$; concentrated nitric acid gives nitrogen oxides.

**Example 5.** The mouth of the tube is tilted slightly downward when heating $\text{KClO}_3/\text{MnO}_2$ (and for $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2$, which produces water).

## 4. Other points

$\text{O}_2$ from $\text{KMnO}_4$: put a **plug of cotton wool** at the mouth of the tube to stop solid blowing into the delivery tube. For $\text{Cl}_2$ use a fume cupboard. $\text{SO}_2$ can also be made from copper and hot concentrated sulfuric acid.

## Common mistakes

- Using a heating set-up for a solid-liquid reaction that needs none.
- Using sulfuric acid with calcium carbonate.
- Forgetting the cotton-wool plug with $\text{KMnO}_4$.

## Practice problems

1. Equation for $\text{CO}_2$ from marble.
2. Why is the mouth of the tube sloped slightly downward?

**Answers.** (1) $\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$. (2) So that condensed water cannot run back and crack the hot tube.
