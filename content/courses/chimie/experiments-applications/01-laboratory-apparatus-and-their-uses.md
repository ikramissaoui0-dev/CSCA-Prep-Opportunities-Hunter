## What you will learn

- the names and uses of common apparatus;
- the precision of measuring instruments;
- how to choose the right apparatus.

## 1. Common apparatus

| Apparatus | Use |
|---|---|
| Test tube (试管), boiling tube | small-scale reactions; can be heated directly |
| Beaker (烧杯) | holding, mixing, heating liquids (with gauze); **not** for accurate volumes |
| Conical (Erlenmeyer) flask (锥形瓶) | titrations; swirling without spilling |
| Round-bottom / distillation flask (圆底烧瓶 / 蒸馏烧瓶) | heating liquids, distillation, reflux |
| Evaporating dish (蒸发皿) | evaporating a solution to crystallise a solid |
| Crucible (坩埚) | strong heating of solids (e.g. removing water of crystallisation) |
| Measuring cylinder (量筒) | approximate volumes |
| Volumetric flask | an exact volume of solution (e.g. $250.0\ \text{mL}$) |
| Pipette | delivering a fixed volume accurately (e.g. $25.00\ \text{mL}$) |
| Burette | delivering a variable volume in a titration |
| Funnel, filter paper | filtration |
| Condenser | cooling vapour to liquid (water in at the bottom, out at the top) |
| Separating funnel | separating immiscible liquids |
| Desiccator | cooling and storing without moisture |
| Tripod, gauze, Bunsen burner, tongs | heating and handling |
| Pan balance (托盘天平) | mass to $0.1\ \text{g}$ |

## 2. Precision

A pan balance reads to $0.1\ \text{g}$; an electronic balance to $0.01\ \text{g}$ or better; a measuring cylinder to about $1\%$ ($\pm 1\ \text{mL}$ for $100\ \text{mL}$); a pipette and volumetric flask to $0.01$–$0.1\ \text{mL}$; a burette to $\pm 0.05\ \text{mL}$ (reading to $0.01\ \text{mL}$ by estimation). Quote results to a consistent number of decimals: a burette reading of $21.40\ \text{mL}$, not $21.4$.

**Example 1.** A student records the volume of NaOH used as $22.5\ \text{mL}$ from a burette: a burette should be read to two decimal places ($22.50\ \text{mL}$), ending in $0$ or $5$.

## 3. Choosing apparatus

- **Heat $2\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O}$ strongly until all the water is driven off**: a crucible on a pipe-clay triangle over a tripod, a desiccator to cool it, a balance.
- **Crystallise a salt from solution**: evaporating dish, then filter.
- **Which burette?** Acidic and oxidising solutions (acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$) go in a burette with a **glass stopcock** because they attack rubber; **alkaline** solutions (NaOH) go in a burette with a rubber tube and glass bead (a glass stopcock would stick because alkali attacks glass).
- **Accurate $25.00\ \text{mL}$ of solution**: a pipette, not a measuring cylinder.

## 4. Care of apparatus

Rinse a **pipette** and **burette** with the solution they will contain; rinse a **conical flask** with distilled water only; never dry a volumetric flask by heating; wash glassware until water forms a continuous film.

## Common mistakes

- Using a beaker for accurate volumes.
- Rinsing the conical flask with the solution.
- Using a glass stopcock with strong alkali.

## Practice problems

1. Choose apparatus to measure exactly $25.0\ \text{mL}$ of acid and add NaOH of unknown volume.
2. Which instrument gives the more precise volume: a measuring cylinder or a burette?

**Answers.** (1) Pipette for the acid, burette for the alkali, conical flask. (2) The burette.
