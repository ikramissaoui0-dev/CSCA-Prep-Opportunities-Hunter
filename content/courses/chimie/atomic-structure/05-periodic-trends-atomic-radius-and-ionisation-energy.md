## What you will learn

- the trend in atomic radius and ion sizes;
- first ionisation energy and its exceptions;
- successive ionisation energies and how they reveal the group.

## 1. The two competing effects

Trends result from two effects: **nuclear charge** (increases across a period) and **number of shells / shielding** (increases down a group).

## 2. Atomic radius

- **Across a period** (left to right): the radius **decreases** (more protons pull the same number of shells closer).
- **Down a group**: the radius **increases** (extra shells).

**Ion sizes** (微粒半径): a cation is **smaller** than its atom (it has lost a shell or has more protons per electron); an anion is **larger** (more electron repulsion). For **isoelectronic** ions the one with more protons is smaller: $\text{Al}^{3+} < \text{Mg}^{2+} < \text{Na}^+ < \text{F}^- < \text{O}^{2-}$.

**Example 1.** Larger radius: $\text{O}$ or $\text{S}$? $\text{S}$ (extra shell). $\text{Na}$ or $\text{Mg}$? $\text{Na}$ (fewer protons, same shells). $\text{O}^{2-}$ or $\text{F}^-$? $\text{O}^{2-}$ (fewer protons for the same electrons).

## 3. Ionisation energy

The **first ionisation energy** is the energy required to remove one electron from each atom in a mole of gaseous atoms: $\text{X}(g) \to \text{X}^+(g) + e^-$.

- **Across a period**: generally **increases** (greater nuclear charge, smaller radius).
- **Down a group**: **decreases** (the outer electron is further away and better shielded).
- **Exceptions in Period 2/3**: Be $>$ B (the $2p$ electron is higher in energy than the $2s$) and N $>$ O (electron–electron repulsion in the paired $2p$ orbital). Likewise Mg $>$ Al and P $>$ S.

**Example 2.** Order Na, Mg, Al by first ionisation energy: $\text{Na} < \text{Al} < \text{Mg}$.

**Example 3.** *Why is potassium more reactive than sodium?* Its outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so its ionisation energy is lower and it is lost more easily.

## 4. Successive ionisation energies

Removing further electrons needs more and more energy. A **large jump** marks the start of a new, inner shell and tells you the group.

**Example 4.** An element has ionisation energies ($\text{kJ/mol}$) $738, 1451, 7733, 10540$: the big jump after the second electron means two valence electrons — Group 2 (magnesium).

## Common mistakes

- Saying ionisation energy decreases across a period.
- Forgetting the exceptions (Be/B, N/O).
- Treating an anion as smaller than its atom.

## Practice problems

1. Arrange $\text{Li}$, $\text{Na}$, $\text{K}$ by atomic radius and first ionisation energy.
2. Which is larger: $\text{Cl}$ or $\text{Cl}^-$?

**Answers.** (1) Radius increases $\text{Li} < \text{Na} < \text{K}$; ionisation energy decreases $\text{Li} > \text{Na} > \text{K}$. (2) $\text{Cl}^-$.
