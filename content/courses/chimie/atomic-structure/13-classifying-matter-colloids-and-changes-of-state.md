## What you will learn

- the classification of matter: pure substances and mixtures; oxides, acids, bases, salts;
- colloids, solutions and suspensions;
- states of matter, changes of state and physical versus chemical change.

## 1. Classification

- **Pure substances** (fixed composition, sharp melting point): **elements** and **compounds**.
- **Mixtures** (variable composition, separable by physical means): solutions (homogeneous), suspensions and emulsions (heterogeneous), colloids.

**Compounds**: *oxides* (acidic: $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{P}_4\text{O}_{10}$; basic: $\text{CaO}$, $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$; amphoteric: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{ZnO}$; neutral/non-salt-forming: $\text{CO}$, $\text{NO}$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$), *acids*, *bases*, *salts*.

**Example 1.** Classify: liquid oxygen — element (pure); clean air — mixture; $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O}$ — compound (pure, a hydrate); brass — alloy (mixture); a mixture of diamond and graphite — a mixture of two allotropes of one element.

**Example 2.** $\text{CO}$ is a neutral oxide (it does not react with water, acid or alkali); $\text{CaO}$ is basic (reacts with acid); $\text{SO}_2$ is acidic (reacts with alkali).

## 2. Colloids

A **colloid** has particles of about $1$–$100\ \text{nm}$ dispersed in a medium (milk, fog, gelatin, blood, smoke, paint). It looks homogeneous but **scatters a beam of light** (the **Tyndall effect**), does not settle, and passes through filter paper but not through a semi-permeable membrane. **Solutions** ($< 1\ \text{nm}$) show no Tyndall effect and pass through everything; **suspensions** ($> 100\ \text{nm}$, muddy water) settle and can be filtered.

## 3. States of matter

- **Solid**: fixed shape and volume; particles vibrate in fixed positions, close together.
- **Liquid**: fixed volume, no fixed shape; particles close but free to move past each other.
- **Gas**: no fixed shape or volume; particles far apart and moving fast; compressible.

**Changes of state**: melting, freezing, boiling/evaporation, condensation, sublimation, deposition. During a change of state the temperature stays constant while the energy supplied overcomes (or the energy released forms) intermolecular forces.

**Example 3.** A constant heater melts ice at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ in $2\ \text{min}$; warming the same mass of water from $0$ to $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ takes about $2.5\ \text{min}$ ($418\ \text{J/g}$ against $334\ \text{J/g}$).

**Example 4.** *Why can gases be compressed but solids cannot?* Gas particles are far apart; solid particles are in contact.

## 4. Physical vs chemical change

A **physical change** makes no new substance (melting, dissolving, boiling). A **chemical change** forms new substances; evidence: a gas, a precipitate, a colour change, a temperature change, light. Physical changes are usually reversible by physical means.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a mixture a compound.
- Saying temperature rises during melting.
- Treating a colloid as a solution.

## Practice problems

1. Classify air, $\text{NaCl}$, brass, oxygen.
2. How would you show that a liquid is a colloid?

**Answers.** (1) mixture, compound, mixture (alloy), element. (2) Shine a beam of light through it: a visible path (the Tyndall effect).
