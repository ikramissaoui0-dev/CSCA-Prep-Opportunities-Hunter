## What you will learn

- why the noble gases are unreactive;
- their physical properties and trends;
- their uses.

## 1. Unreactive because of full shells

The noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn) have **full outer electron shells** ($2$ for helium, $8$ for the others), so they have no tendency to gain, lose or share electrons. They exist as **single atoms** (monatomic gases) with no chemical bonds between atoms, and are **extremely unreactive** (a few compounds of Xe and Kr with fluorine and oxygen exist).

## 2. Physical properties

Colourless gases at room temperature, poor conductors, with **very low melting and boiling points** because only weak London forces act between atoms. Boiling points **increase down the group** (more electrons, stronger London forces): He $-269\ ^\circ\text{C}$, Ne $-246$, Ar $-186$, Kr $-153$, Xe $-108$. The density also increases down the group.

**Example 1.** *Why does helium have a lower boiling point than neon?* Helium atoms have fewer electrons, so the London forces are weaker.

## 3. Uses

| Gas | Uses and the property involved |
|---|---|
| Helium | balloons and airships (low density, **non-flammable** unlike hydrogen); cooling superconducting magnets (very low boiling point); deep-sea breathing mixtures |
| Neon | red-orange advertising signs (glows in a discharge tube) |
| Argon | filament lamps (inert atmosphere prevents the tungsten from burning); welding shield gas |
| Krypton, xenon | high-intensity lamps, camera flashes, headlights |
| Radon | radioactive; a health hazard in some homes |

**Example 2.** *Why is argon used in light bulbs?* It is inert, so the white-hot tungsten filament does not oxidise.

**Example 3.** *Why was helium chosen for airships instead of hydrogen?* Hydrogen is flammable and explosive; helium is not and is nearly as light.

## 4. Discovery

Argon was found by Rayleigh and Ramsay in $1894$ because nitrogen from the air was slightly denser than nitrogen from chemical sources; helium was first detected in the Sun's spectrum.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the noble gases are diatomic.
- Saying they have no outer electrons (they have a *full* outer shell).
- Using the trend for reactivity (there is none: they are all unreactive).

## Practice problems

1. Why do noble gases have such low boiling points?
2. Name the noble gas used in a neon sign.

**Answers.** (1) Only weak London forces between the single atoms. (2) Neon.
