## What you will learn

- isotopes and why they have the same chemistry;
- relative atomic mass from isotope abundances;
- allotropes (a different idea) and uses of isotopes.

## 1. Isotopes

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element (same $Z$) with different numbers of neutrons (different $A$). They have the same chemical properties (the same electron arrangement) but slightly different physical properties (mass, density, rate of diffusion).

**Example 1.** ${}^{12}\text{C}$ and ${}^{14}\text{C}$: both $6$ protons; $6$ and $8$ neutrons. They behave identically in chemical reactions because chemistry depends on the electrons, and the number of electrons equals the number of protons.

## 2. Relative atomic mass

The **relative atomic mass** $A_r$ is the weighted mean of the isotopic masses:

$$
A_r = \frac{\sum(\text{mass number} \times \text{abundance})}{100}.
$$

**Example 2.** Boron: $20\%$ ${}^{10}\text{B}$, $80\%$ ${}^{11}\text{B}$: $A_r = 0.2 \cdot 10 + 0.8 \cdot 11 = 10.8$.

**Example 3.** Chlorine: $75\%$ ${}^{35}\text{Cl}$, $25\%$ ${}^{37}\text{Cl}$: $A_r = 35.5$. A mass spectrum shows peaks at $35$ and $37$ in a $3 : 1$ ratio.

**Example 4.** Copper has $A_r = 63.5$ with isotopes $63$ and $65$: the fraction of ${}^{65}\text{Cu}$ is $\dfrac{63.5 - 63}{2} = 25\%$.

**Example 5.** Silicon: $92.2\%$ ${}^{28}\text{Si}$, $4.7\%$ ${}^{29}\text{Si}$, $3.1\%$ ${}^{30}\text{Si}$: $A_r = 28.1$.

## 3. Uses of isotopes

- **Medicine**: ${}^{131}\text{I}$ for the thyroid, ${}^{99m}\text{Tc}$ as a tracer, ${}^{60}\text{Co}$ in radiotherapy.
- **Archaeology**: radiocarbon dating with ${}^{14}\text{C}$.
- **Energy**: ${}^{235}\text{U}$ in reactors; deuterium and tritium for fusion.
- **Industry**: tracers for leaks, thickness gauges.

## 4. Allotropes (同素异形体)

Different forms of the **same element** in the same state, with different structures: carbon (diamond, graphite, graphene, $\text{C}_{60}$), oxygen ($\text{O}_2$ and ozone $\text{O}_3$), phosphorus (white and red), sulfur (rhombic and monoclinic). They differ in physical properties but burn to the same oxide. **Do not confuse allotropes (same element, different structure) with isotopes (same element, different neutrons).**

## Common mistakes

- Saying isotopes have different numbers of protons.
- Forgetting the weighted mean in $A_r$.
- Confusing isotopes with allotropes.

## Practice problems

1. Neon: $90\%$ ${}^{20}\text{Ne}$, $10\%$ ${}^{22}\text{Ne}$: $A_r$.
2. Why do ${}^{12}\text{C}$ and ${}^{14}\text{C}$ react identically?

**Answers.** (1) $20.2$. (2) They have the same number of electrons and hence the same electron arrangement.
