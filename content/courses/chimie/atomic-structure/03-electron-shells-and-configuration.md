## What you will learn

- shells, subshells and orbitals;
- the filling rules and how to write configurations;
- configurations of ions and valence electrons; shell diagrams.

## 1. Shells, subshells, orbitals

Electrons occupy **shells** $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ holding up to $2n^2$ electrons ($2, 8, 18, 32$). Within a shell, **subshells** $s, p, d, f$ contain $1, 3, 5, 7$ orbitals and hold up to $2, 6, 10, 14$ electrons. Each orbital holds at most **two** electrons with opposite spins.

## 2. Filling rules

1. **Aufbau**: fill the lowest-energy orbitals first: $1s\ 2s\ 2p\ 3s\ 3p\ 4s\ 3d\ 4p\ 5s\ 4d\ 5p\ \dots$
2. **Pauli exclusion**: at most two electrons per orbital, opposite spins.
3. **Hund's rule**: degenerate orbitals fill singly first, with parallel spins.

**Examples.**

- N ($Z = 7$): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^3$ (three $2p$ electrons unpaired).
- Al ($Z = 13$): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^1$, i.e. $2, 8, 3$.
- Cl ($Z = 17$): $[\text{Ne}]\,3s^23p^5$, i.e. $2, 8, 7$.
- Fe ($Z = 26$): $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^6\,4s^2$.
- **Exceptions** (half-filled or full $d$ subshells are extra stable): Cr is $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^54s^1$; Cu is $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^{10}4s^1$.

## 3. Ions

**Cations** lose the outermost electrons first (the $4s$ electrons before the $3d$): $\text{Fe}^{3+} = [\text{Ar}]\,3d^5$; $\text{Fe}^{2+} = [\text{Ar}]\,3d^6$. **Anions** gain electrons into the outer shell: $\text{Cl}^- = [\text{Ar}]$; $\text{S}^{2-} = 1s^22s^22p^63s^23p^6$. Ions with the same configuration are **isoelectronic** ($\text{O}^{2-}$, $\text{F}^-$, $\text{Na}^+$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Al}^{3+}$).

## 4. Valence electrons and shell diagrams

**Valence electrons** are those in the outermost shell: they determine the chemistry. Oxygen ($Z = 8$): shell arrangement $2, 6$ — six valence electrons, so it needs two more for a full shell. Sulfur ($Z = 16$): $2, 8, 6$ — six valence electrons. A full outer shell ($2$ for the first, $8$ otherwise) is stable: the noble gases.

To **draw a shell diagram** put a nucleus with the symbol and $Z$ in the centre, concentric circles for shells and dots or crosses for electrons ($2$ on the first, up to $8$ on the second and third).

## 5. Link with the periodic table

Period number = number of occupied shells; group number (main groups) = number of valence electrons. Blocks: $s$ (Groups 1–2), $p$ (13–18), $d$ (transition metals), $f$.

## Common mistakes

- Filling $3d$ before $4s$ for a neutral atom, or removing $3d$ electrons before $4s$ when forming a cation.
- Putting more than two electrons in an orbital.
- Forgetting the Cr and Cu exceptions.

## Practice problems

1. Configuration of $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and of $\text{S}^{2-}$.
2. Valence electrons of sulfur.
3. Why does Cr have the configuration $3d^54s^1$?

**Answers.** (1) $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^6$ ; $[\text{Ne}]\,3s^23p^6$. (2) $6$. (3) A half-filled $d$ subshell is especially stable.
