## What you will learn

- the physical and chemical properties of the alkali metals;
- the trend in reactivity and its explanation;
- reactions with water and oxygen; storage and tests.

## 1. Physical properties

Lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, caesium. Soft (cut with a knife), silvery when freshly cut, with **low melting points and low densities** (Li, Na, K float on water). Melting points and densities generally change down the group: melting point decreases; density increases (with K as an exception).

## 2. Chemical properties

Each atom has **one valence electron** and forms $\text{M}^+$ ions. Compounds are ionic, white and soluble.

**Reactivity increases down the group**: the outer electron is further from the nucleus and better shielded, so it is lost more easily (lower ionisation energy).

- **With water**: $2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$. The solution is **alkaline**. Li fizzes steadily; Na melts into a ball and moves around; K burns with a lilac flame; Rb and Cs react explosively.
- **With oxygen**: $4\text{Li} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{Li}_2\text{O}$; sodium also forms $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$ (peroxide); potassium forms superoxide; they tarnish quickly.
- **With halogens**: $2\text{Na} + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{NaCl}$.

**Flame colours**: Li red, Na yellow, K lilac.

## 3. Storage and safety

Stored under **oil** (not water or air). Small pieces only; goggles; a fire of alkali metals must be smothered with dry sand — **never use water or a $\text{CO}_2$ extinguisher**.

## 4. Worked examples

**Example 1.** Potassium with water: $2\text{K} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{KOH} + \text{H}_2$.

**Example 2.** *Why is caesium more reactive than sodium?* Its outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so less energy is needed to remove it.

**Example 3.** *Why are alkali metals stored under oil?* They react with oxygen and water vapour in the air.

**Example 4.** Test for the product solution: universal indicator turns purple (pH about $13$–$14$).

**Example 5.** $0.23\ \text{g}$ of sodium with water: $n = 0.01\ \text{mol}$, producing $0.005\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{H}_2$ ($112\ \text{mL}$ at STP) and $0.01\ \text{mol}$ of NaOH.

## Common mistakes

- Saying alkali metals have two valence electrons.
- Forgetting that the solution formed with water is alkaline.
- Using water on an alkali-metal fire.

## Practice problems

1. Equation for lithium with water.
2. Which gives the most vigorous reaction with water: Li, Na or K?

**Answers.** (1) $2\text{Li} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{LiOH} + \text{H}_2$. (2) K.
