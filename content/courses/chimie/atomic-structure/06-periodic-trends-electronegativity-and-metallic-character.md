## What you will learn

- electronegativity and bond polarity;
- the trends in electronegativity and metallic/non-metallic character;
- how to compare 金属性 and 非金属性.

## 1. Electronegativity (电负性)

**Electronegativity** is the tendency of an atom to attract the shared electrons in a covalent bond. It **increases across a period** and **decreases down a group**; fluorine is the most electronegative element ($3.98$ on the Pauling scale).

The difference $\Delta\chi$ between two bonded atoms tells the bond type:

- $\Delta\chi \approx 0$: **non-polar covalent** (e.g. $\text{Cl}_2$);
- moderate $\Delta\chi$ ($\approx 0.4$–$1.7$): **polar covalent** (HCl, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) with partial charges $\delta^+$ and $\delta^-$;
- large $\Delta\chi$ ($> 1.7$): **ionic** (NaCl).

This is a continuum, not a sharp boundary.

**Example 1.** Order C, N, O, F by electronegativity: $\text{C} < \text{N} < \text{O} < \text{F}$.

**Example 2.** Is the bond in HCl polar covalent or ionic? $\chi(\text{Cl}) = 3.16$, $\chi(\text{H}) = 2.20$: $\Delta\chi = 0.96$: polar covalent.

## 2. Metallic and non-metallic character

**Metallic character** (金属性) is the ease of losing electrons: it **decreases across a period** and **increases down a group**. **Non-metallic character** (非金属性) is the ease of gaining electrons: the opposite.

Ways to compare:

| For metals (e.g. Mg vs Ba) | For non-metals (e.g. Cl vs Br) |
|---|---|
| reaction with water or acid | reaction with hydrogen |
| strength of the hydroxide as a base | strength of the oxoacid / hydride |
| displacement from salt solutions | displacement of one halogen by another |
| ease of losing electrons (ionisation energy) | ease of gaining electrons |

**Example 3.** More metallic: $\text{Mg}$ or $\text{Ba}$? $\text{Ba}$ (lower in the group). More non-metallic: $\text{S}$ or $\text{Cl}$? $\text{Cl}$ (further right).

## 3. Consequences for the oxides and hydroxides

Across Period 3: $\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{MgO}$ are basic, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ amphoteric, $\text{SiO}_2$, $\text{P}_4\text{O}_{10}$, $\text{SO}_3$ acidic. The hydroxides go from strongly basic $\text{NaOH}$ to amphoteric $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ to acidic ($\text{H}_2\text{SiO}_3$, $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$, $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $\text{HClO}_4$).

## 4. Putting the trends together

| Property | Across a period | Down a group |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | decreases | increases |
| First ionisation energy | increases | decreases |
| Electronegativity | increases | decreases |
| Metallic character | decreases | increases |

## Common mistakes

- Reversing the reactivity trends of metals and non-metals.
- Treating the polar/ionic division as sharp.
- Forgetting that noble gases are usually excluded from electronegativity scales.

## Practice problems

1. Which has the higher electronegativity: Si or S?
2. Predict the bond type in $\text{CsF}$.

**Answers.** (1) S. (2) Ionic (a very large $\Delta\chi$).
