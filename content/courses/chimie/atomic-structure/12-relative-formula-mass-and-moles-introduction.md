## What you will learn

- relative atomic and formula mass;
- the mole, molar mass and Avogadro's constant;
- empirical formulae and simple calculations.

## 1. Relative masses

$A_r$ (relative atomic mass) is read from the periodic table. **Relative formula mass** $M_r$ is the sum of the $A_r$ of all atoms in the formula (relative molecular mass for molecules).

**Example 1.** $M_r(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98$. $M_r(\text{Ca(OH)}_2) = 40 + 2(17) = 74$. $M_r(\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 160 + 90 = 250$.

## 2. The mole

The **mole** (物质的量, symbol $n$, unit mol) is the amount of substance that contains $N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ particles (the Avogadro constant, 阿伏加德罗常数). The **molar mass** $M$ (摩尔质量, g/mol) is numerically equal to $M_r$.

$$
n = \frac mM = \frac N{N_A}.
$$

**Example 2.** $20\ \text{g}$ of NaOH ($M = 40$) is $0.5\ \text{mol}$; $11\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ ($M = 44$) is $0.25\ \text{mol}$, i.e. $1.5 \times 10^{23}$ molecules and $4.5 \times 10^{23}$ atoms.

**Example 3.** Mass of $0.2\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $0.2 \cdot 18 = 3.6\ \text{g}$.

**Example 4.** $18\ \text{g}$ of water contains $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ molecules and $3 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23}$ atoms.

## 3. Percentage composition and empirical formula

$$
\text{mass \%} = \frac{\text{mass of element in one formula unit}}{M_r} \times 100.
$$

**Empirical formula** (simplest whole-number ratio): divide each mass (or %) by the $A_r$, then divide by the smallest result. The **molecular formula** is a whole-number multiple, found from $M_r$.

**Example 5.** A compound with $40.0\%\ \text{C}$, $6.7\%\ \text{H}$, $53.3\%\ \text{O}$: moles $3.33 : 6.7 : 3.33 = 1 : 2 : 1$, so $\text{CH}_2\text{O}$; if $M_r = 180$, the molecular formula is $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$.

**Example 6.** A hydrocarbon with $85.7\%\ \text{C}$: $\text{C} : \text{H} = \dfrac{85.7}{12} : \dfrac{14.3}1 = 1 : 2$, so $\text{CH}_2$.

## 4. Reacting masses (preview)

Using the equation $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{MgO}$: $4.8\ \text{g}$ of Mg ($0.2\ \text{mol}$) gives $0.2\ \text{mol}$ of MgO, $8.0\ \text{g}$.

## Common mistakes

- Using $M_r$ with the wrong subscripts.
- Dividing by the wrong $M$.
- Writing the molecular formula as the empirical formula.

## Practice problems

1. $M_r$ of $\text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$.
2. Number of moles in $4.4\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$.
3. Empirical formula of a compound with $\text{Fe}\ 70\%$, $\text{O}\ 30\%$.

**Answers.** (1) $342$. (2) $0.1\ \text{mol}$. (3) $\text{Fe} : \text{O} = 1.25 : 1.875 = 2 : 3$, so $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$.
