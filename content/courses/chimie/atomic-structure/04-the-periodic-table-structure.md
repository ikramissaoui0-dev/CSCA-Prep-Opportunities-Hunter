## What you will learn

- how the periodic table is organised: periods, groups, blocks;
- how position follows from electron configuration;
- metals, non-metals and metalloids.

## 1. Organisation

Elements are arranged by increasing **atomic number**. Horizontal rows are **periods**; vertical columns are **groups**.

- **Period number** = number of electron shells. Period 1 has $2$ elements, periods 2 and 3 have $8$ each (the *short* periods), periods 4 and 5 have $18$, period 6 has $32$ (the *long* periods).
- **Group number** (main groups) = number of valence electrons: Group 1 has $1$, Group 2 has $2$, Groups 13–17 have $3$–$7$, Group 18 has $8$ (He has $2$).
- **Blocks**: $s$-block (Groups 1–2), $p$-block (13–18), $d$-block (transition metals), $f$-block (lanthanides, actinides).

## 2. Reading position from configuration

**Example 1.** Potassium is in Period 4, Group 1: it has $4$ shells and $1$ valence electron ($2, 8, 8, 1$).

**Example 2.** Element X with arrangement $2, 8, 7$: Period 3, Group 17 (chlorine, a halogen).

**Example 3.** $1s^22s^22p^63s^23p^64s^2$: Period 4, Group 2 (calcium).

**Example 4.** *Why do lithium, sodium and potassium all react similarly with water?* Each has one valence electron, which it loses to form $\text{M}^+$.

**Example 5.** An element in Period 3 with $3$ valence electrons: Group 13, aluminium.

## 3. Metals, non-metals and metalloids

- **Metals** are on the left and in the centre: shiny, conduct heat and electricity, malleable, ductile; they lose electrons to form cations; their oxides are basic.
- **Non-metals** are on the upper right: poor conductors (except graphite), brittle as solids; they gain or share electrons; their oxides are acidic.
- **Metalloids** (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te) lie along the "staircase" and have intermediate properties (semiconductors).

## 4. Useful families

Group 1: alkali metals. Group 2: alkaline earth metals. Group 17: halogens. Group 18: noble gases. The central block: transition metals (variable oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalysts). Hydrogen is placed on its own or with Group 1.

**Development**: Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass and by properties, leaving gaps for undiscovered elements (gallium, germanium) which he predicted; Moseley showed that atomic number is the correct ordering.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing periods (rows) and groups (columns).
- Counting the group number wrongly for transition metals.
- Placing elements by atomic mass instead of atomic number.

## Practice problems

1. Position of the element with electrons $2, 8, 18, 6$.
2. How many valence electrons does a Group 15 element have?

**Answers.** (1) Period 4, Group 16 (selenium). (2) $5$.
