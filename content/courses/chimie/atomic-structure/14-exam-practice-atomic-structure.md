## What you will learn

- the common question types for this module;
- a strategy for each;
- worked exam-style questions with answers.

## 1. Question types and strategy

| Type | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Sub-atomic particle calculations | $N = A - Z$; subtract electrons for cations, add for anions |
| Isotope calculations | weighted mean of masses |
| Electron configuration | Aufbau order; $4s$ before $3d$; exceptions Cr, Cu |
| Periodic trends | nuclear charge vs shells/shielding |
| Group properties | one valence electron (Group 1), seven (Group 17); trends in reactivity |
| Mole calculations | $n = m/M$; convert first |
| Molecular shapes | count pairs, remove lone pairs; check symmetry for polarity |

**Exam strategy**: start from the electron configuration; state the two competing effects in trend questions; for property questions, decide the structure type first; always show the working in mole questions.

## 2. Worked questions

**Q1.** Silicon has isotopes ${}^{28}\text{Si}$ ($92.2\%$), ${}^{29}\text{Si}$ ($4.7\%$) and ${}^{30}\text{Si}$ ($3.1\%$). Find $A_r$. *Answer*: $0.922 \cdot 28 + 0.047 \cdot 29 + 0.031 \cdot 30 = 28.1$.

**Q2.** Explain why chlorine is more reactive than bromine. *Answer*: both have seven valence electrons, but a chlorine atom is smaller, so its nucleus attracts an incoming electron more strongly (less shielding, shorter distance); reactivity decreases down Group 17.

**Q3.** An ion has $10$ electrons and $11$ protons. *Answer*: $\text{Na}^+$ (charge $+1$).

**Q4.** Write the electron configuration of $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and give the number of unpaired electrons. *Answer*: $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^6$; four unpaired electrons.

**Q5.** Explain why the boiling point of HF is higher than that of HCl. *Answer*: hydrogen bonding between HF molecules (H bonded to the highly electronegative F) is much stronger than the dipole–dipole forces in HCl.

**Q6.** How many moles of atoms are in $4.4\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$? *Answer*: $0.1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ gives $0.3\ \text{mol}$ of atoms.

**Q7.** Arrange $\text{Na}^+$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ and $\text{F}^-$ in order of increasing radius. *Answer*: $\text{Mg}^{2+} < \text{Na}^+ < \text{F}^-$ (isoelectronic: more protons, smaller radius).

**Q8.** Predict the shape of $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and give bond angles. *Answer*: pyramidal ($107^\circ$) and bent ($104.5^\circ$): one and two lone pairs squeeze the angle.

**Q9.** A compound contains $75\%\ \text{C}$ and $25\%\ \text{H}$: empirical formula. *Answer*: $\text{C} : \text{H} = 6.25 : 25 = 1 : 4$, so $\text{CH}_4$.

**Q10.** Why does graphite conduct electricity but diamond does not? *Answer*: in graphite each carbon uses three of its four outer electrons in bonding, and the fourth is delocalised between layers; in diamond all four are in bonds.

## 3. Quick formula sheet

$N = A - Z$; $A_r = \dfrac{\sum(\text{mass} \times \%)}{100}$; $n = \dfrac mM = \dfrac N{N_A} = \dfrac V{V_m} = cV$; trends: radius $\downarrow$ across, $\uparrow$ down; ionisation energy $\uparrow$ across, $\downarrow$ down; electronegativity $\uparrow$ across, $\downarrow$ down.

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting instead of adding electrons for an anion.
- Reversing the halogen reactivity trend.
- Forgetting lone pairs in VSEPR.

## Practice problem

How many neutrons and electrons are in ${}^{65}_{30}\text{Zn}^{2+}$?

**Answer.** $35$ neutrons and $28$ electrons.
