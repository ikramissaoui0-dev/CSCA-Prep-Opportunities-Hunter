minutes: 8

## What you will learn

- properties of fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine;
- the reactivity trend and displacement reactions;
- halide tests and uses.

## 1. Physical properties

The halogens exist as **diatomic molecules** ($\text{F}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{Br}_2$, $\text{I}_2$):

| Halogen | State at room temperature | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Fluorine | gas | pale yellow |
| Chlorine | gas | yellow-green |
| Bromine | liquid | red-brown |
| Iodine | solid | grey-black (purple vapour) |

Melting and boiling points **increase** down the group (larger molecules, stronger London forces). They are toxic, with pungent smells.

## 2. Chemical properties

Seven valence electrons; each atom gains one electron to form a $-1$ ion (halide). **Reactivity decreases down the group**: the incoming electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is less strongly attracted. Fluorine is the most reactive non-metal.

- **With metals**: ionic halides: $2\text{Fe} + 3\text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{FeCl}_3$.
- **With hydrogen**: $\text{H}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{HCl}$ (explosive for F, light-sensitive for Cl, slow for I).
- **With water**: $\text{Cl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{HCl} + \text{HClO}$ (disinfectant).

## 3. Displacement reactions

A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from a solution of its halide:

$$
\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KBr} \to 2\text{KCl} + \text{Br}_2 \ (\text{orange}), \qquad \text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KI} \to 2\text{KCl} + \text{I}_2 \ (\text{brown}), \qquad \text{Br}_2 + 2\text{KI} \to 2\text{KBr} + \text{I}_2.
$$

**Example 1.** Chlorine gas bubbled into potassium iodide: the colourless solution turns brown (iodine). No reaction occurs if iodine is added to potassium chloride.

**Example 2.** *Why is fluorine more reactive than iodine?* The F atom is small, so the nucleus attracts an extra electron strongly; the weak $\text{F-F}$ bond also makes it easy to break.

**Example 3.** Chlorine is more reactive than bromine for the same reason (electron attraction decreases down the group).

## 4. Tests for halide ions

Add dilute nitric acid and silver nitrate: $\text{Cl}^-$ gives a **white** precipitate (soluble in dilute ammonia), $\text{Br}^-$ **cream** (slightly soluble in conc. ammonia), $\text{I}^-$ **yellow** (insoluble). **Example 4.** A solution that gives a cream precipitate contains bromide.

## 5. Uses

Chlorine for water treatment and bleach; fluoride in toothpaste; iodine as an antiseptic and for the thyroid; halogenated compounds in plastics (PVC, PTFE) and solvents.

## Common mistakes

- Reversing the reactivity trend (it **decreases** down Group 17).
- Using a halogen to displace a *more* reactive halogen.
- Forgetting to acidify before adding $\text{AgNO}_3$.

## Practice problems

1. Will bromine displace chloride from KCl?
2. Equation for chlorine with sodium bromide.

**Answers.** (1) No (Br is less reactive). (2) $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaBr} \to 2\text{NaCl} + \text{Br}_2$.
